# Formation signatures for decentralized 3-D airways v1

## Goal

Different mechanisms can all produce persistent individual routes after they are established.

The discriminating information is therefore **how individuality appears through time and how it responds to a known reset**.

This note specifies qualitative signatures before any prospective formation dataset is analysed.

---

## Mechanism A — social seeding + personal refinement

### Process

1. social information / colony tradition narrows the broad destination set;
2. the individual initially explores multiple ways of reaching that destination;
3. repeated experience reinforces one or a few personal route solutions.

### Temporal signature

Early:

[
\text{group destination similarity high}
quad\text{but}\quad
\text{self route predictability low}.
]

Later:

[
\text{group destination similarity remains high}
quad\text{and}\quad
\text{self route predictability rises}.
]

Thus:

> **group convergence should precede personal corridor convergence.**

### Parent / demonstrator prediction

A juvenile may initially resemble its mother or group in **where it goes** more than in its exact detailed path.

With independent experience:
- maternal/group detailed-route similarity may weaken;
- personal self-history similarity should strengthen.

---

## Mechanism B — individual stochastic path dependence without social seeding

### Process

All animals encounter the same route options independently.

Early stochastic choices become reinforced.

### Signature

- no necessary early group-level destination convergence;
- initially high route exploration;
- different individuals diverge idiosyncratically;
- early random choices predict later route bias;
- self-predictability rises while population route fractions can remain symmetric.

Key discriminator:

> early personal choices, not group identity, predict later route use.

---

## Mechanism C — stable performance matching

### Process

Morphology or persistent flight-performance differences make different route solutions cheaper for different individuals.

### Signature

- individual differences can appear from the **first independently observed flights**;
- wing loading, aspect ratio or body size predict route geometry;
- little or no preceding high-entropy exploration phase is required;
- route preference transfers across resource changes if the aerodynamic constraint remains.

Key discriminator:

> stable traits predict the eventual route before extensive route-specific experience accumulates.

---

## Mechanism D — learned spatial information / switching-cost reuse

### Process

A known route has an information advantage because landmarks, obstacles, resources and approach geometry are already learned.

### Signature

In a familiar landscape:
- strong route persistence;
- low exploration.

After a controlled reset (translocation, moved resource, changed access):
- immediate exploration increases;
- old self-history prediction falls;
- route stereotypy rebuilds with repeated exposure;
- new self-history gradually replaces old self-history.

Key discriminator:

> **reset -> exploration spike -> re-stabilization.**

---

## Mechanism E — active competition / contemporaneous avoidance

### Process

Current conspecific presence changes route utility.

### Signature

- route choice depends on real-time co-presence;
- vertical or horizontal separation increases during encounters;
- removing or experimentally changing competitor density changes route use quickly;
- route differences need not persist when competitors are absent.

Key discriminator:

> current traffic predicts current route adjustment above personal history.

This differs sharply from path-dependent reuse, which can persist with no contemporaneous interaction term.

---

## Mechanism F — exact resource/task specialization

### Process

Individuals repeatedly visit different resources or perform different movement tasks.

### Signature

- identity appears in unconditioned routes;
- identity weakens or disappears after conditioning on exact resource, destination and task;
- moving or removing the focal resource causes route change.

Key discriminator:

> resource/task identity explains the personal route.

---

## Minimal longitudinal measurements

For each independent trip t, define before analysis:

### Destination convergence

[
D_i(t)=\text{similarity of individual i's destination choice to colony/group destination distribution}.
]

### Personal route predictability

[
R_i(t)=\text{held-out route prediction from the individual's own previous trips}.
]

### Group route predictability

[
G_i(t)=\text{held-out route prediction from group/conspecific histories}.
]

### Exploration

[
E_i(t)=\text{route entropy / corridor dispersion / novel-area use}.
]

### Morphological prediction

[
M_i=\text{pre-flight stable trait predictor of route geometry}.
]

---

## Strongest signature for social seeding + personal refinement

The distinctive ordering is:

[
D(t) \uparrow
\quad\text{before}\quad
R(t) \uparrow,
]

while

[
R(t)-G(t)
]

becomes increasingly positive with independent experience.

In words:

> **the animal first learns where the shared airway goes, then develops how it personally flies it.**

This is the clearest prospective test of a decentralized “no traffic controller” architecture.

---

## Experimental design

Best case:

1. tag newly volant juveniles and mothers/group members;
2. begin before first independent foraging;
3. map roosts, resources and major landscape/canopy constraints;
4. record repeated high-resolution x-y-z trajectories;
5. quantify destination and route similarity separately;
6. introduce an independent reset in a subset, such as a novel/moved feeder or controlled access change.

The design estimates both levels:

[
\text{social destination seeding}
\rightarrow
\text{personal route refinement}.
]

---

## Claim ceiling

No one temporal signature alone uniquely identifies cognition.

Inference requires contrasts among predeclared mechanism classes and, ideally, a known naivety/reset boundary.
