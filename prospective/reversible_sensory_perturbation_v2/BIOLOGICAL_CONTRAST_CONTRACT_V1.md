## Central question

Does a persistent individual movement-policy bias remain identifiable when the same bat is forced to alter its movement by a controlled acoustic masking challenge?

The source already establishes a condition-level movement response.
This programme asks a different question:

> **what happens to individual identity inside that shared perturbation response?**

## H1 — perturbation-resilient personal bias

After condition-specific mean/scale are removed, individual identity should transfer across:

- styrofoam no-masker;
- styrofoam + 30 cm masker;
- styrofoam + 10 cm masker.

Preferred estimator if raw 3-D trajectories are available:
- derive one predeclared low-dimensional movement representation from source coordinates;
- standardize within condition;
- use held-out own-versus-other identity transfer across conditions.

Primary biological replication unit:
**bat**, not flight.

Interpretation if supported:

> shared sensory perturbation moves the population operating point without erasing the persistent individual movement bias.

## H2 — condition-specific expression of individuality

Compare identity strength across the three styrofoam conditions.

This is secondary unless a direct cross-condition contrast can be frozen before outcome opening.

Question:

> does stronger masking attenuate or amplify expression of the personal policy bias?

Do not select the condition with the strongest identity after opening.

## H3 — second perturbation under changed target material

If the public archive structurally exposes both:
- foam target, no masker;
- foam target + 30 cm masker;

test whether personal identity persists across this second within-individual perturbation.

This is an independent within-source contrast because target material is held fixed within the pair.

It cannot rescue H1.

## H4 — common perturbation versus individual-specific response

Only after H1 is adjudicated, compare:

[
M_{shared}: y_{ic}=\theta_i+\mu_c
]

against a model allowing an individual-specific perturbation deviation:

[
M_{individual}: y_{ic}=\theta_i+\mu_c+\delta_{ic}.
]

The exact predictive implementation must be cross-validated by held-out flights/conditions.

This asks whether the perturbation response itself is an individual trait.

Descriptive variation in condition effects is not enough; individual-specific response must improve held-out prediction.

## H5 — no universal two-axis assumption

Do not force the existing *Rhinolophus* FlightIntensity/ManeuveringExtent axes onto this source unless the exact required variables are structurally recoverable.

Preferred hierarchy:
1. source-compatible transparent representation if possible;
2. fixed predeclared representation derived without individual labels;
3. no identity-fitted axis selection.

## Key falsifiers

The persistent-bias hypothesis is weakened if:
- condition-standardized identity transfer is absent despite adequate support;
- apparent identity is carried only by one condition;
- between-condition individual ordering is not more stable than exchangeability;
- only condition means, not biological identity, predict held-out flights.

## Claim ceiling

Even strong H1 support would establish persistence through **this controlled sensory perturbation**, not:
- causal origin of the bias;
- learned versus biomechanical source;
- universal persistence across all bat species;
- equivalence to the wild vertical-policy carrier.
