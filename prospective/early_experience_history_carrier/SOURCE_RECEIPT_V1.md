## Experimental leverage

The source study provides an unusually strong discriminator for the maintenance problem:

1. juveniles were obtained before independent flight experience;
2. baseline behavioural measurements were collected first;
3. bats were then randomly divided between enriched and impoverished captive environments;
4. the environmental treatment lasted approximately ten weeks;
5. bats were subsequently released into an open colony and allowed to forage in the wild;
6. outdoor movements were tracked by GPS after the second wild-foraging night.

The source paper analysed 19 bats with outdoor GPS data, with long repeated series per bat.

## Inherited source result

The published paper already establishes that early environmental treatment affects later outdoor behaviour:
enriched bats were more exploratory/active/bold outdoors, whereas baseline behavioural predisposition was a weaker predictor of those outdoor measures.

Those published endpoints are inherited source knowledge and are not retested as new results here.

## New question

The unresolved mechanism question from the bat programme is:

> **Does experimentally altered early experience change how strongly later wild movement is carried by an individual's own spatial history?**

This is distinct from:
- whether enriched bats travel farther;
- whether enriched bats explore more area;
- whether a baseline personality score predicts distance.

The new endpoint is **personal-history predictive advantage**.

## Relation to previous prospective results

The Harten 2020 first-flight programme found:
- the predeclared common monotonic build-up rule failed;
- later personal history was strongly predictive for most juveniles;
- self-history advantage was already positive at the earliest estimable target days.

That leaves an architectural question:

> is the strength of this history carrier fixed by stable predisposition, or can early experience causally alter it?

The Rachum experiment is designed to distinguish those possibilities.

## Claim ceiling

A positive randomized-treatment contrast may support:

> early-life experience causally changes the strength or form of later personal spatial-history dependence.

It cannot alone establish:
- a specific memory mechanism;
- reinforcement learning;
- fitness optimality;
- universal effects across bat species;
- that enrichment is generally beneficial in the wild.
