# From late-winter geographic sharing to March separation in one wild-turkey tracking source

**28 October 2026 · Original Movebank GPS executed; POST-INSPECTION exploratory analysis.**
This report compares *month-wise geographic configuration of observed GPS use*,
not actual bird interactions, flock membership, migratory mechanism or a
taxon-general ecological law. No individual animal ID, original coordinates,
site locations, observation timestamps or individual monthly trajectories
are included.

## Main empirical finding

**In the 2020 turkey source cohort, the one animal whose ANNUAL GPS-use
centroid is disconnected from a six-animal <=5-km centroid graph shifts from
near-coincident MONTHLY usage centers with the six core animals in February,
to clearly more separated MONTHLY usage centers in March, and much farther
apart by April.**

Median monthly great-circle distance among its **six isolate–core dyads**:

| 2020 month | Median isolate–core GPS monthly-center distance | Median of 15 core–core pairs | Valid animals | Observed animal-calendar-days |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| January | 0.378 km | 0.973 km | 7/7 | 70 |
| February | **0.019 km** | **0.342 km** | 7/7 | 203 |
| March | **5.151 km** | 1.934 km | 7/7 | 217 |
| April | **16.508 km** | **5.088 km** | 7/7 | 210 |
| May | 16.184 km | 2.645 km | 7/7 | 217 |
| June | 14.979 km | 3.031 km | 7/7 | 210 |
| July | 14.394 km | 2.942 km | 7/7 | 217 |
| August–December | NOT OBSERVED | NOT OBSERVED | 0/7 | 0 |

Each animal/month GPS center requires **at least seven distinct original
recorded calendar dates**; original GPS fixes are first averaged per date
on the sphere, then each observed date carries equal weight in the month.
All 7 source animals have sufficient monthly GPS on every month
**January–July 2020**. February (203 animal-calendar-days) has 29 recorded
days per bird, March (217) has 31, and April (210) has 30;
there is **no missing tracked calendar month around the observed
February–March separation**. January has only 70 animal-days,
equal to 10 dates per bird, and therefore offers weaker within-month
coverage. NO original eligible animal has monthly fixes in
August–December. The calculated monthly February→March shift in the
*median interanimal center separation* is **+5.132 km**, and
March→April **+11.357 km**. These are changes in recorded
month-specific center distances, **not measured individual displacement
or inferred exact dates of movement**.

The predeclared descriptive category uses <=1 km as CLOSE
and >=5 km as SEPARATED for the six isolate–core source pairs:
January/February CLOSE; March–July SEPARATED; August–December
UNOBSERVED. Consequently the first *observed source-month
classification* transition is **February to March, 2020**, with
**no unobserved month between them**. Do NOT interpret this as proof
that an individual physically relocated on a particular day,
that a common flock separated, or that April source spread
reflects competition, reproduction or habitat change.

### Important second component of 2020 reconfiguration

The source-defined **six core animals also change their within-core
spatial geometry**: their 15-pair median monthly-center separation
rises from 0.342 km in February to 1.934 km in March and
5.088 km in April, then remains about 2.6–3.0 km through July.
Thus the source GPS pattern is **not simply one perfectly stationary
core plus one moving bird**. The one bird is the dominant
Jan–Feb to summer 14.524-km center shift relative to the
six-animal core (whose individual seasonal shifts have
median 0.455 km), but transient within-core seasonal spatial
expansion is also measured in monthly centers.

No exact original tag-location accuracy, missed fix process or
UTC-vs-GPS clock basis is independently calibrated. A median
GPS-use-center distance of 0.019 km **must not** be described as
animals actually coming within 19 meters of one another.
A monthly utilization centroid is not a same-time animal position.

the nineteenth tracked bird has no <=5-km graph edge.

Within 2019 the smaller component remains relatively

spreading is not identical across the source's two
geographic components.

## Why this revises the previous winter narrative

The old study defined winter as months **Jan, Feb, Dec of
the SAME calendar year**, which could sound like coverage of
the whole northern winter season. The **actual original
GPS data contain no eligible turkey fixes in December
for EITHER 2019 or 2020**. The previous winter versus summer
source contrasts are therefore empirically
**January–February vs June–August in 2019** (with August
incompletely tracked), and
**January–February vs June–July in 2020**.
They do not constitute a continuous December–February
overwintering season. This is a meaningful temporal
coverage limitation, not merely terminology.

Earlier source-verified 2020 annual-center analysis showed
five 10–25-km dyads all incident to **one** source animal,
and one original bird's Jan–Feb to summer GPS-center
shift of 14.5236 km versus a six-core median of
0.4550 km. The month-wise observations now show the
separation is already apparent in **March**, while all
original birds were tracked sufficiently. This is a
new supported temporal distinction from the previous
season-wide aggregate; those five dyads are nevertheless
**one focal animal's repeated pair memberships**, not
five independent animal-event replications.

## Next ecological discrimination

A simple mechanistic explanation is that **one animal shifting its
seasonal utilization range can produce several correlated
individual-pair convergence observations**, and a local
core of birds can also expand within season.
The observed 2020 late-February/early-March transition in
pair-center separation is consistent with changing
spatial opportunity, but does NOT identify its cause.

The next strong empirical test is **a separate published
wild-turkey population/source**, with original tag
life stage, sex, local capture/site identity,
source GPS accuracy and recording schedule,
to test whether this timing/reconfiguration recurs
without relying on the same regional Movebank source.
No new mechanistic or global claim is permitted
from this one source's repeated animals or pair counts.
