# Two seasonal geographic-sharing configurations in source-verified wild-turkey GPS data

**22 October 2026 — source-year descriptive ecology, explicitly POST-INSPECTION.**
This analysis uses the same original published Movebank GPS records, animals,
months, original recorded source times and original day-shift reference as the
preceding spatial influence and dual-anchor studies. It is a new source-original
GPS *geographic decomposition* with a design fixed after prior outcomes were
already examined, **not** an independently preregistered test.

## Central biological result

**Two distinct configurations of seasonal geographic co-use occur in two
different annual sets of wild turkeys from one published source:**

1. **2019 — day-specific proximity inside TWO distinct geographic GPS-use
   components.** At the <=5-km pair annual-GPS-center separation
   threshold, the original source forms disconnected graphs with
   **11 animals/53 dyads** and **7 animals/20 dyads**. Both components
   have greater winter than summer source-GPS same-day proximity
   relative to an original previous/next-day trajectory control,
   at <=500 m and <=15 minutes, under both directions of
   original focal-animal anchoring and all **12 deterministic source
   subsampling draws**. Original/reversed winter-minus-summer
   L1 contrasts are **+0.084475/+0.089585** in the larger component
   and **+0.058482/+0.055655** in the smaller. Disconnected
   sample graphs are **not verified independent biological populations**.
2. **2020 — marked winter rapprochement of animals whose ANNUAL GPS-use
   centers are separated.** The five source-qualified dyads whose
   animal YEAR-centers are **10–25 km apart** have a median distance
   between their WINTER GPS-use centers of **0.172 km**, versus
   **14.671 km between SUMMER centers**. Winter center distance is
   smaller in **all 5/5** dyads; the *paired mean winter-minus-summer*
   center separation is **−15.461 km**. This is a large, directly
   documented seasonal shift in the configuration of recorded GPS
   space use. It does **not** prove interaction or a single biological
   flock; the five dyads may share animal identities and the
   tag/location/true population precision remain unvalidated.

These configurations **do not require the same behavioral mechanism**.
They help explain why an across-source-year statement that the turkey
2019/2020 500-m winter *daily proximity excess* is universally
positive is too strong: in 2020 the 14 **closest annual-center dyads**
have an original/reversed 500-m L1 winter-minus-summer value
**−0.016652/−0.000425**, whereas the original all-21-dyad
<=25-km cohort shows **+0.026644/+0.027211**.
Geographic proximity of seasonal average ranges and excess
same-day time-specific proximity over adjacent-day GPS paths
are **different estimands**.

The source-specific existence of a strong winter center
convergence among annual-center-separated dyads, and
distinct geographically conditioned nearness patterns in
2019 vs 2020, is the main empirical result. No quantified
universal seasonal animal ecology rule is asserted.

## Original source provenance and scope

Original Movebank:
- southeastern-US wild turkey *Meleagris gallopavo*,
  [10.5441/001/1.671](https://doi.org/10.5441/001/1.671);
- Connecticut American black bear *Ursus americanus*,
  [10.5441/001/1.706](https://doi.org/10.5441/001/1.706);
- western-Virginia white-tailed deer *Odocoileus virginianus*,
  [10.5441/001/1.718](https://doi.org/10.5441/001/1.718).

Full original publisher MD5 + pinned original-GPS SHA256, animal/tag
reference and positional-clock multiset were reverified. The
fixed complete-source 2019/2020 original GPS fix counts were
**435,214 / 148,394 / 371,080** respectively. All 576
prior original dual-anchor GPS source response scores replayed
exactly in the precursor source-geography audit and again in
these original-source runs.

Among original dual-anchor-qualified dyads, turkey has
**19 animals (112 dyads) in 2019** and **7 animals (21 dyads) in
2020**, with **0 repeated original animal IDs** across years.
This establishes distinct source-tagged individuals, NOT
independently sampled local demes. White-tailed deer instead
reuses all eleven 2019 source-tagged animals in 2020.
Original reference/event fields do not certify capture-group
labels, actual GPS positional error or source-clock UTC/GPS
basis, so neither a spatially disconnected set of points nor
a 25-km centroid risk set can be called a known local
biological population.

## 1. What the 2019 geographically disconnected components actually show

The year-2019 <=5-km source animal graph contains two
disconnected components, defined *before scoring* solely
by qualified dyad eligibility and annual day-equal
unit-sphere GPS centroid separations.
A single animal has no qualified <=5-km graph edge
and is not in either component.

| 2019 turkey source graph at <=5 km | Source animals | Eligible dyads | Original winter−summer daily L1 within 500 m | Reversed-anchor daily L1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Larger geographic component | 11 | 53 | +0.084475 | +0.089585 |
| Smaller geographic component | 7 | 20 | +0.058482 | +0.055655 |

Both original and reversed anchors have **12/12 positive**
winter−summer L1 source-subsample contrasts in each component.
Both component estimates are descriptive and dependent:
source animals appear in multiple dyads and all
recording times arise from one published regional study.
The disconnected annual-center components are geographical,
not necessarily different capture programs, winter flocks,
genetic populations or independent replicates.

This result strengthens the previous 2019 source-level
observation and identifies an original-source geometric
sample structure where it occurs. A separate validation
of publisher study locations and sampled demographic
groups is still required before saying that two
independent ecological populations replicated the effect.

## 2. A measured spatial reconfiguration of annual-center-separated turkeys in 2020

For each original source animal, reconstruct the winter
and summer GPS-use centroid independently, giving **each
original observed calendar date equal weight** after
normalizing its spherical mean of GPS fixes; each
season requires ≥7 original dates in ≥2 months.
Define winter=(January, February, December) within one
calendar year and summer=(June, July, August).
The winter category is **NOT one uninterrupted period**
spanning a calendar boundary or a verified flocking stage.
Animal pairs are the same matched source-pair set as
the previous 15-minute 2-anchor trajectory analysis.

| Turkey year | ANNUAL-center separation band | Source dyads | Winter season-center pair-distance median | Summer median | Pairs with winter center closer |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2019 | ≤5 km | 73 | 1.026 km | 2.508 km | 55/73 |
| 2019 | 10–25 km | 34 | 22.000 km | 22.583 km | 23/34 |
| 2020 | ≤5 km | 14 | 0.509 km | 2.916 km | 11/14 |
| 2020 | 5–10 km | 2 | 0.114 km | 10.706 km | 2/2 |
| 2020 | **10–25 km** | **5** | **0.172 km** | **14.671 km** | **5/5** |
| 2020 | ≤25 km | 21 | 0.370 km | 6.282 km | 18/21 |

For turkey 2020's five 10–25-km annual-center pairs:
- mean winter between-animal season centers **0.664 km**;
- mean summer center separation **16.125 km**;
- paired winter minus summer mean distance **−15.461 km**;
- median winter/summer center separation **0.172/14.671 km**;
- **five out of five dyads** show smaller winter than summer
  center separation.

**What is supported:** these recorded animals shared
much closer average geographic space in that winter
calendar category than in summer, even though their
annual source-GPS centers were 10–25 km apart.
This is a directly measured *seasonal geographical
configuration*, not a conclusion inferred from
total spatial overlap alone.

**What is not supported:** that five distinct winter
flocks formed, that their close average ranges imply
direct real-time encounters, that there is active
winter flocking caused by resource selection, or
that the GPS tags resolve all true animal positions
to 172 m precision. Original source time/accuracy,
migration/reproduction state, weather and capture
sites are not fully independently verified.

A particularly important distinction:
the 14 closest 2020 annual-center dyads also have
closer winter than summer season centers in
11/14 cases, but their **500-m same-day versus
adjacent-day trajectory L1 season contrast is
negative or near zero**. Therefore winter
geographical convergence **does not logically
imply increased timing-specific proximity surplus**.
Conversely a positive proximity surplus can
arise under several different geographic layouts.

## 3. Related ecology and plausible but unresolved mechanisms

Wild turkeys are a useful natural-history context for
testing winter spatial reconfiguration. Published
research describes winter sex-specific flocks and
movement away from winter ranges toward breeding
areas, as well as group-level GPS use and female
social arrangements:
- [Ulrey et al. 2026, Scientific Reports](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-34883-7),
  which compares wild-turkey winter ranges and
  breeding-season revisited areas;
- [Reproductive asynchrony within social groups
  of female eastern wild turkeys (2023)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10266966/),
  which uses hourly GPS records and examines
  documented female group arrangements.

**These are external mechanistic hypotheses, not
evidence that the specific tagged birds in this
Movebank source are members of a documented flock
or identical to those published samples.**
The published reference data do not yet supply
per-animal confirmed capture-group identity,
tag-specific horizontal GPS location error,
or independently calibrated UTC clock type
for this source. Animal sex, breeding stage,
flock and habitat-resource layers have not
been added to the current source-level
spatial test. No source causal effect,
competition or dietary partitioning is inferred.

## 4. Contrast with white-tailed deer and black bear source

White-tailed deer: both original annual/source-year
study sets are re-analysed, including all 11
2019 animal identities reused in 2020. The
2019 source's <=5-km graph has one adequately
supported 8-animal/26-dyad component and
a 2-animal/1-dyad STOP component. The 2020
source has a 12-animal/51-dyad adequate
component and 4-animal subgraphs too
small for the fixed L1 support gate.
The white-tailed deer same-day/adjacent-day
surplus remains small and A/B-anchor unstable
at 1 km. Its pair-centroid seasonal
distance contrasts are not consistent
with a taxon-general wild-turkey convergence
rule: in 2020, 205 qualified deer dyads
have an overall mean winter−summer
season-center separation of just +0.029 km,
with winter centers closer in **96/205**
pairs.

Black bear: 2019/2020 original-source
sub-hour adjacent-day sample support
remains inadequate under the previously
fixed >=8 dyads/3 animals gate. This is
**NOT ESTIMABLE**, not a zero ecological effect.
No error or missing temporal comparison is
imputed as a biological lack of co-use.

## Strongest defensible ecological story

**The same species can show different seasonal
configurations of geographical co-use in
different tracked source-year cohorts**:
- in 2019, greater winter day-specific
  GPS proximity relative to adjacent-day
  paths occurs inside TWO disconnected
  qualified annual-center graph components;
- in 2020, winter seasonal GPS-use centers
  of some animals whose annual centers are
  separated by 10–25 km become dramatically
  closer, while the nearest annual-center
  pairs do not show the same winter 500-m
  *day-specific proximity surplus*.

The distinction between *which pair of
animals shares a region in a season* and
*whether those animals' recorded positions
align more on the same date than on
neighboring dates* is empirically supported.
**The causes, true social/encounter processes,
independence of local populations and any
cross-taxon general rule remain unestablished.**

The next independent test should use
publisher-verified original capture groups
and tag source clock/accuracy, and a
separate original GPS source population
where winter/social context can be
specified *without first looking at the
proximity outcomes*. The current source
can motivate a source-independent hypothesis,
not constitute its proof.
