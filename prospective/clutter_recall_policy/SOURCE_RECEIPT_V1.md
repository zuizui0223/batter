## Source experimental architecture

Five adult *Pipistrellus kuhlii* were followed through four stages:

1. large, less-cluttered flight room;
2. first encounter with a small constantly cluttered chamber for two months;
3. return to the large room for six months;
4. second clutter encounter for two weeks.

The peer-reviewed source additionally reports:
- all five bats were naive to the long-duration clutter treatment at onset;
- four of five progressively shortened pre-takeoff inter-group interval (IGI) across the first two-month clutter encounter;
- after six months back in the large room, the learned short-IGI strategy was expressed immediately on clutter re-entry;
- two bats were also tested in an acoustically enhanced clutter chamber and expressed a similar learned strategy.

These are inherited source results, not new outcomes of this programme.

## Mechanistic question

The current bat programme has established:
- persistent low-dimensional individual policy;
- policy identity after shared peer/day effects are removed;
- no positive mapping from policy distance to synchronous spatial separation.

The unresolved causal question is the origin and persistence of the personal policy state.

This source permits a sharper question:

> **Does an experimentally acquired sensorimotor policy survive a long interval in another environment and reappear immediately when the task class returns?**

A positive answer is direct evidence that learned policy can be stored without continuous expression or spatial exclusion.

## New prospective endpoint

The primary new endpoint is **individual-level recall fidelity**, not the already-published group comparison.

If the public archive provides individual identity and repeated IGI observations:

- estimate each bat's learned late-stage clutter state from the first encounter;
- hold out the second encounter;
- ask whether second-encounter observations are better predicted by that bat's own late learned state than by other bats' late learned states.

This is an identity-preserving recall test.

## Enhanced-clutter secondary

If source-native identifiers expose the two enhanced-clutter bats and sufficient observations:

- compare the same learned-state predictor against their enhanced-clutter re-entry values.

This asks whether the stored policy transfers to a changed acoustic scene of the same task class.

It cannot rescue a failed primary same-clutter recall test.

## Causal interpretation ceiling

If supported, the allowed claim is:

> an experimentally acquired individual sensorimotor policy can persist across months without continuous use and can be recalled when the relevant environmental class returns.

This would strongly weaken explanations based only on:
- immediate behavioral inertia;
- continuously maintained local spatial partition;
- short-lived contemporaneous environmental state.

It would **not** establish:
- the neural substrate of memory;
- reinforcement learning as the unique algorithm;
- that the field H/V policy has the same biological basis;
- universal bat policy recall.
