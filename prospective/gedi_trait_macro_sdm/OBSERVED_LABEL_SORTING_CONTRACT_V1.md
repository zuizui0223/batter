## Question
Within a common GBIF dataset, year and 200-km block, does predicted vertical return-height profile help discriminate the location of two recorded species labels on distinct forest cells, and can sharing profile slopes along a physical aspect-ratio axis improve prediction in withheld blocks? Species and location margins are conditioned on in each comparison. Neither label is an absence; a cell can record both species. This observed-label allocation is not calibrated relative biological occurrence, habitat choice, coexistence, or actual altitude. Common source and year do not prove common survey effort or equal species detectability. Unmeasured terrain, roost, accessibility, phenology, source-specific recording and phylogeny remain limitations. No p-values or inferential claims based on composite pair scores.

## Models, held-out composite log scores
For two labels s,t and their recorded cells x,y, model observed orientation vs swapped as logistic of Delta = [f_s(x)-f_t(x)]-[f_s(y)-f_t(y)]. Neutral B0 log probability -log(2). Candidate f_s(x) is a linear species-specific score. Standardize environmental features using only training unique cells, with zero-variance features set to zero; fixed AR z-score across all 14 trait taxa, not estimated from outcomes. Baseline covariates: six CHELSA encoded-then-converted metrics bio1,4,5,6,12,15; tree/shrub/grass/cropland/built-up class fractions 10,20,30,40,50; area-mean RH98. B1 uses species-specific baseline slopes plus shared AR×baseline slopes (centered residuals orthogonal to constant and AR). B2 adds species-specific centered R50 slopes, ridge on that vector. B3 adds R50 slope decomposition gamma*AR_z + centered species deviations orthogonal to AR. B2 and B3 can express the same contrasts; different shrinkage along trait axis (ridge on gamma and residual coefficients) is the only change, so B3-B2 tests trait-informed regularization, not extra degrees of freedom or proof of trait causality. Fixed ridge 0.1 sum(beta^2) for all fitted coefficients; analytic gradient, fixed solver tolerances, no tuning.
