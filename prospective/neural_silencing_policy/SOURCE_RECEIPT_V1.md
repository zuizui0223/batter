## Source experimental leverage

The paper reports reversible chemogenetic suppression of excitatory neurons in the inferior colliculus using inhibitory DREADDs.

Behavioral task:
- free flight through one of three randomized curtain openings;
- synchronized ultrasound and stereo/high-speed video;
- treatment conditions include baseline/control, saline and DREADD ligand;
- central auditory suppression changes navigation performance and flight trajectory.

Source subject architecture:
- seven adult big brown bats total;
- **four DREADDs-treated bats participated in behavioral treatment/testing**;
- one additional DREADDs bat was used for ABR only;
- two sham-surgery bats contributed behavioral controls.

The intended primary biological replication unit for a neural-perturbation identity test is the four DREADDs behavioral bats.

## Mechanistic question

The current batter programme shows that:
- a low-dimensional movement bias predicts unseen tasks;
- wild policy individuality need not map to spatial partitioning;
- an external sensory masker shifts movement while preserving personal movement bias.

The next causal question is deeper:

> **When central auditory processing itself is reversibly impaired, does an identity-bearing movement bias remain recoverable across the neural perturbation?**

This asks whether the portable personal bias is downstream of one intact sensory-processing regime or remains expressed through compensatory sensorimotor reorganization.

## Exact-design warning frozen now

With four DREADDs behavioral bats, a pure individual-label exchangeability test has:

[
4! = 24
]

exact mappings.

Therefore the smallest attainable exact one-sided probability is:

[
1/24 = 0.04167.
]

A 5% exact test is possible only if the observed mapping is uniquely at or near the extreme tail.

This design limitation is accepted prospectively.

Do not later:
- add sham bats merely to obtain a smaller p;
- use trial rows as independent biological replicates;
- replace exact permutation with asymptotic p-values.

## Claim ceiling

If supported:

> **An individual movement bias remains identifiable despite reversible central auditory-processing suppression.**

This would strengthen the interpretation of personal policy as a robust sensorimotor state.

It would not prove:
- where the bias is neurally stored;
- that inferior-colliculus activity is irrelevant to the bias;
- equivalence to Rhino I/M or P. hastatus H/V;
- learning versus biomechanics as the origin of the bias.
