## Cross-source synthesis, exploratory only
Whitworth Peruvian mammal data: canopy Q falls with disturbance, beta rises mainly through within-canopy concentration. Haysom Borneo mammal subset: canopy Q rises, beta falls as alpha gain exceeds C change, consistent across weighting. Yoh Brazilian bat old-secondary canopy foraging-affinity guild Q rises; guild beta sign not robust. Thus reject a simple universal mammal-versus-bat opposite direction narrative; same broad locomotor taxon has contrasting recorded canopy diversity patterns in different contexts. Different disturbance intensities, recovery ages, detection, species composition and estimands confounded; test context as a prospective hypothesis, not established cause. Original Haysom already reported terrestrial richness decline and guild composition/relative detections in logged forest.
