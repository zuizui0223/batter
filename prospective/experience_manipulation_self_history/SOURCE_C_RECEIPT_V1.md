## Experimental architecture inherited from the source

The source study used young Egyptian fruit bats captured before independent flight or born in the laboratory.

Before release to the open colony, juveniles were housed under one of two early-life environmental conditions:

- **enriched**: frequent environmental changes/enrichment requiring repeated trial-and-error interaction;
- **impoverished**: stable environment with minimal environmental change.

The source paper reports that:
- conditions were maintained for at least approximately two months before release;
- animals from source colonies were distributed across the two environmental conditions to reduce origin bias;
- bats were later released to an open colony and allowed to forage freely;
- GPS devices were fitted after the second foraging night;
- outdoor movement was analysed for 19 bats, with a mean of approximately 39 recorded foraging nights per bat.

The published paper already establishes that enriched and impoverished bats differ in outdoor activity, maximal distance and explored area. Those are inherited findings, not new outcomes here.

## New question

The new endpoint is not whether enrichment changes gross exploration.

It is:

> **Does experimentally imposed early-life environmental experience change how strongly a bat's own recent spatial history predicts its later movement after release?**

This directly tests whether early experience affects the **maintenance architecture** of individual spatial specialization.

## Primary biological contrast fixed before Source C GPS route outcome

Because the published treatment increases exploration, the direction of its effect on personal-history reuse is not theoretically unique.

Therefore the primary Source C contrast is **two-sided**:

> late personal-history advantage differs between enriched and impoverished juveniles.

No directional enrichment > impoverishment or impoverishment > enrichment hypothesis is authorized as the primary.
