## Primary object

The randomized treatment contrast is not the treatment mean effect.

It asks whether early environmental enrichment changes the **dispersion of individual behavioral change vectors** after the common treatment-group shift is removed.

[
D=V_{enriched}-V_{impoverished}.
]

## State A — SUPPORTED_INDIVIDUALIZATION

Requirements:
- D > 0;
- frozen one-sided stratified randomization p <= 0.05.

Allowed conclusion:

> **Randomized enriched early experience increased among-individual divergence in behavioral change beyond the shared treatment effect. Early experience therefore generated individual differentiation rather than merely shifting a group mean.**

This would be direct experimental evidence for:

[
	ext{early experience}
ightarrow
	ext{formation of behavioral individuality}.
]

Boundary:
- the result concerns the three frozen laboratory personality traits;
- it does not by itself establish movement-policy individuality;
- it does not identify the neural or biomechanical mechanism.

## State B — POSITIVE_BUT_UNRESOLVED

Condition:
- D > 0;
- p > 0.05.

Allowed conclusion:

> Enriched bats show greater residual divergence in the frozen behavioral-change vector, but the randomized evidence is insufficient to establish treatment-induced individualization.

Do not:
- switch to one trait;
- select a subset of bats;
- use outdoor survivors;
- use PCA;
- change baseline definition.

## State C — NO_INCREASE

Condition:
- D <= 0.

Allowed conclusion:

> The randomized enrichment treatment did not increase among-individual divergence in the frozen laboratory behavioral-change vector.

If D < 0:
- report that the direction is compatible with reduced dispersion / canalization;
- do **not** open a new reverse one-sided confirmatory test after seeing the result.

This state would imply that the paper's broader environmental effect is more consistent with common calibration or later context-specific expression than with increased laboratory personality differentiation.

## Relation to the published mean-effect result

The source paper's established result is that enriched early experience predicts later wild foraging behavior.

The new primary asks a different question:

- **mean shift:** do enriched bats behave differently on average?
- **individualization:** does enrichment create more divergence among individuals?

These are not interchangeable.

Possible synthesis:

### Published mean effect + new individualization support

Early experience changes both:
- the average behavioral state;
- the degree of individual differentiation.

### Published mean effect + no individualization

Early experience mainly calibrates a common state or later ecological expression rather than increasing the amount of laboratory individuality.

## Relation to the juvenile-history batter result

The existing juvenile-history programme shows that later own history becomes more predictive of later movement than the earliest history.

A positive randomized early-experience result would add causal leverage:

> personal history is not only predictive; experimentally altered early experience can change the amount of individual differentiation.

A negative result would instead separate:
- identity-specific historical refinement;
from
- environmental enrichment as a generator of extra among-individual divergence.
