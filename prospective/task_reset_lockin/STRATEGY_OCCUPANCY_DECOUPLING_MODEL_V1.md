# Strategy–occupancy decoupling model v1

## Status

**POST-JAE THEORETICAL SYNTHESIS.**

This note formalizes the empirical distinction between persistent individual movement strategy and spatial partitioning.

It is a general model class, not a claim that one linear dynamical law has been identified from the bat data.

## 1. Three ecological objects

Let individual i carry a persistent low-dimensional movement phenotype:

[
\boldsymbol\theta_i\in\mathbb R^p.
]

This is the stable component supported empirically by repeated movement behavior.

In environment/task e, the phenotype is expressed as a realized movement state:

[
\mathbf z_{iet}
=
\boldsymbol\mu_e
+
\mathbf L_e\boldsymbol\theta_i
+
\mathbf m_{i,e}
+
\boldsymbol\varepsilon_{iet}.
]

Here:
- (oldsymbol\mu_e): common environmental/task effect;
- (mathbf L_e): context-specific expression of persistent individual differences;
- (mathbf m_{i,e}): learned or scene-specific solution;
- (oldsymbol\varepsilon): unresolved within-individual realization.

Physical space use is a further mapping:

[
\mathbf x_{iet}
=
\mathbf H_e\mathbf z_{iet}.
]

The key ecological point is that:

[
\boldsymbol\theta_i
\rightarrow
\mathbf z_{iet}
\rightarrow
\mathbf x_{iet}
]

contains **two mappings**, not one identity relation.

Individuals can therefore differ persistently in movement strategy while producing strongly overlapping physical occupancy.

---

## 2. Persistent strategy variation

Let:

[
\Sigma_\theta
=
\operatorname{Var}(\boldsymbol\theta_i).
]

A simple population-scale magnitude of persistent strategy individuality is:

[
P
=
\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_\theta).
]

Large P means biological identity occupies different regions of movement-strategy space.

This corresponds conceptually to:
- repeatable individual movement-policy identity;
- behavioral-type variation;
- temporal self-predictiveness.

It does **not** imply spatial segregation.

---

## 3. Spatial expression of strategy differences

Define the local strategy-to-space operator:

[
\mathbf A_e
=
\mathbf H_e\mathbf L_e.
]

The component of spatial among-individual variation generated by persistent strategy is:

[
S_e
=
\operatorname{tr}
\left(
\mathbf A_e
\Sigma_\theta
\mathbf A_e^T
\right).
]

This is the expected squared magnitude of strategy-derived spatial differentiation, up to the usual factor of two for pairwise differences.

For two independent individuals:

[
E\|\mathbf A_e\theta_i-\mathbf A_e\theta_j\|^2
=
2S_e.
]

Thus **strategy differentiation and spatial differentiation are mathematically separate**.

---

## 4. A normalized strategy-to-space coupling

After variables are placed on a defined standardized scale, define:

[
\kappa_e
=
\frac{
\operatorname{tr}
(\mathbf A_e\Sigma_\theta\mathbf A_e^T)
}{
\|\mathbf A_e\|_2^2
\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_\theta)
}.
]

For nonzero denominator:

[
0\le \kappa_e\le1.
]

Interpretation:

- (kappa_e\approx1): persistent strategy variation is strongly expressed as physical-space differentiation along the maximally transmitted direction;
- intermediate (kappa_e): only part of strategy differentiation becomes spatial separation;
- (kappa_e\approx0): persistent strategy differences are largely hidden from the measured spatial-segregation axis.

The zero case occurs whenever the dominant individual-difference directions lie near the null space of the strategy-to-space mapping.

This supplies a general mathematical reason why:

[
P>0
\quad\text{can coexist with}\quad
S\approx0.
]

No paradox is required.

---

## 5. Biological examples of a low-coupling regime

Two individuals may differ persistently in:
- flight intensity;
- turning/maneuvering style;
- approach geometry;
- route timing;
- vertical excursion pattern;
- search sequence;

while still:
- visiting the same patch;
- using the same altitude range;
- crossing the same air volume;
- or occupying overlapping environmental hypervolumes.

Such individuals are specialized in **how they solve the task**, not necessarily in exclusive ownership of the task space.

---

## 6. Stable segregation and active avoidance are also different

Spatial segregation can arise from at least two sources.

### Persistent strategy-derived segregation

[
S_e
=
\operatorname{tr}(A_e\Sigma_\theta A_e^T).
]

This exists even when individuals never respond to one another in real time.

### Interaction-dependent separation

Add a contemporaneous interaction term:

[
\mathbf x_{iet}
=
\mathbf H_e\mathbf z_{iet}
+
\gamma_e\mathbf C_{-i,e,t}
+
\epsilon_{iet},
]

where (C_{-i,e,t}) summarizes local conspecific state.

Then:
- (gamma_e=0): no required active co-presence adjustment;
- (gamma_e>0): current traffic can generate additional separation.

Therefore there are three distinct quantities:

[
\boxed{
P:\text{ persistent strategy individuality}
}
]

[
\boxed{
S:\text{ realized stable spatial segregation}
}
]

[
\boxed{
I:\text{ interaction-dependent separation}
}
]

No equality among P, S and I is mathematically necessary.

---

## 7. Four empirical regimes

The P–S plane yields four biologically different cases.

### High P, high S — partitioned specialization

Individuals retain different strategies and those differences map onto different physical regions/resources.

Classical niche-partitioning interpretations are natural here.

### High P, low S — overlapping specialization

Individuals retain distinct movement strategies but use strongly overlapping physical space.

This is the central regime supported by the current *P. hastatus* / *Hypsignathus* synthesis.

### Low P, high S — context-driven sorting

Individuals are not persistently differentiated in strategy, but transient environmental or social conditions generate spatial separation.

Persistent individual specialization is not required.

### Low P, low S — approximate exchangeability

Neither persistent strategy identity nor spatial partitioning is strong.

These regimes should not be collapsed into one scalar specialization index.

---

## 8. Relation to nested individualized niches

Nested or overlapping individualized environmental niches already demonstrate that specialization need not imply disjoint niche hypervolumes.

The present model asks a different question:

> **How strongly does persistent variation in movement strategy map into realized spatial separation?**

A nested-niche analysis characterizes the geometry of realized environmental use.

The P–S decomposition additionally distinguishes:
- persistent generator-level individuality;
- spatial expression of that individuality;
- active interaction-dependent separation.

This distinction matters whenever different behavioral solutions can lead to similar occupancy.

---

## 9. Empirical correspondence in batter

### P: persistent strategy individuality

Supported by:
- laboratory cross-configuration individual movement identity;
- fixed-axis external *Carollia* validation;
- wild *P. hastatus* H/V policy carrier;
- strict-past self-history prediction;
- peer-day residual identity.

### S: stable spatial segregation

Terrain-relative JAE diagnostics:
- personal vertical strategy fidelity supported in four evaluable panels;
- positive added terrain-relative segregation supported in **0/4**.

### I: interaction-dependent separation

Synchronous co-use:
- positive extra vertical separation supported in **1/4**;
- three panels have no supported positive effect and compatibility upper endpoints near 1–2 m.

### Direct P-to-S mapping test

Wild *P. hastatus* dyads:
- 2022: rho(policy distance, co-use separation)=+0.188, p=0.2743;
- 2023: rho=-0.190, p=0.6887.

Thus persistent strategy distance does not monotonically map onto pairwise spatial separation in the directly matched field system.

---

## 10. What the model does not claim

The current data do not estimate a literal matrix (A_e) or a numerical (kappa_e).

The operator formulation is a conceptual linearization showing how the quantities can be independent.

Do not claim:
- a fitted causal transfer operator;
- a measured null space;
- exact linearity;
- universal low (kappa) across bats;
- absence of competition.

Historical competition can help create persistent strategies even if current maintenance no longer requires ongoing separation.

---

## 11. Main ecological principle

The most useful general statement is:

> **The degree of individual specialization and the degree to which that specialization is expressed as spatial partitioning are separate ecological properties.**

For movement systems:

> **individuals may differ persistently in how they move through a shared environment even when those differences do not create exclusive regions of space.**

This turns the empirical JAE result into a general mechanism question:

[
\text{what maintains }P,
\qquad
\text{and what controls }\kappa?
]

These are different questions.

---

## 12. Testable comparative predictions

Across systems:

### Prediction 1 — task degeneracy lowers spatial coupling

When many movement solutions reach the same resources/destinations:
- P may be high;
- (kappa) may be low;
- spatial overlap remains high.

### Prediction 2 — exclusive resources raise spatial coupling

When alternative strategies require different physical resources or corridors:
- persistent strategy differentiation should more strongly translate into spatial segregation.

### Prediction 3 — active competition raises I without necessarily raising P

Short-term competitor exposure can increase instantaneous separation even if individuals lack stable strategy differences.

### Prediction 4 — learning can increase P without increasing S

If individuals acquire different familiar solutions to the same shared task, self-history predictability can rise while physical overlap remains high.

These predictions distinguish formation, maintenance and spatial expression of individual specialization.

---

## Bottom line

The general model is not:

[
\text{individual specialization}=\text{niche partitioning}.
]

It is:

[
\boxed{
\text{persistent movement strategy}
\xrightarrow[\kappa_e]{\text{context-dependent mapping}}
\text{spatial expression}
}
]

with contemporaneous interaction as a separate layer.

The empirical novelty lies in measuring those layers separately rather than assuming that persistent individual differentiation is maintained by the spatial separation it may or may not produce.
