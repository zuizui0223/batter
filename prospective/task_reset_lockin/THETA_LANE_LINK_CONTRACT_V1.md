## Question

Is the configuration-specific lane-placement term partly generated by the same portable personal parameter theta?

Test:

[
mathbf c_{i,e}=oldsymbolmu_e+mathbf b,	heta_{i,-e}+epsilon_{i,e},
]

where:
- (mathbf c_{i,e}) is the individual's mean absolute 3-D trajectory centroid in environment e;
- (oldsymbolmu_e) is an environment-specific intercept;
- (	heta_{i,-e}) is FlightIntensity estimated only from other environments;
- (mathbf b) is one common 3-D slope across Env1–Env3.

A positive result means the portable personal control coordinate predicts part of the absolute lane/location chosen in a new obstacle configuration.

## Route target cells

Use exactly Env1–Env3.

For each bat × environment:
- retain route-valid trajectories;
- require >=2 route-valid trajectories;
- route centroid for each trajectory = mean of its 101 arc-length-normalized XYZ points;
- target cell centroid = equal-trajectory mean of those route centroids.

This structural rule is frozen before linkage output.

Expected source-count support from filenames:
- Env1: B, C, D;
- Env2: B, C, D, E;
- Env3: C, D, E.

If route-valid support differs from this 10-cell expectation, stop rather than retune.

## Portable theta

Use the authoritative transparent FlightIntensity scalar.

For every bat i and target environment e:
- compute a centroid within each other observed environment;
- average those environment centroids equally;
- require >=2 other environments.

No FlightIntensity from target environment e enters (	heta_{i,-e}).

## Held-out-bat prediction

Leave one biological bat out.

Training data:
all eligible bat×environment route-centroid cells from the other bats.

Fit separately for X, Y and Z:

### Baseline
environment-specific intercept only.

### Theta model
environment-specific intercept + one common theta slope.

Predict the held-out bat's eligible cells.

Per cell squared 3-D error:
[
E=||mathbf c-hat{mathbf c}||^2.
]

Bat-level gain:
equal-environment mean
[
G_i=E_{baseline}-E_{	heta}.
]

Programme statistic:
equal-bat mean (G).

Positive gain means theta improves unseen-individual lane prediction beyond knowing the obstacle configuration.

## Exact identity null

Candidate route bats are expected to be B,C,D,E.

Precompute each bat's theta profile for Env1–Env3.

Enumerate all 4! = 24 global assignments of complete theta profiles to route-bat labels.

For each assignment:
- repeat the entire leave-one-bat-out fitting and prediction;
- preserve route centroids and environment labels;
- break only correspondence between personal theta and personal lane placement.

Exact one-sided:
[
p=#(G_{perm}>=G_{obs})/24.
]

The identity assignment is included in the 24 permutations.

## Support rule

Call linkage supported only if:
- G > 0;
- exact p <= 0.05;
- >=3/4 held-out bats have positive G_i.

## Interpretation

Supported:

> a single portable personal movement coordinate predicts a common direction of individual lane displacement across multiple obstacle configurations.

This would reduce the context term from an arbitrary individual-specific route to a low-dimensional reaction of spatial placement to theta.

Unsupported:

> portable movement intensity and configuration-specific lane placement are separable individual components under this dataset.

## Claim ceiling

Even if supported, theta does not uniquely determine the route.

The model does not include:
- obstacle geometry descriptors;
- learning order;
- sensing state;
- start-state provenance.

It tests only a cross-level linear linkage between portable policy and absolute lane placement.
