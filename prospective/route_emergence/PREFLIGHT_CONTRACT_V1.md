## Biological question

How can individually persistent 3-D movement corridors emerge without centralized allocation or active spatial partitioning?

The focal mechanistic model is **distributed route formation by exploration and reuse**:

1. an individual initially samples multiple viable movement solutions;
2. successful solutions acquire informational or performance value;
3. repeated use reduces search / sensory / decision costs;
4. the resulting personal corridor becomes more stereotyped and self-predictive;
5. different individuals may converge on different overlapping corridors without any central controller.

This is motivated by, but not equivalent to, the exploration-refinement hypothesis and learned stereotyped flight paths reported in other taxa and bat experiments.

## Critical identifiability distinction

A tracking deployment's first recorded session is **not automatically the individual's first experience of the environment**.

Therefore three evidence tiers are frozen before any trajectory-shape outcome is inspected.

### Tier A — route formation identifiable

At least one of the following is known independently of the focal movement outcome:
- newly volant / juvenile individual entering independent movement;
- first arrival in a demonstrably novel environment;
- experimental translocation to an unfamiliar environment;
- documented environmental reconfiguration that invalidates the previous route;
- documented first access to a newly introduced resource landscape.

Only Tier A supports a direct **route-emergence / exploration-to-stereotypy** claim.

### Tier B — route re-formation identifiable

The individual is experienced, but an independently documented reset creates a new movement problem:
- roost relocation;
- resource relocation or turnover;
- major habitat/access change;
- migration arrival into a newly occupied site with no evidence of prior familiarity.

Tier B can support **route re-formation after reset**, but not ontogenetic learning.

### Tier C — repeated use only

Ordinary tracking of experienced individuals in a familiar environment with no independent reset.

Tier C can test:
- within-deployment stabilization;
- persistence;
- recency dependence;
- route stereotypy.

Tier C **cannot** establish how the personal route originally formed.

## Source-only preflight

Before any route-shape outcome is opened, inspect only:
- reference-table metadata;
- deployment/site metadata;
- age / life stage;
- reproductive status;
- timestamps;
- individual/session identifiers;
- x-y coordinates only as needed to count sessions and date spans.

Do not calculate:
- route overlap;
- route distance;
- trajectory entropy;
- corridor width;
- self-similarity;
- vertical distributions;
- terrain-relative height;
- early-versus-late path convergence.

## Structural support gate

For each panel, record:
- number of individuals;
- number of sessions per individual;
- observed calendar span;
- whether >=6 sessions occur for an individual;
- whether those sessions span >=7 days;
- independently documented novelty/reset status.

A panel is structurally adequate for a three-phase chronological trajectory analysis only if >=5 biological individuals each have:
- >=6 eligible sessions;
- >=7 days between first and last eligible session.

This gate concerns only replication. It does **not** upgrade Tier C to route-formation evidence.

## Prospective outcome design if a Tier A/B panel exists

Freeze before trajectory outcomes:
- split each qualifying individual chronologically into early / middle / late thirds by complete sessions;
- represent route geometry at a spatial resolution fixed from positional accuracy, not selected from outcome;
- quantify held-out same-individual route predictability and corridor dispersion;
- primary prediction: early exploration > late exploration and early-to-late self-predictability increases;
- compare against whole-session temporal-order permutations or another predeclared chronological null preserving complete trajectory blocks;
- separate horizontal route stabilization from vertical/3-D stabilization.

Exact estimators must be separately frozen after source resolution and before any route outcome.

## If only Tier C panels exist

STOP the existing-data route-formation test.

Allowed conclusion:
> the archive contains repeated use of established personal strategies but does not observe their origin.

Then move to a genuinely prospective design involving juvenile emergence, translocation, route novelty, resource relocation, or another independently observed reset.

## Claim ceiling

Even a Tier A exploration-to-stereotypy pattern does not by itself prove reinforcement learning or fitness optimization.

A Tier C early-to-late trend must never be described as ontogeny, learning, canalization, or formation because tag deployment onset is not a biological-naivety boundary.
