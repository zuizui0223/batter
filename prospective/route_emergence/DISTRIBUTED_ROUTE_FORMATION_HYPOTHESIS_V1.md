# Distributed personal-airway formation hypothesis v1

## Core idea

Persistent bat “airways” need not be centrally assigned.

A plausible two-level mechanism is:

### Level 1 — socially seeded movement landscape

Conspecific information, colony traditions, lek/roost structure and shared knowledge can bias individuals toward a subset of destinations or commuting sectors.

This produces broad shared corridors without requiring a controller.

### Level 2 — personal 3-D refinement

Inside the shared movement landscape, each individual repeatedly solves the same movement problem under its own:
- prior experience;
- morphology and flight performance;
- resource knowledge;
- sensory information;
- microtopography / canopy geometry;
- local airflow.

Repeated success makes some solutions cheaper to reuse than to rediscover. Personal trajectories therefore become stereotyped even though different individuals' corridors can overlap.

## Why this fits the existing geometry

The JAE v0.4.0 evidence shows:
- persistent same-individual vertical organization;
- persistence after 500-m place × broad kinematic matching;
- multi-day to week-scale self-history information;
- no general positive terrain-relative segregation;
- no general co-use-dependent vertical separation.

That geometry is difficult to reconcile with continuous traffic-control-like lane allocation.

It is compatible with:
> **shared broad navigation information + individually refined overlapping corridors.**

## Phyllostomus-specific motivation

The source study for the 2021–2023 *P. hastatus* data reports:
- persistent colony-specific long-distance foraging directions across years and seasons;
- low exploration despite seasonal changes in resources;
- shared foraging areas that may reflect socially learned information / traditions;
- unresolved historical acquisition of those foraging preferences.

This makes *P. hastatus* a strong candidate for a hierarchy:
**colony-level route tradition → individual-level 3-D realization.**

The current adult tracking data cannot reconstruct the initial acquisition step.

## Hypsignathus-specific motivation

Adult males repeatedly use an established lek landscape while balancing mating and foraging, and revisit probability increases after prior use of a foraging area.

This provides another setting in which recurring shared landmarks/tasks can coexist with persistent individual solutions.

Again, the current deployment begins after the animals already know the landscape.

## Distinguishing predictions for future data

### Social seeding

If broad routes are socially acquired:
- juveniles should initially resemble maternal/group destination sectors;
- newly joining individuals should converge toward colony-level corridors;
- between-colony differences should exceed within-colony differences even before strong personal stereotypy develops.

### Personal refinement

With repeated independent use:
- within-individual trajectory dispersion should decline;
- held-out self-route predictability should increase;
- corridor width / trajectory entropy should decline;
- recent successful routes should disproportionately predict subsequent routes;
- individual differences should persist inside the same colony-level corridor.

### Performance matching

If morphology stabilizes the individual solution:
- wing loading / aspect ratio should predict the eventual 3-D corridor across different resource contexts;
- route reconfiguration after experimental resource movement should remain constrained by individual flight performance.

### Information-reuse / memory

If learned spatial information is the main carrier:
- translocation or resource relocation should temporarily increase exploration;
- repeated exposure should rebuild stereotypy;
- familiar-route advantage should transfer across nights but break when the task geometry is changed.

## Strong prospective design

Best design:
1. tag newly volant juveniles plus mothers / group members;
2. map roost, feeding trees and major canopy gaps;
3. record high-resolution x-y-z and local airflow;
4. track from first independent flights through repeated foraging;
5. identify colony-level corridor convergence and individual-level refinement separately;
6. optionally relocate a resource or introduce a novel feeder to create a known reset.

This design directly tests how a decentralized movement system produces stable overlapping personal airways.

## Claim ceiling

The phrase “distributed route formation” refers to an ecological mechanism class.

It does not imply:
- explicit communication of route instructions;
- collective optimization;
- central coordination;
- cultural transmission unless social-learning predictions are independently supported;
- reinforcement learning as a specific cognitive algorithm.
