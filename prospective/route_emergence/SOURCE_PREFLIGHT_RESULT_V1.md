## What the existing data can still say

The JAE result already establishes persistence of personal vertical organization.

The source literature adds a plausible **hierarchical distributed mechanism** for future testing:

1. broad destinations or commuting sectors may be socially shared at colony / group level;
2. individual experience, morphology, local resources and sensory constraints may refine a personal 3-D corridor inside that shared movement landscape.

This is a hypothesis, not an outcome of this preflight.

## Literature precedent

- Barchi et al. 2013 (J Exp Biol, doi:10.1242/jeb.073197): bats learned individually stereotyped paths in familiar clutter and resumed them after a month.
- Votier et al. 2017 (Proc R Soc B, doi:10.1098/rspb.2017.1068): exploration–refinement hypothesis, with more exploratory immature animals and stronger route/site fidelity in experienced breeders.
- Toledo et al. 2020 (Science, doi:10.1126/science.aax6904): wild bats showed repeated goal-directed routes and shortcuts consistent with learned spatial representations.
- Goldshtein et al. 2021 (Current Biology, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2021.11.010): maternal transport provided pups with spatial information later used during independent navigation.

These studies make route learning and social information biologically plausible but cannot retrospectively turn adult deployment onset in the present archive into a formation boundary.

## Next valid test

A direct route-emergence study needs an independently observed reset or naivety boundary:
- newly volant juveniles tracked through first independent flights;
- known dispersal into a new colony / site;
- experimental translocation;
- experimentally moved resource;
- roost relocation;
- major habitat reconfiguration.

The primary trajectory prediction can then be frozen as:
**exploration decreases, corridor stereotypy/self-predictability increases, and personal solutions emerge through repeated experience.**
