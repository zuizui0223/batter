## Important negative evidence / paradoxes

1. The idea **"crowding always makes bats separate their call frequencies" is empirically false**: 2012 CF-FM pair comparisons and 2026 colony-mixing longitudinal observations include convergence.
2. The idea **"acoustic interference necessarily causes animals to split their 3-D trajectories" is not supported** by 2021 FM dyads, which became MORE aligned than virtual-pair controls.
3. The idea **"spectral convergence necessarily destroys useful individual sensory information" is not supported** by CF-FM Doppler compensation and the 2026 spectral-window predation manipulation; but no *combined* group+prey+3D experiment establishes a direct compensatory substitution.
4. Acoustic frequency individuality, emitted waveform difference, beam-pointing individuality, 3D kinematics, memory and ecological resource-use individuality are distinct biological outcomes. A decrease in one cannot be called a decrease in every other.
5. 2012 paired-flight CF convergence and 2026 resting colony CF2 convergence do NOT estimate the same parameter or time scale. There is no published within-species same-apparatus paired/group-size 1,2,4 causal crossover in this crosswalk.

## Biologically nontrivial alternative hypotheses

H1 **Emission-channel differentiation (FM)**: At fixed encounter geometry and cue/effort controls, jamming from similar pulses could increase *pairwise terminal-frequency separation / reduce emitted-pulse similarity*, without necessarily changing position overlap. This is supported as a conditional pattern in 2018 quartet but not unconditionally in 2021 dyad.

H2 **Receiver-channel stabilization/sharing (CF-FM)**: CF-FM bats may preserve an echo-reference band / Doppler fovea (possibly by convergence of resting CF2 and active beam scanning) despite high overlap of emitted acoustic spectra. The relevant performance is high-frequency clutter suppression and prey-glint discrimination, **not maximizing between-bat CF2 distance**. 2026 clutter-jamming result provides a functional test of window use, not experimental link from the 101 mixing-history frequency shifts.

H3 **Social information reuse**: Following a partner or scanning its route can increase physical path alignment and potentially access useful information when perceived interference is manageable; in this regime a negative association of interbat spatial distance with information intake need not imply spatial competition or niche segregation.

H4 **Individual latent sensing–action policy**: After accounting for changing role, geometry, group density and partner noise, some bats may consistently emphasize frequency adjustments, beam scanning or path steering. This **cross-context individual identity of response mode is entirely untested** by the cited data, and is the mechanism-level bridge to `batter` personal 3D niche signatures.

## Research position
The high-value question is NOT the already known fact that sonar jamming exists or that FM bats can alter call frequency, nor a universal ecological law "multimodal compensation".

> **When multiple bats share the same three-dimensional task space, does individual specialization persist through independent differentiation of *information acquisition/control* rather than through exclusion from physical positions—and when does collaboration instead favor convergence of signals/paths?**

Empirical proof needs **simultaneous 3D kinematics, individual-emitter resolved sonar, stable biological IDs, manipulated neighbor/jamming load and actual task performance**. None of the independent studies above alone meets the whole chain.
