## Experimental leverage fixed from the peer-reviewed source

The source experiment provides a genuine learning boundary:

- **14** *Rhinolophus ferrumequinum nippon*;
- two acoustic obstacle conditions;
- **7 bats per condition**;
- bats were naive to the obstacle layout before their first flight;
- each bat performed **12 repeated flights**;
- obstacle layout was the same within a condition;
- the experimenter returned the bat to a fixed starting position after every flight;
- while carrying the bat to the start, the experimenter covered it to prevent collection of spatial information;
- flight path and echolocation behaviour were recorded.

Thus flight 1 is source-defined unfamiliar-space behaviour and later flights are repeated experience with the same task.

## Biological question

The Teshima 2026 configuration-transfer programme now indicates that individual identity can persist across obstacle configurations in:

- movement-performance features;
- steering/efficiency features;
- echolocation timing.

The unresolved causal question is:

> **Is this portable individual sensorimotor policy already present at the first naive encounter, or does repeated experience strengthen / reorganize it?**

This dataset directly addresses that question.

## Core discriminating predictions

### Stable predisposition / morphology dominated

Individual sensorimotor identity should already be strong on the earliest flights and remain approximately stable with experience.

### Experience-dependent policy formation

Held-out individual identity should strengthen across the 12 repeated flights.

### Rapid symmetry breaking

Weak or unstable identity on the very first flight, followed by a rapid increase over the first few repetitions and a plateau, rather than a long monotonic ramp.

### Shared learning only

All bats may change mean speed / route / pulse behaviour with experience while individual discriminability remains unchanged.

This separates **population learning** from **formation of individual specialization**.

## Important inherited source results

The source paper already reports population-level changes across repeated flights, including changes in flight path/speed and echolocation behaviour.

Those source-published learning effects are inherited background and are not counted as new support.

The new endpoint is **individual-specific predictive information through learning**.

## Claim ceiling

A positive formation result may support:

> repeated spatial experience changes the strength or organization of an individual's sensorimotor policy.

It cannot by itself establish:
- reinforcement learning as a unique algorithm;
- genetic versus developmental origin;
- fitness benefit;
- generality outside the tested species/task.
