# Behavioral Ecology title, lay summary and abstract compliance v3

**Working title (83 characters):** Persistent individuality without consistent additional vertical segregation in bats

## Lay Summary (65 words; journal ceiling 75)

Bats can retain individual flight-height patterns without consistently separating into different height layers. Across published experiments, bats remained recognizable by their behavior after changes to sensory and navigation conditions. Wild tracking likewise showed individually predictive height use, yet two tropical species showed no consistent support for added vertical segregation after accounting for terrain. Horizontal differences, prey use and the mechanisms maintaining personal patterns remain unresolved.

## Abstract (244 words; journal ceiling 250)

Individual differences may persist without consistently supporting additional vertical segregation. We integrated provenance-controlled reanalyses of public experiments and three-dimensional tracking in bats, separating behavioral identity under intervention, task-specific expression, development, and spatial partitioning. Four controlled programmes manipulating sensory masking, auditory processing, or navigation retained correct-individual behavioral correspondence, although biological sample sizes were three to six. A coarse movement representation transferred across tasks and into an independent bat system, whereas a fixed detailed trajectory representation did not pass transfer testing. Two randomized developmental experiments did not support simple effects on total multivariate individual differentiation, and a frozen primary juvenile formation rule failed despite predictive later personal history. In six original wild tracking panels, individual identity predicted vertical space use after standardized coarse horizontal weighting; five retained full vertical-distribution shape identity following session-median altitude centering. In four post-outcome terrain-relative panels from two tropical species, individual fidelity remained significant in all four under within-endpoint Holm adjustment, but three under a broader twelve-test adjustment; none supported positive added segregation. Separate post-outcome tests retained individuality after matching 500-m place and broad speed-turning categories in all four eligible panels. One co-use panel was nominally positive under a coarser encounter definition, but not after Holm adjustment. A frozen laboratory-style carrier-to-wild bridge also failed its overall criterion. Thus persistent individual information, detailed movement geometry and added vertical segregation are different properties. Horizontal or resource partitioning remains possible; neither a shared laboratory-to-field causal carrier nor the proximate mechanism maintaining wild strategies is established.

## Scientific and publication scope

The journal-length edits summarize the source-frozen JAE/BE comparative reanalyses. This is not an independent animal-data result. Prior Carlson et al. 2021 work already demonstrated individually differentiated overlapping environmental niches; the distinct evidence here is 3D bat vertical probability shape with common-cell/conditional movement controls and terrain/co-use separation nulls. The lag/state/terrain/co-use tests are post-outcome diagnostics, and the frozen scalar laboratory-to-field bridge failed its overall support gate.
