# Behavioral Ecology title, lay summary and abstract compliance v3

**Working title (69 characters):** Persistent individuality need not entail spatial partitioning in bats

## Lay Summary (73 words; journal ceiling 75)

Bats can behave differently from one another without keeping to separate patches of airspace. Across published experiments, individuals remained recognizable by their behavior after changes to sensory and navigation conditions. In wild tracking data, bats repeatedly used flight heights in individually distinctive ways, yet two tropical species showed little consistent evidence of extra vertical separation. Persistent personal patterns and exclusive spatial niches are not the same thing; what carries those patterns remains uncertain.

## Abstract (233 words; journal ceiling 250)

Persistent differences among individuals may survive environmental change without requiring ecological segregation. We integrated provenance-controlled reanalyses of public experiments and three-dimensional tracking in bats, separating behavioral identity under intervention, task-specific expression, development, and spatial partitioning. Four controlled programmes manipulating sensory masking, auditory processing, or navigation retained correct-individual behavioral correspondence, although biological sample sizes were three to six. A coarse movement representation transferred across tasks and into an independent bat system, whereas a fixed detailed trajectory representation did not pass transfer testing. Two randomized developmental experiments did not support simple effects on total multivariate individual differentiation, and a frozen primary juvenile formation rule failed despite predictive later personal history. In six original wild tracking panels, individual identity predicted vertical space use after standardized coarse horizontal weighting; five retained full vertical-distribution shape identity following session-median altitude centering. In four post-outcome terrain-relative panels from two tropical species, individual fidelity persisted throughout, yet no panel supported positive added vertical segregation. Separate post-outcome tests retained individuality after matching 500-m place and broad speed-turning categories in all four eligible panels. Additional separation during contemporaneous co-use was supported in only one panel under a coarser encounter definition. A frozen laboratory-style carrier-to-wild bridge also failed its overall criterion. Thus maintaining individually predictive behavioral organization, reproducing fine-scale movement geometry, and dividing three-dimensional space are different empirical properties. Neither a shared laboratory-to-field causal carrier nor the proximate mechanism maintaining wild strategies is established.

## Scientific and publication scope

The journal-length edits summarize the source-frozen JAE/BE comparative reanalyses. This is not an independent animal-data result. Prior Carlson et al. 2021 work already demonstrated individually differentiated overlapping environmental niches; the distinct evidence here is 3D bat vertical probability shape with common-cell/conditional movement controls and terrain/co-use separation nulls. The lag/state/terrain/co-use tests are post-outcome diagnostics, and the frozen scalar laboratory-to-field bridge failed its overall support gate. These are the boundaries to preserve in title/abstract revision.
