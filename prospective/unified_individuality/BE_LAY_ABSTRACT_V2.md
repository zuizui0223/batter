# Behavioral Ecology title, lay summary and abstract compliance v3

**Working title (80 characters):** Persistent behavioral individuality and the limits of 3D niche inference in bats

## Lay Summary (65 words; journal ceiling 75)

A bat's flight-height pattern can be recognizable without revealing whether bats divide up airspace. Across published experiments, individual behavior remained identifiable after changes to sensory and navigation conditions. Wild tracking likewise showed predictable session-centered relative-height use. Yet smoothed probability overlap and time-matched, individually centered height differences did not directly measure physical separation. Why these personal signatures persist—and whether they affect food use or competition—remains unresolved.

## Abstract (247 words; journal ceiling 250)

Individual differences may persist without consistently supporting additional vertical segregation. We integrated provenance-controlled reanalyses of public experiments and three-dimensional tracking in bats, separating behavioral identity under intervention, task-specific expression, development, and spatial partitioning. Four controlled programmes manipulating sensory masking, auditory processing, or navigation retained correct-individual behavioral correspondence, although biological sample sizes were three to six. A coarse movement representation transferred across tasks and into an independent bat system, whereas a fixed detailed trajectory representation did not pass transfer testing. Two randomized developmental experiments did not support simple effects on total multivariate individual differentiation, and a frozen primary juvenile formation rule failed despite predictive later personal history. In six original wild tracking panels, individual identity predicted vertical space use after standardized coarse horizontal weighting; five retained full vertical-distribution shape identity following session-median altitude centering. In four post-outcome panels from two tropical species, terrain subtraction altered apparent segregation, while individual fidelity survived within-endpoint Holm adjustment in four and broader twelve-test adjustment in three; none supported positive added segregation. A separate 500-m place-and-movement test retained individuality across four panels; source artifacts verify partial bat-ID overlap, without a paired V–I test. One coarse-window co-use panel showed nominal excess in session-centered height-deviation differences, but not after Holm adjustment. A frozen laboratory-style carrier-to-wild bridge also failed its overall criterion. Thus behavioral identity and estimated vertical segregation are distinct but algebraically coupled measures. Horizontal or resource partitioning remains possible; neither a shared laboratory-to-field causal carrier nor the proximate mechanism maintaining wild strategies is established.

## Scientific and publication scope

The journal-length edits summarize the source-frozen JAE/BE comparative reanalyses. This is not an independent animal-data result. Prior Carlson et al. 2021 work already demonstrated individually differentiated overlapping environmental niches; the distinct evidence here is 3D bat vertical probability shape with common-cell/conditional movement controls and terrain/co-use separation nulls. The lag/state/terrain/co-use tests are post-outcome diagnostics, and the frozen scalar laboratory-to-field bridge failed its overall support gate. These are the boundaries to preserve in title/abstract revision.
