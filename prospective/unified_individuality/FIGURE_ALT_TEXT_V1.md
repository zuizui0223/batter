# Accessible alternative text for unified manuscript Figures 1–6 (draft)

**Status:** scientific accessibility drafts for Behavioral Ecology; coauthor review required before submission. Descriptions summarize visually encoded evidence; they do not replace the complete figure legends.

## Figure 1 — Conceptual separation of individuality layers

Four boxes represent distinct questions: how individual behavior develops, whether correct-individual identity survives sensory or navigational intervention, whether a coarse behavioral representation transfers across tasks, and whether wild 3D movement results in exclusive spatial partitioning. Dashed links between boxes are conceptual. A separate strip records two passes, one failure and one structural stop in a frozen laboratory-style to wild-field carrier test, insufficient to meet its required three-of-four gate. The graphic does not claim a single mechanism connecting the experiments and field tracking.

## Figure 2 — History and developmental individualization

A dot plot shows fourteen juvenile individual experience slopes, eight positive and six negative; the predeclared primary required at least ten positives and therefore failed, although the mean permutation statistic had p=0.0102. A neighboring panel records a positive late personal-history secondary in twelve of fourteen juveniles. Separate boxed developmental contrasts show enrichment D=+0.735 with p=0.168 and auditory-feedback D=−1.425 with p=0.717. These differ in representation and are not compared on a common effect scale or described as equivalent treatments.

## Figure 3 — Individual correspondence under acute manipulations

Four horizontal rows display the within-source fraction of biological individuals with positive identity correspondence. External sensory masking in Pipistrellus is five of six; graded noise in Myotis, three of three; reversible auditory-midbrain manipulation in Eptesicus, four of four; and a navigation-context manipulation in Pipistrellus, four of four. Each row includes the biological sample size and source-specific exact assignment p-value; marker locations represent fractions of individuals, not a common behavioral effect size.

## Figure 4 — Transfer of a representation and limits of laboratory-to-field interpretation

Three cards show source-specific identity tests. The coarse I/M movement representation is supported across Rhinolophus obstacle contexts and transfers to independent Carollia flight records; a fixed detailed-geometry representation does not pass its Carollia transfer test. A separate four-cell diagram marks pass, pass, fail and structural stop for the scalar laboratory-style to wild field test, resulting in an overall fail at the predeclared three-of-four requirement.

## Figure 5 — Centered-shape fidelity and profiles of 103 panel-indexed individual records

Panel A is a six-row dot plot of calibrated original, held-out vertical-distribution identity. Five comparative panels have positive above-null values; the motivating Tadarida panel has a negative calibrated excess and does not pass. Panel B uses exactly the original 103 individual-by-panel probability vectors (20 Eidolon, 24 Hypsignathus, 33 Phyllostomus 2022, 16 Phyllostomus 2023 and 10 Phyllostomus 2016), each displayed as ten rectangular colored bins of session-centered height. Most mass occurs near the median, but the concentration and tail widths visibly vary across individuals. A shared scale indicates probability; panel B is descriptive only. The original source PNG/CSV/JSON are unchanged, while the 177-by-238-mm combined vector plate redraws the original probability cells without interpolation.

## Figure 6 — Centered terrain-relative individual identity and distinct estimated deviations

Four rows represent one Hypsignathus study and three Phyllostomus hastatus study-years, hence two biological species. Panel A shows original terrain-relative *centered vertical-distribution* identity supported in all four. Panel B shows additional segregation of centered probability shapes with gray ranges from source permutation nulls; none supports positive added segregation, while Hypsignathus has a nominal negative-direction raw two-sided p=0.036. Panel C shows the **excess absolute difference in each animal's separately session-median-centered terrain-relative height deviations**, with gray dyad-bootstrap compatibility ranges, **not physical between-bat altitude gap estimates or equivalence intervals**. Only Phyllostomus 2023 had a nominal positive source test (+3.57 m in this residual-deviation metric, raw p=0.023) under a 600-second all-space co-use rule; it does not survive either retrospective Holm adjustment. Co-use definitions differ: 60 seconds endpoint-excluded in Hypsignathus/2022, 600 seconds all-space in 2023 and 600 seconds endpoint-excluded in 2016. Figure C is not a direct physical avoidance test, and the field tests remain post-outcome diagnostics.

## Supplementary Figure S1 — Cohort-resolved centered-height probability-shape similarity

A source-derived one-dimensional overlap coefficient is shown separately for **12 original collection cohorts**, grouped visually in five tracking panels: six cohorts from *Eidolon helvum*, one from *Hypsignathus monstrosus*, two each from *Phyllostomus hastatus* 2022 and 2023, and one from 2016. Each row names the collection location/colony and year, the number of individual-by-panel probability-profile records and the number of within-cohort unordered dyads. Eleven cohorts have at least two source profiles; **Ghana, Accra 2009 has one profile, zero pairs and no estimable median**. Colored dots mark observed within-cohort dyad medians. Horizontal bars show the **empirical 10th–90th percentiles among reused dependent dyads where at least three pairs exist**, not confidence intervals. **Hollow dots** denote three two-profile *Eidolon* cohorts with **one dyad** each; these lack replication even within the cohort. The one-dimensional similarity scale extends from zero (no shared probability mass) to one (identical ten-bin session-centered shape). The five former dependent-dyad-pooled panel medians are retained in Supplementary Table S8 rather than plotted as independent species estimates. This display was redesigned **after inspecting the cohort-size imbalance**, with no new statistical test or alteration of the original 103 profile records or 684 pair overlaps. Some original *Phyllostomus* source animal identifiers repeat across years without validated global individual-identity crosswalk. The figure **does not** represent overlap in real 3D geographic space, synchronous co-presence, food consumption, competition, source-null significance or numerical uncertainty about a bat population.

## Supplementary Figure S2 — Recovered native-height horizontal versus joint 3D overlap

Four original study-year rows, each containing two session-pair comparisons: repeat sessions from the same bat and sessions from other bats. For each, a hollow marker shows the source-calculated horizontal 500-m-cell probability overlap O_XY and a filled marker shows the **alpha=0.5-smoothed** joint 500-m-cell by **independently session-median-centered relative-height** probability overlap O_XYZ. Each occupied cell has half-count regularization in ten bins, so a positive modeled overlap does not imply raw observations in the same physical XY×absolute-altitude bin. They are connected by a line indicating the within-eligible-session-pair reduction of intersection when adding height; the markers are source descriptive averages without confidence intervals. The eligible biological n per panel is 8 for *Hypsignathus monstrosus*, then 5, 6 and 9 for *Phyllostomus hastatus* 2022, 2023 and 2016. Different-bat horizontal-to-joint values are 0.322→0.240, 0.334→0.309, 0.202→0.190 and 0.208→0.181. The values come from original **native-height, session-centered and smoothed** source estimation, not terrain-corrected or observed physical 3D habitat co-use. In original subsequent terrain correction the source native added-segregation signal attenuated and no panel supported positive S_rel under source permutation; this figure has no new tests. The earlier one-dimensional profile-overlap Figure S1 uses distinct animals and source-specific horizontal weights and does not measure this gridded 3D joint overlap.
