## Lay Summary

Bats can retain individual flight-height patterns without consistently separating into different height layers. Across published experiments, bats remained recognizable by their behavior after changes to sensory and navigation conditions. Wild tracking likewise showed individually predictive height use, yet two tropical species showed no consistent support for added vertical segregation after accounting for terrain. Horizontal differences, prey use and the mechanisms maintaining personal patterns remain unresolved.

## Abstract

Individual differences may persist without consistently supporting additional vertical segregation. We integrated provenance-controlled reanalyses of public experiments and three-dimensional tracking in bats, separating behavioral identity under intervention, task-specific expression, development, and spatial partitioning. Four controlled programmes manipulating sensory masking, auditory processing, or navigation retained correct-individual behavioral correspondence, although biological sample sizes were three to six. A coarse movement representation transferred across tasks and into an independent bat system, whereas a fixed detailed trajectory representation did not pass transfer testing. Two randomized developmental experiments did not support simple effects on total multivariate individual differentiation, and a frozen primary juvenile formation rule failed despite predictive later personal history. In six original wild tracking panels, individual identity predicted vertical space use after standardized coarse horizontal weighting; five retained full vertical-distribution shape identity following session-median altitude centering. In four post-outcome panels from two tropical species, terrain subtraction altered apparent segregation, while individual fidelity survived within-endpoint Holm adjustment in four and broader twelve-test adjustment in three; none supported positive added segregation. Separate post-outcome tests retained individuality after matching 500-m place and broad speed-turning categories in all four eligible panels. One coarse-window co-use panel showed nominal excess in session-centered height-deviation differences, but not after Holm adjustment. A frozen laboratory-style carrier-to-wild bridge also failed its overall criterion. Thus persistent individual information, detailed movement geometry and added vertical segregation are different properties. Horizontal or resource partitioning remains possible; neither a shared laboratory-to-field causal carrier nor the proximate mechanism maintaining wild strategies is established.

## Introduction

Populations can be composed of individuals that use resources or space in non-exchangeable ways even when their aggregate niche appears broad (Bolnick et al. 2003; Araújo et al. 2011). Such differences are common in movement studies, but the observation that identity predicts a future behavioral measurement is not by itself an explanation of how the difference arose, why it persists, or whether it produces ecological separation. Importantly, **stable individual environmental niches need not occupy disjoint parts of environmental space**: white-stork niches can be both repeatably different and strongly nested/overlapping (Carlson et al. 2021). Within bats, *Ia io* spatial individual specialization and home-range overlap vary together across seasons (Wang et al. 2023). More directly, the *P. hastatus* La Gruta tracking archives from 2016 and 2022 were already studied for social structure and **horizontal** foraging-area use, with an approximately 0.20 foraging-area Bhattacharyya overlap coefficient and no clear difference between within- and between-social-group overlap (Calderón-Capote et al. 2025). That geographical overlap metric is **not** the one-dimensional residual-height profile coefficient estimated below. Thus neither individuality within shared space nor the broad distinction between individual specialization and exclusive niche partitioning is, by itself, a novel discovery. These distinctions matter especially in three-dimensional movement systems: different foraging depths, flight heights and spatial histories are established phenomena, but the stability of a full individual vertical probability distribution cannot be inferred from horizontal occupancy alone (Ratcliffe et al. 2013; McIntyre et al. 2017; Gámez and Harris 2022; Xing et al. 2023).

Four logically different questions are often gathered under the label of behavioral individuality (Fig. 1; White et al. 2020; de Bivort 2025). **Formation** asks how history, development and predispositions produce between-individual differences. **Maintenance** asks whether identifiable individual organization persists after a change in current conditions. **Expression** asks how that organization is realized as observable movements, vocalizations or task solutions. **Ecological consequence** asks whether the resulting differences generate resource or spatial partitioning. A context manipulation can change mean behavioral expression while preserving the ordering of individuals; individual-specific covariance structure also depends on context (Houslay et al. 2018; Mitchell and Houslay 2021). A developmental treatment can alter phenotype without yielding a supported change in overall among-individual dispersion (for related prior art, see Gallagher et al. 2026). A population may exhibit repeatable individual space-use probability distributions without individuals occupying mutually exclusive subspaces. None of these statements requires one shared latent policy measured identically in different species.

The relevant ecological question is therefore not simply whether bats possess repeatable personalities or fly at different heights. It is **when persistent individual-specific behavioral information predicts additional vertical differentiation beyond horizontal structure, and when it does not**. We use "individuality" operationally to mean predictive information associated with a biological individual under a declared within-study comparison. We reserve "additional partitioning" for spatial separation exceeding an explicit within-study exchangeability expectation, rather than treating any statistical difference between individuals as segregation.

Public bat datasets make it possible to examine these questions at several inferential levels. Experiments have altered current sensory masking, graded acoustic interference, central auditory processing and navigation conditions while tracking identified bats. Other experiments have manipulated early environment or auditory feedback and measured later phenotype. Task-based recordings test whether a low-dimensional description of individual movement transfers better than detailed trajectory geometry. Finally, repeated wild tracking allows the individual-specific distribution of height to be evaluated after coarse horizontal-occupancy standardization, altitude centering, local terrain adjustment and contemporary co-use comparison.

The evidence is deliberately heterogeneous. A within-experiment assignment permutation does not have the same estimand or sampling precision as a between-session vertical-distribution prediction, and separate species cannot establish an uninterrupted causal chain from developmental state to wild niche use. We therefore do not pool incompatible numerical scores or claim an experiment that directly connects a laboratory behavioral carrier to individual field niches. Instead, we organize the source-level tests around three central propositions: (1) current perturbations need not erase identity-bearing behavioral organization; (2) persistent organization need not retain the same detailed expression across tasks; and (3) persistent individual vertical-space use need not entail positive additional vertical segregation. Developmental and early-history tests delimit what is known about formation, while a separately frozen field bridge assesses whether laboratory-style carrier evidence generalizes to wild individuality.

Our contribution is an integrated, evidence-ranked account of these propositions. The novelty is **not** the general existence of animal individuality, persistent overlapping environmental niches (Carlson et al. 2021), bat spatial specialization despite seasonal overlap (Wang et al. 2023), context-dependent behavior (Mathejczyk et al. 2026), or mean phenotype shifts without changes in scalar individuality. Rather, it is the specific comparison of (i) full-pipeline-calibrated **individual vertical-distribution shape** under common horizontal weights, (ii) persistence after 500-m place × coarse speed–turning matching, (iii) added terrain-relative, centered-shape 3D segregation and matched co-use **relative-height-deviation** phase-shift tests, and (iv) distinct experimental identity/representation results **without assuming a validated laboratory–field carrier**.

## Materials and methods

### Evidence sources, provenance and units of replication

We assembled publicly archived datasets spanning controlled sensory and navigation experiments, randomized developmental manipulations, task-transfer recordings, and repeated wild three-dimensional tracking. This study is a comparative secondary reanalysis, not a newly conducted experiment. Within each source, analysis endpoints, support rules and null models were recorded before the corresponding numerical results were inspected. Public-source discovery and the across-system synthesis developed iteratively, however, and the overall study was not prospectively preregistered as a multisystem meta-analysis. Field terrain-relative geometry, contemporaneous co-use and maintenance stress tests were undertaken after earlier tracking results had been observed, and are explicitly designated **post-outcome** throughout. The archived source contracts, results and computational workflows remain authoritative; the synthesis did not reopen them.

In each programme the biological individual, rather than GPS fix, flight, trial or call, is the biological replication unit. Within-study repeated measurements estimate predictive distributions or correspondence statistics. Permutations and randomizations follow the original source-specific eligible exchangeability sets. Several experimental datasets comprise only three to five biological individuals; a small exact assignment p-value describes consistency of a mapping within that experiment, not population prevalence.

### Three distinct estimands

We distinguish (i) **identity correspondence** after context changes, (ii) **amount of individualization** under developmental treatments, and (iii) **ecological differentiation and partitioning** in wild three-dimensional space.

For the first, source-specific contrasts compare a correct identity mapping to alternative individual mappings after the shared shift in condition has been accounted for. Angles, flight time, vocal coordinates and turning positions are not placed on one common effect-size scale. For developmental experiments, the estimand is the randomized-treatment contrast in residual multivariate among-individual dispersion after common treatment-group shifts are removed. Lack of a significant treatment contrast does not constitute proof of equivalence. For wild data, the target is whether an individual's held-out vertical probability distribution is better predicted by its own other sessions than by comparable conspecific histories under standardized horizontal support, and separately whether different individuals show added terrain-relative separation or contemporaneous avoidance.

An individual wild use distribution can be factored exactly as \(p_i(h,z\mid c)=p_i(h\mid c)\,p_i(z\mid h,c)\), where \(h\) is horizontal place, \(z\) is height and \(c\) denotes observed context or session. This makes **horizontal site allocation** and **vertical use conditional on place** different statistical objects. The source-defined horizontal overlap \(O_{XY}\), joint overlap \(O_{XYZ}\) and shared-cell conditional vertical-overlap predictor \(O_{Z\mid XY}\) therefore answer distinct questions, and pairwise averages of those overlaps are not interchangeable. The original terrain-relative \(S_{rel}\) asks the still narrower question of whether adding height yields excess *between*-animal versus *within*-animal overlap loss beyond its calibrated source null. Individually predictive \(p_i(z\mid h,c)\) need not imply positive \(S_{rel}\), and neither object identifies behavior within an unobserved feeding or commuting task. This factorization is a descriptive identity, **not** a fitted universal bat-policy model or causal decomposition of actual prey use.

### Ontogeny and randomized developmental manipulations

The first-flight tracking data of Harten et al. (2020) for juvenile *Rousettus aegyptiacus* tested a predeclared individual-level monotonic increase in own-history predictive advantage over eligible flight days. The fixed primary required both a positive permutation-supported programme tendency and positive slopes in at least 10 of 14 juveniles. A separately specified late-history secondary compared recent own history with experience-matched conspecific history; an early-seed-versus-recent-history comparison was added only as a post-primary diagnostic.

Two randomized developmental studies were analysed under their frozen tests. For the randomized environmental enrichment study of Rachum et al. (2025), 29 complete bats (14 enriched, 15 impoverished) contributed three baseline-scaled personality dimensions, and origin-stratified assignments evaluated the difference in residual dispersion after subtracting each group's shared behavioral change vector. In the randomized auditory-feedback experiment of Elie et al. (2024), ten bats (five hearing, five deafened) were compared on the residual multivariate dispersion of their adult whole-repertoire vocal features using the exact 120 eligible assignments. Feature-level reallocations in either study were descriptive, not separately confirmatory. A separate 18-bat early-environment history-carrier contrast from the Rachum et al. (2025) source was evaluated under its original randomized endpoint.

### Acute sensory, neural and navigation manipulations

Four independent source-specific experimental contrasts were evaluated: *Pipistrellus kuhlii* external sensory masking and a second foam-controlled contrast (Taub and Yovel 2020); *Myotis daubentonii* flight-performance correspondence across five noise levels (Foskolos et al. 2022); *Eptesicus fuscus* vocal correspondence during reversible auditory-midbrain perturbation (Diebold et al. 2024); and *P. kuhlii* bilateral turning-position organization across three manipulated navigation contexts (Aharon et al. 2017). The identity-mapping nulls, support gates and biological n are source-specific. They estimate resistance of *measured individual correspondence* to an acute intervention, not the anatomical storage location of a shared neural controller.

### Task portability and representation

For *Rhinolophus nippon*, a pre-specified two-coordinate movement representation—FlightIntensity and ManeuveringExtent (I/M)—was tested across obstacle contexts using the Teshima et al. (2026) public flight-policy source. The fixed representation was then transferred to an independent *Carollia perspicillata* corridor system (Eveland et al. 2026). A fixed detailed, scale-free trajectory-geometry representation provided the comparison. Transfer of predictive organization is not interpreted as evidence that the same latent parameter or policy exists in the two species.

### Repeated wild three-dimensional tracking

The outcome-blind wild-data screen considered 23 Movebank parent bat datasets and admitted six tracking panels from four taxa, building on established work on individually differentiated space use and seasonal movement (Kerches-Rogeri et al. 2020; Wang et al. 2023). The sources comprised *Tadarida teniotis* (O'Mara et al. 2021), *Eidolon helvum* (O'Mara et al. 2019), *Hypsignathus monstrosus* (Schloesing et al. 2023), and three *Phyllostomus hastatus* panels. The 2016 *P. hastatus* source corresponded to O'Mara and Dechmann (2023; Movebank DOI 10.5441/001/1.282), while the 2021–2022 and 2023 longitudinal sources corresponded to Calderón-Capote et al. (2024; Movebank DOIs 10.5441/001/1.321 and 10.5441/001/1.322, respectively). These are separate source-study panels, not six independent species or a phylogenetically balanced sample. Furthermore, the 2016 and 2022 La Gruta source datasets had already been analysed for horizontal female foraging-area and social-group overlap by Calderón-Capote et al. (2025); the present independent contribution is not discovery of horizontal foraging-area overlap in those cohorts. Held-out same-individual and other-individual vertical predictors were compared under identical weights over mutually supported 5-km horizontal cells. Whole-session individual-label permutations calibrated each complete prediction pipeline. Each retained session was also median-centered before vertical binning to remove additive constant height offsets; this does not remove all state-dependent or variance-related device error.

The original archived display contains **103 individual-by-panel profiles** (20, 24, 33, 16 and 10 source-specific individuals within the five positive comparative panels), not a verified sample of 103 globally distinct living bats. **Two source identifiers** recur in both the 2016 and 2022 *P. hastatus* files from La Gruta, leaving 101 distinct literal identifier strings across the 103 profile rows. The original underlying studies/identification registry have not yet established whether these refer to the same bats resampled six years apart or to cross-dataset identifier reuse. Accordingly, source biological n is reported **within each panel**, and no cross-year repeated-individual persistence or extra independent animal replication is inferred from these labels. The descriptive overlap calculation forms pairs only within the same source panel and exact source cohort, so it contains no cross-year duplicate-label comparisons.

An additional, **post-outcome descriptive-only** check quantified the overlap coefficient of the already archived individual 10-bin, session-centered self-prediction probability profiles. We included all distinct unordered pairs of bats **within the exact same original study panel and site/colony/year cohort**, with O_ij defined as the sum across bins of the smaller of the two estimated probabilities. Original source profiles and their source-derived horizontal weights were not refitted or changed. Median and 10th–90th dyad percentiles describe the distribution of nonindependent pairs, **not a confidence interval or a hypothesis test**. This 1D centered-height shape coefficient cannot measure overlap of 3D flight habitat or co-presence (Supplementary Methods S13 and Table S8; Fig. S1).

A later, explicitly **post-outcome**, ecological geometry and maintenance family evaluated four structurally eligible panels from *H. monstrosus* and *P. hastatus*. For each eligible GPS event, the original workflow bilinearly interpolated local terrain elevation from the DEM and subtracted it from the source GPS height **before** constructing 500-m-horizontal-cell joint distributions. This creates a terrain-relative height **proxy**, not a verified above-ground-level or above-canopy measurement: the original GPS height field was ellipsoid-referenced for *H. monstrosus* and *P. hastatus* 2023, but mean-sea-level-referenced for *P. hastatus* 2022 and 2016. The family separately tested the repeatability of session-centered terrain-relative vertical configuration (V_rel), added segregation of those **centered vertical probability shapes** (S_rel), and excess **session-centered height-deviation dissimilarity during matched co-use events** (I). An inherited individual-label null calibrated S_rel, while within-session-by-cell height-deviation phase shifts and dyad resampling were used for co-use; the latter yielded compatibility intervals for the **centered-deviation endpoint**, not physical-gap equivalence tests. Persistence was further checked across nonadjacent days, common fine spatial and kinematic contexts, and same-night conspecific comparison. A retrospective multiplicity sensitivity applied Holm's procedure first to four source p-values **within each** terrain-fidelity, two-sided added-segregation and upper-tail co-use endpoint family, then to the **twelve displayed p-values jointly** as an expanded, illustrative family. Neither grouping was specified prospectively, captures all programme tests, alters original permutation results or establishes programme-wide familywise error control.

The original 500-m joint-grid estimator used ten session-centered height bins and added **α = 0.5 pseudocounts to every bin within each occupied horizontal cell**. Original pair eligibility required at least **50 fixes per session summed across all pairwise-shared horizontal cells**, not 50 fixes in each cell. Thus O_XYZ and O_Z|XY are intersections of **smoothed, session-median-centered estimated probability distributions**, not observed fractions of physically shared 3D airspace. Session centering also removes each session's absolute vertical level; two physically separated height ranges can have identical centered shapes. Original permutation nulls retained this fixed estimator (Supplementary Methods S15).

Previously frozen tests in *Nyctalus noctula* (Reusch et al. 2023), *Hipposideros armiger/pratti* (Si et al. 2025), *Myotis vivesi* (Hurme et al. 2019) and *Pteropus poliocephalus* (Boardman et al. 2021) remain external **boundary** programmes. Their different source gates preclude interpreting the four as a bat-wide prevalence sample. The first frozen *Nyctalus* result remains authoritative even though a later eligibility revision was more favorable.

### Predeclared laboratory-style carrier-to-field bridge

The BE programme separately froze a scalar FlightIntensity carrier bridge to wild vertical individuality. The overall criterion required at least three of four panels to pass. The frozen result was two passes, one failure and one structural stop. Later field H/V decompositions are exploratory and cannot reclassify the failed primary. Crucially, the bridge does not provide repeated laboratory and field measurements of the same individuals. It is an operational representation-transfer test, not a direct test of a shared ontogenetic or neural mechanism.

### Evidence hierarchy

We use the words **supported**, **unsupported/failed**, **unadjudicated structural stop**, and **descriptive/post-outcome diagnostic** according to the two original ledgers. A numerically small p-value cannot override a failed prespecified sign or sample-size gate; a nonsignificant test cannot establish exact absence of an effect. Across heterogeneous sources we compare designs and directional inferential statuses, not pooled effect sizes, pooled p-values, or an implied common latent trait. Detailed technical methods, source provenance, source-specific null architectures and the four-panel co-use design are retained in the original frozen packages and the [combined Supporting Information draft](SUPPORTING_INFORMATION_DRAFT_V1.md).

## Results

### Individual history is informative, but its formation trajectory is not a common monotonic law

The first-flight analysis (Fig. 2) showed a positive mean relation between experience and own-history advantage (B = +0.16733; permutation p = 0.0102). However, only 8 of 14 juveniles had positive individual slopes, below the frozen requirement of 10 of 14. The **primary formation hypothesis failed**, despite its positive mean statistic. The predeclared late-history secondary was supported (L = +112.5554; p = 0.0001; 12/14 positive): own recent movement history predicted later spatial use better than experience-matched conspecific history. An exploratory comparison favored recent over earliest-two-day history (Q = +144.0791; p = 0.0001), but this post-primary result does not convert the original formation gate to a pass.

In the independent randomized early-environment history-carrier test, enrichment did not detectably strengthen later personal-history dependence relative to impoverishment (T = +0.7081; two-sided p = 0.3244). Thus the public evidence establishes informative later personal histories without identifying a general monotonic acquisition rule or a simple causal effect of broad environmental enrichment on the history carrier.

### Developmental treatment does not map simply onto total individual differentiation

The randomized enrichment programme included 29 bats. Its contrast in residual three-trait change-vector dispersion was D = +0.734699, with enriched greater than impoverished in the observed direction, but the origin-stratified randomization test did not support an increase (p = 0.167785). All three feature contributions were positive descriptively, so the nonsupport was not explained by cancellation among opposite trait directions. A separate descriptive predictor using individual baseline plus a shared treatment-associated shift performed better than baseline alone or treatment-group state alone.

The randomized auditory-feedback programme included ten bats and produced D = -1.425497 in adult whole-repertoire dispersion, with exact two-sided p = 0.716667. The original experiment established an effect of auditory feedback on learned vocal phenotype, but the reanalysis did not support an effect on the **total amount** of individualization. Across 28 acoustic dimensions, 15 contributions were positive and 13 negative (descriptive cancellation ratio 0.840173). These opposing contributions suggest possible reallocation of individual information across measured features, but no feature-specific reallocation hypothesis was confirmatorily tested here. Neither nonsignificant total-dispersion test establishes equality between developmental treatments.

### Same-individual correspondence survived multiple acute current-context interventions

In the *P. kuhlii* external-masker programme, individual movement/approach bias remained detectable from baseline to masker (K = +4.941 degrees; five of six bats positive; exact p = 0.04028). The separate foam no-masker to foam-plus-masker contrast was also supported (K = +6.779 degrees; five of five positive; p = 0.025).

In *M. daubentonii*, individual flight-time organization remained identifiable over five graded acoustic conditions (A = +0.377542; all three individuals positive; exact identity mapping rank 1 of 1,296; p = 0.00077160). In *E. fuscus*, correct pre/post identity matching of multivariate vocal organization under reversible auditory-midbrain intervention ranked first among 24 assignments (K = +1.004452; four of four positive; p = 0.041667). In the *P. kuhlii* navigation manipulation, all four bats showed positive turning-location identity advantages, with the correct mapping ranked second of 576 allowed assignments across the three contexts (K = +3.695264; p = 0.00347222).

The common inferential result is *retained identity correspondence under changed current conditions* (Fig. 3). Because each programme measured a different behavior with a different null and only three to six biological individuals, these outcomes do not estimate a pooled physiological invariance or species-wide prevalence.

### Coarse personal organization transferred where detailed realized geometry did not

The fixed coarse I/M organization in *R. nippon* retained cross-task individual information (K = +0.55428; five of five positive; p = 0.0001). Applied without refitting the representation to an independent *C. perspicillata* system, the coarse I/M representation remained supported (K = +0.34758; p = 0.0007). By contrast, the fixed detailed scale-free Rhino trajectory-geometry representation did not pass its *Carollia* test (K = -0.0350; p = 0.2144).

These source-level PASS/FAIL outcomes (Fig. 4) support a narrower distinction between portable measured organization and detailed trajectory realization; they are not a formal between-representation K-effect difference test. It does not demonstrate one common neural policy, and nonsupport of the detailed representation does not mean that *Carollia* lacks individually repeatable fine-scale movement in every possible representation.

### Wild individuality persisted after coarse horizontal standardization and absolute-altitude centering

The six admitted wild tracking panels all had identity-matched vertical profiles outperforming their full-pipeline whole-session permutation expectation when self and other profiles were integrated under the same coarse 5-km horizontal occupancy weights. This comparison limits horizontal-occupancy confounding among tested cells; it does not eliminate within-cell spatial fidelity.

More importantly, session-median centering retained calibrated **vertical-distribution shape** identity in all five non-*Tadarida* comparative panels (Fig. 5): *E. helvum* calibrated excess +0.443 nats/fix (p = 0.0002), *H. monstrosus* +0.177 (p = 0.0002), and *P. hastatus* 2022 +0.111 (p = 0.0002), 2023 +0.118 (p = 0.0002), and 2016 +0.574 (p = 0.0076). The motivating *T. teniotis* panel failed the corresponding shape test (calibrated excess -0.022; p = 0.5121), even though absolute vertical-location identity was repeatable. Centering removes constant altitude zero-point shifts exactly but does not establish that every remaining difference is biological rather than state-dependent tag error.

A further **post-outcome descriptive-only** calculation quantified how similar the original 103 **individual-by-panel session-centered vertical self-predictor profile records** look on their shared ten-bin height axis. After restricting comparisons to the **same original site/colony/year cohort**, there were 684 nonindependent unordered individual pairs. Their median 1D relative-height probability overlap was 0.817 (*E. helvum*, 41 pairs), 0.801 (*H. monstrosus*, 276), 0.919 (*P. hastatus* 2022, 262), 0.899 (2023, 60) and 0.802 (2016, 45; Supplementary Table S8). **Figure S1 displays the original 12 source-cohort medians and sizes directly**, rather than portraying these dependent-pair-pooled values as species-level estimates. These medians describe similar **estimated normalized height-profile shapes**, despite inferentially detectable individual identity. They are not tests of competition or 3D niche overlap: individuals may have different self-derived horizontal cell supports and weights, and session centering/coarse bins/smoothing influence the values.

The descriptive values also depend on which source cohort contributes the individual pairs. In *E. helvum*, a Zambian collection cohort supplies 28/41 pairs (**68.3%**) and has median O = 0.728, while the overall pair-pooled median is 0.817; treating each pair-eligible source cohort's median equally gives 0.855. In *P. hastatus* 2023, one colony supplies 45/60 pairs (**75.0%**): the overall pair-pooled median is 0.899 versus 0.865 when the two colony medians are weighted equally. These alternative numbers are **post-outcome descriptive sensitivity summaries**, not species-level niche-overlap estimates, independence-adjusted confidence intervals or additional evidence against partitioning. Equal cohort weighting also overweights the *Eidolon* source cohorts containing only two profiles and a single pair. The exact source-cohort medians and cohort weights are shown in **Figure S1**, Supplementary Table S9 and Methods S14. The figure was **redesigned after the cohort-size imbalance was inspected**; no new biological test was performed.

Previously frozen external tests were heterogeneous: the first *N. noctula* primary failed (+0.05175; p = 0.1224), *H. armiger/pratti* failed (-0.04468; p = 0.8616), *M. vivesi* failed under a separate four-individual design (+0.00470; p = 0.4419), and *P. poliocephalus* passed under its separately frozen four-individual design (+0.16873; p = 0.0001). The *Pteropus* excess attenuated strongly after terrain correction (+0.03231; p = 0.0023). These tests define conditional scope; they do not form a representative four-species prevalence sample.

### Original horizontal and joint three-dimensional overlap reveal the underlying geometry

The original post-outcome 500-m geometry programme had already quantified source-defined **horizontal probability overlap** (O_XY) and **smoothed joint horizontal-by-height overlap** (O_XYZ) between eligible repeated source tracking sessions. These are original source-run, native-height, **session-median-centered** descriptive overlap statistics (Supplementary Table S10 and Fig. S2), not a new analysis of GPS fixes and **not yet corrected for terrain**. Only four original tropical panels met the source structural support rules (biological n = 8 *H. monstrosus*, 5 *P. hastatus* 2022, 6 in 2023, 9 in 2016). The focal *Tadarida* (n = 4) and *Eidolon* (n = 0) were structurally non-evaluable at 500 m. The between-individual **O_XY** values were 0.322, 0.334, 0.202 and 0.208, whereas the corresponding **O_XYZ** values were 0.240, 0.309, 0.190 and 0.181. Within-individual session-pair joint O_XYZ values were 0.523, 0.507, 0.508 and 0.425. The original calibrated self-versus-other **conditional** vertical-overlap predictor was separately supported in 4/4 eligible panels.

In each panel, mean **same-individual O_XY** was descriptively higher (0.619, 0.549, 0.547 and 0.511) than the corresponding other-individual horizontal overlap. No source-calibrated test here establishes either the presence or the absence of **horizontal** partitioning. For two normalized distributions on the same pairwise grid, O_XYZ cannot exceed O_XY by the probability-intersection inequality. An absolute decrease in overlap after adding height is therefore geometric, not on its own evidence of **individual-specific added vertical segregation**; the original within-versus-between R_3D comparison and its later terrain-relative S_rel permutation test address that narrower question.

The original native-height mean fraction of horizontal probability overlap lost on adding height, R_3D, differed across the four studies. Its **other-minus-self** descriptive contrast S_native was +0.103 in *H. monstrosus*, −0.017 in 2022, −0.004 in 2023 and +0.130 in 2016. After local terrain elevation subtraction, those contrasts instead became −0.0158, −0.0090, +0.0163 and +0.0289, **none supporting positive terrain-relative added segregation** under its later inherited source permutation null. The native S values and terrain-corrected S_rel values are different processing states, so the former cannot be substituted for the latter's inferential status. In a descriptive matched-source audit, V increased on terrain subtraction in the 2022/2023 *P. hastatus* panels but decreased in *H. monstrosus* and 2016 *P. hastatus*; the corresponding S changes also had both directions. The larger positive native S values declined substantially for *H. monstrosus* (+0.1029 to −0.0158) and 2016 *P. hastatus* (+0.1304 to +0.0289), while exact source horizontal overlap contrast H stayed unchanged. These **processing differences were not separately permutation-tested**, and cannot identify topography as a causal driver (Supplementary Table S13).

The joint-gridded **O_XYZ** values describe overlap of eligible, **smoothed session-pair probability distributions**, not observed sharing of three-dimensional grid cells, instantaneous co-presence, identical prey/resource use or lifetime 3D habitats. Because the fixed α = 0.5 prior makes every height bin's probability positive in any occupied horizontal cell, modeled O_XYZ can be positive even when two sessions have **zero directly observed shared XY×height bins**. The aggregate 50-fix common-support gate does not impose a minimum per cell, and the original panel summaries cannot determine empirical smoothing sensitivity without returning to source observations (Supplementary Methods S15). Nor can they be compared by simple subtraction with Figure S1's much larger (0.80–0.92) **one-dimensional centered profile-shape** coefficients: S1's 103 panel-indexed source records and pair-specific horizontal supports differ from the original native 500-m structurally eligible animals. The native 3D measurements clarify the nested horizontal-versus-vertical geometry; S_rel and synchronous co-use I remain the separate tests of **additional vertical segregation**.

### Terrain-relative fidelity did not require added vertical segregation

In four structurally eligible 500-m panels from *H. monstrosus* and *P. hastatus*, terrain-relative vertical configuration remained individually repeatable after local elevation subtraction: V_rel = +0.042 (p = 0.0001) for *H. monstrosus*, and +0.100 (p = 0.0001), +0.077 (p = 0.0001) and +0.079 (p = 0.0345) for *P. hastatus* 2022, 2023 and 2016. These are **post-outcome ecological diagnostics**, not an independent prospective discovery series.

Yet the terrain-relative added-segregation statistics were -0.0158, -0.0090, +0.0163 and +0.0289 for the same four panels, respectively. None supported positive added segregation under its permutation null. The *H. monstrosus* comparison had a **nominal negative-direction** two-sided p = 0.036, uncorrected across the four exploratory panels; the three *P. hastatus* tests were p = 0.364, 0.270 and 0.480. This does not establish a generalized negative-segregation effect.

A separate local co-use analysis compared **absolute differences between each bat's height deviation from its own session median** during x-y-time-matched visits, rather than direct altitude gaps between individuals. Explicitly, the coded endpoint was |(h_rel,a − median_session,a) − (h_rel,b − median_session,b)|: the two independently subtracted session medians prevent interpreting these metre-valued numbers as actual physical vertical separation. No positive excess *centered-deviation difference* was supported in *H. monstrosus*, *P. hastatus* 2022 or *P. hastatus* 2016; their positive 95% dyad-bootstrap upper compatibility endpoints were approximately 0.98 m, 1.92 m and 1.13 m **on that deviation scale only**. *P. hastatus* 2023 was the nominal exception (+3.57 m above its phase-shift null; p = 0.0231), under a 600-s **all-space** encounter rule and wide dyad compatibility (−4.63 to +11.73 m). The 2016 panel likewise used 600 s but endpoint exclusion; the first two used 60 s with endpoint exclusion. Phase shifts were within original individual × session × 500-m-cell groups while retaining the fixed matched events. These comparisons **do not test actual between-bat altitude avoidance** or provide equivalence bounds for real physical height gaps (Fig. 6; Supplementary Methods S7).

An exploratory Holm check **within each four-panel endpoint family** retained terrain-relative V_rel support in all four panels (largest adjusted p = 0.03453). The nominal negative-direction *H. monstrosus* S_rel (raw p = 0.0362, adjusted p = 0.1448) and positive *P. hastatus* 2023 co-use result (raw p = 0.0231, adjusted p = 0.0924) did not survive their respective four-panel adjustments. When the same **twelve displayed V_rel/S_rel/co-use p-values** were instead treated as one illustrative family, V_rel remained below 0.05 in only three panels (adjusted p = 0.0012 for *H. monstrosus*, *P. hastatus* 2022 and 2023; *P. hastatus* 2016 adjusted p = 0.2762); no S_rel or co-use test survived. These retrospectively chosen families are **sensitivity analyses**, not exhaustive or preregistered multiplicity control; contrasting significance counts does not establish that the true fidelity effect is larger than the true segregation effect or that competition is absent (Supplementary Tables S11–S12 and Methods S16).

Together, source-calibrated repeatability of individual **centered relative-height-use distributions** and tests for added segregation of those distributions had different outcomes in the same two-species family (Fig. 6). The separate co-use phase-shift test measures differences in *within-session deviations*, not physical separation, and cannot demonstrate either avoidance or its absence. The supported within-domain conclusion concerns distinct statistical properties of centered vertical use, **not** a demonstrated lack of exclusive physical altitude layers.

### Repeatability extended beyond adjacent sessions and broad movement contexts

Post-outcome restrictions to self-training sessions at least one day from the target preserved individuality in 4/4 structurally eligible panels; at least three days in 3/3; and at least seven days in 2/2. These are changing panel subsets with different eligible animals, **not** a longitudinal decay series. More decisively for the ecological interpretation, simultaneous matching on **500-m horizontal cells and broad speed × turning categories** retained centered vertical individuality in *H. monstrosus* (n = 19, calibrated excess +0.384, p = 0.0002), and *P. hastatus* 2022 (n = 23, +0.0868, p = 0.0002), 2023 (n = 11, +0.1015, p = 0.0006), and 2016 (n = 9, +0.4514, p = 0.0286). Thus repeated use of different ≥500-m patches or differing *broad* speed-turn composition does not exhaust the predictive individual information in these four eligible subsets.

Under a separate 2-km place × speed × turning test with contemporaneous shifted-night conspecifics, own history was more informative in 3/4 panels; the 2016 panel failed its frozen support threshold (p = 0.0544) and remains unresolved rather than a positive fourth case. These tests limit explanations confined to immediately adjacent sessions, coarse patch allocation or shared calendar-night response. But a 500-m cell can contain different trees, corridors, atmospheric niches and foraging tasks, and speed/turning are proxies rather than independently validated behavioral states. No conditional task-specific causal rule was identified.

### The frozen laboratory-style carrier did not establish a field mechanism

The previously frozen scalar FlightIntensity bridge required success in at least three of four wild panels. Its observed sequence was **PASS / PASS / FAIL / STRUCTURAL STOP**, yielding two of four and an overall **failed predeclared gate**. Positive post-outcome H/V components remain exploratory. Thus laboratory-style identity organization cannot currently be treated as a demonstrated explanation of the wild vertical shapes or their overlap. The wild distribution and segregation results stand independently of this unsuccessful cross-domain carrier test.

## Discussion

### What persists is individual information, not necessarily a fixed trajectory

The integrated evidence separates persistence of identity-bearing organization from invariance of the measured behavior. Across controlled acute sensory, auditory and navigational interventions, true individual correspondence survived after common context effects were accounted for. The relevant inference is not that bats behaved identically in different environments; it is that some relationship among individuals survived substantial changes in measured behavioral expression. The coarse-I/M versus detailed-geometry transfer contrast provides a related, though independent, representation-level observation: a compact description can remain predictive when a fixed, richly geometric one does not.

These experiments involve different species, behaviors and small biological cohorts; the pattern does not locate a common neural memory or imply that one latent organization is shared across every bat. Still, it motivates treating an animal's immediately observed trajectory as only one possible realization of a more persistent, empirically measurable individual difference. An individual can be predictable without executing one literal route repeatedly.

### Individual differentiation does not demonstrate added vertical segregation

The ecological payoff comes from the wild evidence. After horizontal standardization and session-median centering, five comparative panels retained individual differences in the **shape** of vertical-use distributions. In the narrower four-panel, two-species terrain-relative family, the measured personal centered-height configurations remained repeatable while positive additional segregation of those estimated distributions was unsupported; a separate, heterogeneous encounter test had one nominal positive result for **centered-deviation dissimilarity**, not a demonstrated physical separation between bats. The native horizontal-overlap comparisons were already descriptively lower between than within individuals; these data cannot rule out horizontal or resource-space differences. Our limited non-support statement concerns **extra terrain-relative vertical separation**, not every form of spatial niche partitioning.

This combination adds a source-specific three-dimensional case to earlier evidence that individual niches may be differentiated yet **not discretely partitioned**. In white storks, repeated individual environmental niches overlapped in nested configurations (Carlson et al. 2021); *Ia io* bats also showed seasonal changes in spatial specialization accompanied by shifts in overlap (Wang et al. 2023). We therefore make **no priority claim** for the general overlap-versus-individuality concept. The empirical increment here is a **nested, estimator-conditional 3D decomposition**: held-out vertical-shape individuality after common-horizontal and coarse-state controls, source-native **smoothed and session-centered** joint XY–relative-height probability overlap, and separate terrain-relative centered-shape segregation and co-use residual-deviation nulls. These components are not estimates of one common niche-overlap parameter. A population can contain overlapping, non-exchangeable probabilistic ways of using common airspace. Conspecifics may visit the same places and altitudes but differ in how frequently they use parts of the vertical distribution, how those probabilities persist across sessions, or which parts of repeated movement tasks they emphasize. The wild analyses confirm whole-distribution identity, not separately calibrated upper-tail strategies, prey preferences, energetic payoffs or fitness optimization. "Differentiation" here means repeatable probability-structure differences; it should not be silently upgraded to demonstrated diet or trophic specialization.

The descriptive same-cohort original-profile overlap medians (approximately 0.80–0.92 on the **1D centered-height axis**) show how extensive relative-height shape similarity can coexist with source-calibrated individual identity. These values must **not** be interpreted as 80–92% shared flight space: an earlier study of the 2016 and 2022 La Gruta source populations found approximately 20% **horizontal foraging-area** overlap using a Bhattacharyya coefficient (Calderón-Capote et al. 2025). The populations, behavioral filters, axes and overlap definitions differ, so **0.20 and 0.80–0.92 are not opposing estimates of the same ecological quantity**. Because these distributions are centered and independently constructed from self-derived spatial supports, **this is not an estimate of shared three-dimensional habitat volume, actual co-occurrence or diet overlap**, and the dyad percentiles are not uncertainty intervals. The inferential S_rel and co-use tests remain distinct, authoritative ecological comparisons.

Even with truly aligned joint horizontal–height probability distributions, the overlap of a projected height marginal can exceed the overlap of the full three-dimensional distribution: two animals at **different horizontal cells but identical height** would have marginal-height overlap 1 and joint spatial overlap 0. Because the archived Figure S1 self profiles use separate session centering and potentially different individual-specific horizontal support, their O values are not guaranteed to be genuine common-grid marginals and cannot be converted into numerical three-dimensional overlap bounds. This mathematical distinction is detailed in Supplementary Methods S13 and involves no new study-data test.

The distribution of apparent shape similarity is also **cohort-dependent**: the largest original source cohort accounts for 68.3% of the within-panel dyads in *Eidolon* and 75.0% in *P. hastatus* 2023. Alternative equal-cohort medians and single-cohort omissions produce different descriptive numbers, with some *Eidolon* cohorts contributing only one pair. Thus pair-pooling cannot be interpreted as independent species-wide replication or as a species' characteristic degree of three-dimensional niche overlap (Supplementary Table S9). It leaves the original within-source individual identity and terrain/co-use null inferences unchanged.

The source-recovered **smoothed joint 3D O_XYZ estimates** (0.181–0.309 between individuals in the 500-m eligible subset) occur alongside individually informative conditional vertical configurations. These absolute magnitudes depend on the original per-cell α = 0.5 smoothing and shared-cell sampling, so they cannot be interpreted as 18–31% of observed three-dimensional flight airspace shared. The differently estimated one-dimensional centered-height shape medians of roughly 0.80–0.92 use a larger, nonidentical sample and are not a competing physical overlap measure. This is **not evidence of contradictory results**, because horizontal occupancy, original structural gates and the underlying estimands differ. Terrain correction also markedly attenuates raw/native added-segregation contrasts in the panels where they looked largest, without uniformly reducing the personal vertical-fidelity statistic in the other panels. The change in datum and local height reference alters **the measured geometry**, not necessarily bat behavior: DEM interpolation and a mixture of ellipsoid- and mean-sea-level source GPS heights cannot identify physical flight clearance above terrain or canopy. Hence topographic structure is an indispensable measurement reference but not a demonstrated adaptive mechanism. These observations make the ecological interpretation explicitly **nested and scale-dependent**, rather than interpreting one 1D shape-overlap number as a complete 3D niche.

Spatial overlap also does not disprove competition. Past competitive sorting could help establish different solutions even if ongoing encounter avoidance is weak. Alternatively, habitual task reuse might arise without strong competition. The supported inference is that **maintenance does not require demonstrated continuing exclusive vertical layers** in the tested panels. The ecological origins and current payoff of these differences remain separate questions.

### Development changes phenotype without yielding a universal individuality-gain rule

The developmental tests keep the synthesis from treating a persistent individual as an invariant object determined at birth. Juvenile personal history became highly predictive later in early movement, but the predeclared monotonic formation rule failed at its across-individual consistency criterion. Randomized enrichment and auditory-feedback treatments affected source-study phenotype yet did not establish a corresponding treatment effect on total multivariate dispersion under the frozen reanalyses. The latter outcome is not equivalent to "development does not matter." Opposing acoustic-feature contributions and coherent but unsupported personality-feature tendencies suggest that the *allocation* or geometry of individual variation might change without a detectable common change in its scalar total.

The causal origin of repeatable movement differences remains weakly identified. Baseline predisposition, identity-specific experiences, biomechanics, social histories and stochastic reinforcement are possible mechanisms, but the current experiments do not eliminate one in favor of another for the wild field patterns. What can be said is that development, current context and ecological consequence cannot be replaced by one interchangeable magnitude of "individuality."

### A failed laboratory-to-field bridge is a boundary, not the connection

Our interpretation does not depend on pretending that the frozen FlightIntensity field bridge passed. It failed the stipulated overall 3/4 threshold, comprising two passes, one failure and a structural stop. This is evidence that the specific laboratory-style scalar carrier has not been shown to account for the wild identity result. It is **not** evidence that no possible laboratory-to-field relationship exists. Nor does it establish that the wild 3D profile of one bat results from the same information carrier preserved during an acute laboratory masker experiment in another species.

The legitimate integration is at the level of **distinguishable empirical propositions**: maintained behavioral correspondence under intervention; partial portability of measured organization across tasks; and maintained, overlapping wild vertical-use distributions. Presenting them together produces a more informative theory of what must and need not accompany persistent individuality, while leaving the direct mechanism bridge open.

### Which ecological mechanisms remain viable?

A simple account consistent with the wild evidence is **reuse of a personal movement solution**, for which stereotyped learned bat flight paths and site fidelity in other flying taxa provide biological plausibility but not direct evidence for the current animals (Barchi et al. 2013; Toledo et al. 2020; Votier et al. 2017): previous individual history remains predictive when the same general task is encountered again, even in shared space. This is a hypothesis about reuse of measurable structure, not a demonstration of memory or learned optimality. At least two nonexclusive pathways remain: individuals may spend different fractions of time in behaviorally distinct states (commuting, feeding-site approach, social travel), or differ **within the same task** through biomechanics, micro-resource choice, fine route familiarity or responses to local atmospheric conditions.

This is an explicit **identifiability problem**. An observed individual vertical-use distribution can be written conceptually as \(p_i(z\mid h,c)=\sum_k\pi_i(k\mid h,c)q_i(z\mid k,h,c)\), where \(\pi_i\) allocates time among unobserved tasks and \(q_i\) is conditional within-task vertical use. Different task proportions and different task-conditional solutions can produce exactly the same observed marginal \(p_i\); neither is uniquely recoverable from altitude tracks without verified task labels or interventions. The source-backed 500-m place × broad speed-turn results restrict **coarse** mixture explanations, not unobserved true task identity or sub-500-m microhabitat. A purely mathematical demonstration and competing prospective predictions are provided in Supplementary Methods S12 and the ecological identifiability analysis.

Place × speed × turning matching, multi-day restrictions and same-night conspecific controls weaken the simplest versions of coarse state-mixture, recent carryover and shared-night explanations, but unobserved state identities, small resource patches and local atmospheric contrasts remain. A predeclared ERA5 continuous wind-reaction-norm family stopped before numerical vertical analysis when the *P. hastatus* 2023 panel retained seven evaluable individuals against eight required; it cannot be reported as a negative wind-mechanism test. Individual morphology, energetic costs, fine environmental response and exact resource use were not independently identified as causes. The existing public-data tests locate the mechanism question without settling it.

### Scope, alternative explanations and evidential strength

Five limitations prevent a universal causal interpretation. First, source systems and outcome scales are heterogeneous, and experimental cohorts of three to five bats provide far less biological generality than their exact mapping p-values might imply. The present article is not a conventional pooled meta-analysis and not a prospectively registered multicenter experiment. Second, within the wild source screen the five centered-shape positive panels contrast with negative or inconclusive external tests; even the strongest spatial partitioning analysis is restricted to four panels of two tropical species. Third, horizontal standardization only operates at its chosen spatial resolution, and centered height removes additive offsets rather than heteroscedastic or behavior-dependent tracking error. The terrain analysis subtracts DEM heights from original GPS fields with differing vertical datums across source panels and must not be described as observed height above ground or canopy. Moreover, the 500-m joint estimator's ten within-cell bins use α = 0.5 pseudocount smoothing, and its 50-fix eligibility gate is aggregated over shared cells rather than imposed per cell. Absolute smoothed 3D overlap values may therefore be sensitive to sparse within-cell observations; their empirical α-robustness remains untested. Seasonal confounding is particularly relevant to the *P. hastatus* 2016 panel, where repeated tracking windows overlap less than in the others. Fourth, two original La Gruta source identifiers recur across the 2016 and 2022 *P. hastatus* records: the 103 archived profiles thus comprise panel-specific entries, not demonstrably 103 different bats. Possible long-term PIT-tag recapture is a source-verification question, not a newly established six-year persistence finding. Nor do the three *P. hastatus* source panels constitute independent species-level replications. Fifth, terrain/co-use/maintenance analyses are post-outcome diagnostics; null calibration performed before opening their respective later outputs does not retroactively make the entire ecological hypothesis family preregistered.

The relevant negative statements also differ in strength. The frozen first-flight primary **failed a composite sign-and-permutation gate** even though its mean test was positive. Developmental tests **did not establish an increase or difference**, not equivalence. The wild permutation tests **did not support positive added segregation**, not complete absence of resource or spatial partition at all resolutions. Three co-use panels provided positive upper compatibility endpoints of roughly one to two metres **for centered height-deviation differences**, while one panel had nominal excess centered-deviation dissimilarity under a different, coarse encounter definition; none of these endpoints measures a physical gap between bats. Selective omission of these distinctions would manufacture a cleaner but scientifically weaker story.

### A decisive integrated experiment

A decisive experiment would use **the same identifiable bats** across controlled tasks and wild trajectories. First, prospectively measure a compact correct-individual movement representation under contrasting sensorimotor conditions. Then record synchronized high-accuracy 3D tracks at **known resources**, objectively classify feeding/commuting/social tasks using independent acoustic, accelerometric or camera evidence, and measure fine wind, microhabitat and performance. Independently randomize ethically permissible task novelty or access while keeping food availability and social group structure measurable.

The hypotheses make different prospective predictions: if stable **task allocation** carries the signal, matching on validated task and resource identity should attenuate held-out individual altitude prediction; if stable **within-task solutions** carry it, identity should remain within observed tasks and patches; if **momentary social avoidance** carries extra partitioning, true synchronized co-use should add separation above a task- and environment-matched phase-shift reference. Morphology or task-specific atmospheric responses must be measured to distinguish learned familiarity from performance. Freeze the observation thresholds, effect margins, biological n gates and nulls **before any vertical outcomes**, with tag-switch or stationary-reference calibration. No result here establishes which prospective prediction will win.

The key causal manipulation is **task opportunity, history or independently measured state**, not another significance-driven mining of the same wild GPS archive. Until a source with direct state/task labels and prospective manipulations exists, personal solution reuse is an explanatory candidate, not a demonstrated learned movement law.

## Conclusion

Independent bat experiments repeatedly retain measurable individual behavioral correspondence despite altered current sensory and navigational conditions, while developmental manipulations do not support a simple common rule for changing the total amount of individualization. Coarse organization can be portable where fixed detailed trajectory geometry is not. Most importantly, several wild bat populations exhibit repeatable individual vertical-use distributions after controlling coarse horizontal occupancy and additive altitude level, and four structurally evaluable panels from two species meet their original terrain-relative individuality tests (three remain below 0.05 under a broader twelve-test sensitivity), while none supports positive added vertical segregation. **Predictive individual vertical-use organization and added vertical segregation are distinct empirical properties: the former remains detectable after coarse task/context matching in these bat panels, without a consistently supported positive segregation signal at the tested scales.** This is not a formal equivalence proof of zero added vertical segregation, nor a test of the absence of physical altitude separation, horizontal, temporal or resource partitioning. The co-use endpoint refers to *session-centered height deviations*, not actual vertical distances between bats. The current evidence does not identify a single carrier that connects laboratory organization to field space use, nor the specific origin or within-task mechanism. Treating maintenance, expression and ecological consequence as separate testable questions makes that uncertainty explicit and provides a sharper basis for future causal work.
