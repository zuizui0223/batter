# Persistent individuality without obligatory spatial partitioning: experimental and wild evidence from bats

## Abstract

Individual specialization is often inferred from repeatable differences among animals, but persistence of individual organization, its expression in a particular setting, and its consequences for ecological partitioning are different empirical propositions. We synthesized provenance-controlled reanalyses of independent public bat experiments and three-dimensional tracking datasets to ask whether identity-bearing behavior persists under changing contexts, whether its realized form transfers between tasks, and whether persistent individual vertical-space use requires additional spatial separation. Across four acute experimental programmes manipulating sensory masking, auditory processing or navigation conditions, same-individual behavioral correspondence remained supported after accounting for shared context shifts, although several programmes had only three to six biological individuals. Coarse movement organization transferred between tasks and an independent bat system, whereas a fixed detailed trajectory-geometry representation did not. Two randomized developmental programmes altered behavioral phenotype in their original studies but did not provide support for a simple effect on the total amount of multivariate individuality; a separate juvenile-history primary failed its predeclared cross-individual consistency rule despite strong late-history prediction. In six original wild tracking panels, identity predicted vertically structured space use after common coarse horizontal weighting; five retained identity after session-median altitude centering. In four structurally evaluable terrain-relative panels representing two tropical bat species, individual fidelity persisted in all four, but positive added vertical segregation was supported in none; synchronous co-use separation was supported in only one, under a coarser encounter definition. A predeclared laboratory-style carrier-to-wild bridge failed its overall gate (two passes, one failure, one structural stop). Thus persistent behavioral individuality should not be equated with fixed movement geometry, a single developmental variance parameter, or continuous exclusive spatial partitioning. The underlying carrier of persistent wild strategies and any direct laboratory-to-field mechanism remain unidentified.

**Keywords:** individual specialization; behavioral individuality; bats; context dependence; development; three-dimensional movement; spatial partitioning; experimental reanalysis

## Introduction

Populations can be composed of individuals that use resources or space in non-exchangeable ways even when their aggregate niche appears broad (Bolnick et al. 2003; Araújo et al. 2011). Such differences are common in movement studies, but the observation that identity predicts a future behavioral measurement is not by itself an explanation of how the difference arose, why it persists, or whether it produces ecological separation. These distinctions matter especially in three-dimensional movement systems: different foraging depths, flight heights and spatial histories are established phenomena, but the stability of a full individual vertical probability distribution cannot be inferred from horizontal occupancy alone (Ratcliffe et al. 2013; McIntyre et al. 2017; Gámez and Harris 2022; Xing et al. 2023).

Four logically different questions are often gathered under the label of behavioral individuality (Fig. 1). **Formation** asks how history, development and predispositions produce between-individual differences. **Maintenance** asks whether identifiable individual organization persists after a change in current conditions. **Expression** asks how that organization is realized as observable movements, vocalizations or task solutions. **Ecological consequence** asks whether the resulting differences generate resource or spatial partitioning. A context manipulation can change mean behavioral expression while preserving the ordering of individuals; individual-specific covariance structure also depends on context (Houslay et al. 2018; Mitchell and Houslay 2021). A developmental treatment can alter phenotype without yielding a supported change in overall among-individual dispersion (for related prior art, see Gallagher et al. 2026). A population may exhibit repeatable individual space-use probability distributions without individuals occupying mutually exclusive subspaces. None of these statements requires one shared latent policy measured identically in different species.

The relevant ecological question is therefore not simply whether bats possess repeatable personalities or fly at different heights. It is **when persistent individual-specific behavioral information yields ecological differentiation and when it does not require ongoing spatial exclusion**. We use "individuality" operationally to mean predictive information associated with a biological individual under a declared within-study comparison. We reserve "additional partitioning" for spatial separation exceeding an explicit within-study exchangeability expectation, rather than treating any statistical difference between individuals as segregation.

Public bat datasets make it possible to examine these questions at several inferential levels. Experiments have altered current sensory masking, graded acoustic interference, central auditory processing and navigation conditions while tracking identified bats. Other experiments have manipulated early environment or auditory feedback and measured later phenotype. Task-based recordings test whether a low-dimensional description of individual movement transfers better than detailed trajectory geometry. Finally, repeated wild tracking allows the individual-specific distribution of height to be evaluated after coarse horizontal-occupancy standardization, altitude centering, local terrain adjustment and contemporary co-use comparison.

The evidence is deliberately heterogeneous. A within-experiment assignment permutation does not have the same estimand or sampling precision as a between-session vertical-distribution prediction, and separate species cannot establish an uninterrupted causal chain from developmental state to wild niche use. We therefore do not pool incompatible numerical scores or claim an experiment that directly connects a laboratory behavioral carrier to individual field niches. Instead, we organize the source-level tests around three central propositions: (1) current perturbations need not erase identity-bearing behavioral organization; (2) persistent organization need not retain the same detailed expression across tasks; and (3) persistent individual vertical-space use need not entail positive additional vertical segregation. Developmental and early-history tests delimit what is known about formation, while a separately frozen field bridge assesses whether laboratory-style carrier evidence generalizes to wild individuality.

Our contribution is an integrated, evidence-ranked account of these propositions. The novelty is not the general existence of animal individuality, context-dependent behavior (also documented outside bats by Mathejczyk et al. 2026), or mean phenotype shifts without changes in scalar individuality. It is the joint evaluation of identity maintenance, representational portability, three-dimensional ecological overlap, and the limits of transferring causal interpretations between those levels.

## Materials and methods

### Evidence sources, provenance and units of replication

We integrated two pre-existing public-data reanalysis programmes without reopening outcomes. The wild ecological analysis is the validated JAE v0.4.0 rc2 candidate; the experiment/development programme is the BE public-causal-synthesis v1 candidate. Their source-level contracts, frozen endpoints, code and outcome receipts remain authoritative; the present arrangement of evidence is **post hoc** and was not prospectively registered as a single integrated multisystem study. Source searches and cross-source synthesis developed iteratively. Analyses originally fixed before numerical opening remain identified below; diagnostics undertaken after initial results remain explicitly marked.

In each programme the biological individual, rather than GPS fix, flight, trial or call, is the biological replication unit. Within-study repeated measurements estimate predictive distributions or correspondence statistics. Permutations and randomizations follow the original source-specific eligible exchangeability sets. Several experimental datasets comprise only three to five biological individuals; a small exact assignment p-value describes consistency of a mapping within that experiment, not population prevalence.

### Three distinct estimands

We distinguish (i) **identity correspondence** after context changes, (ii) **amount of individualization** under developmental treatments, and (iii) **ecological differentiation and partitioning** in wild three-dimensional space.

For the first, source-specific contrasts compare a correct identity mapping to alternative individual mappings after the shared shift in condition has been accounted for. Angles, flight time, vocal coordinates and turning positions are not placed on one common effect-size scale. For developmental experiments, the estimand is the randomized-treatment contrast in residual multivariate among-individual dispersion after common treatment-group shifts are removed. Lack of a significant treatment contrast does not constitute proof of equivalence. For wild data, the target is whether an individual's held-out vertical probability distribution is better predicted by its own other sessions than by comparable conspecific histories under standardized horizontal support, and separately whether different individuals show added terrain-relative separation or contemporaneous avoidance.

For conceptual clarity, an individual-specific wild profile can be denoted by p_i(z | h, c), where h is horizontal place and c denotes an observed context or session. Repeatable differences in these profiles do not logically imply a positive added-segregation statistic: different distributions can still overlap extensively. This expression is a conceptual common language and not a newly fitted universal bat-policy model.

### Ontogeny and randomized developmental manipulations

A first-flight history programme in juvenile *Rousettus aegyptiacus* tested a predeclared individual-level monotonic increase in own-history predictive advantage over eligible flight days. The fixed primary required both a positive permutation-supported programme tendency and positive slopes in at least 10 of 14 juveniles. A separately specified late-history secondary compared recent own history with experience-matched conspecific history; an early-seed-versus-recent-history comparison was added only as a post-primary diagnostic.

Two randomized developmental studies were analysed under their frozen tests. For Rachum et al.'s environmental enrichment study, 29 complete bats (14 enriched, 15 impoverished) contributed three baseline-scaled personality dimensions, and origin-stratified assignments evaluated the difference in residual dispersion after subtracting each group's shared behavioral change vector. In an Elie et al. auditory-feedback experiment, ten bats (five hearing, five deafened) were compared on the residual multivariate dispersion of their adult whole-repertoire vocal features using the exact 120 eligible assignments. Feature-level reallocations in either study were descriptive, not separately confirmatory. A separate 18-bat early-environment history-carrier contrast was evaluated under its original randomized endpoint.

### Acute sensory, neural and navigation manipulations

We retained four independent programme-level endpoints from the BE evidence ledger: *Pipistrellus kuhlii* external sensory masking and a second foam-controlled contrast; *Myotis daubentonii* flight-performance correspondence across five noise levels; *Eptesicus fuscus* vocal correspondence during reversible auditory-midbrain perturbation; and *P. kuhlii* bilateral turning-position organization across three manipulated navigation contexts (Aharon et al.). The identity-mapping nulls, support gates and biological n are source-specific. They estimate resistance of *measured individual correspondence* to an acute intervention, not the anatomical storage location of a shared neural controller.

### Task portability and representation

For *Rhinolophus nippon*, a pre-specified coarse I/M movement representation was tested across obstacle contexts. The fixed representation was then transferred to an independent *Carollia perspicillata* corridor system. A fixed detailed, scale-free trajectory-geometry representation provided the comparison. Transfer of predictive organization is not interpreted as evidence that the same latent parameter or policy exists in the two species.

### Repeated wild three-dimensional tracking

The outcome-blind wild-data screen considered 23 Movebank bat parent datasets and admitted six tracking panels from four taxa, building on established individual space-use and seasonal movement ecology (Kerches-Rogeri et al. 2020; Wang et al. 2023; O'Mara and Dechmann 2023): *Tadarida teniotis*, *Eidolon helvum*, *Hypsignathus monstrosus* and three *Phyllostomus hastatus* panels. Held-out same-individual and other-individual vertical predictors were compared under identical weights over mutually supported 5-km horizontal cells. Whole-session individual-label permutations calibrated each complete prediction pipeline. Each retained session was also median-centered before vertical binning to remove additive constant height offsets; this does not remove all state-dependent or variance-related device error.

A later, explicitly **post-outcome**, ecological geometry and maintenance family evaluated four structurally eligible panels from *H. monstrosus* and *P. hastatus*. Terrain-relative height was calculated after local digital-elevation-model subtraction at 500-m spatial resolution. The family separately tested the repeatability of terrain-relative vertical configuration (V_rel), added vertical segregation (S_rel), and excess separation during local synchronous co-use. An inherited individual-label null calibrated S_rel, while phase-shift controls and dyad resampling were used for co-use; the latter yielded compatibility intervals, not formal equivalence tests. Persistence was further checked across nonadjacent days, common fine spatial and kinematic contexts, and same-night conspecific comparison.

Previously frozen tests in *Nyctalus noctula*, *Hipposideros armiger/pratti*, *Myotis vivesi* and *Pteropus poliocephalus* remain external **boundary** programmes. Their different source gates preclude interpreting the four as a bat-wide prevalence sample. The first frozen *Nyctalus* result remains authoritative even though a later eligibility revision was more favorable.

### Predeclared laboratory-style carrier-to-field bridge

The BE programme separately froze a scalar FlightIntensity carrier bridge to wild vertical individuality. The overall criterion required at least three of four panels to pass. The frozen result was two passes, one failure and one structural stop. Later field H/V decompositions are exploratory and cannot reclassify the failed primary. Crucially, the bridge does not provide repeated laboratory and field measurements of the same individuals. It is an operational representation-transfer test, not a direct test of a shared ontogenetic or neural mechanism.

### Evidence hierarchy

We use the words **supported**, **unsupported/failed**, **unadjudicated structural stop**, and **descriptive/post-outcome diagnostic** according to the two original ledgers. A numerically small p-value cannot override a failed prespecified sign or sample-size gate; a nonsignificant test cannot establish exact absence of an effect. Across heterogeneous sources we compare designs and directional inferential statuses, not pooled effect sizes, pooled p-values, or an implied common latent trait. Detailed technical methods, source provenance, source-specific null architectures and the four-panel co-use design are retained in the original frozen packages and the [combined Supporting Information draft](SUPPORTING_INFORMATION_DRAFT_V1.md).

## Results

### Individual history is informative, but its formation trajectory is not a common monotonic law

The first-flight analysis (Fig. 2) showed a positive mean relation between experience and own-history advantage (B = +0.16733; permutation p = 0.0102). However, only 8 of 14 juveniles had positive individual slopes, below the frozen requirement of 10 of 14. The **primary formation hypothesis failed**, despite its positive mean statistic. The predeclared late-history secondary was supported (L = +112.5554; p = 0.0001; 12/14 positive): own recent movement history predicted later spatial use better than experience-matched conspecific history. An exploratory comparison favored recent over earliest-two-day history (Q = +144.0791; p = 0.0001), but this post-primary result does not convert the original formation gate to a pass.

In the independent randomized early-environment history-carrier test, enrichment did not detectably strengthen later personal-history dependence relative to impoverishment (T = +0.7081; two-sided p = 0.3244). Thus the public evidence establishes informative later personal histories without identifying a general monotonic acquisition rule or a simple causal effect of broad environmental enrichment on the history carrier.

### Developmental treatment does not map simply onto total individual differentiation

The randomized enrichment programme included 29 bats. Its contrast in residual three-trait change-vector dispersion was D = +0.734699, with enriched greater than impoverished in the observed direction, but the origin-stratified randomization test did not support an increase (p = 0.167785). All three feature contributions were positive descriptively, so the nonsupport was not explained by cancellation among opposite trait directions. A separate descriptive predictor using individual baseline plus a shared treatment-associated shift performed better than baseline alone or treatment-group state alone.

The randomized auditory-feedback programme included ten bats and produced D = -1.425497 in adult whole-repertoire dispersion, with exact two-sided p = 0.716667. The original experiment established an effect of auditory feedback on learned vocal phenotype, but the reanalysis did not support an effect on the **total amount** of individualization. Across 28 acoustic dimensions, 15 contributions were positive and 13 negative (descriptive cancellation ratio 0.840173). These opposing contributions suggest possible reallocation of individual information across measured features, but no feature-specific reallocation hypothesis was confirmatorily tested here. Neither nonsignificant total-dispersion test establishes equality between developmental treatments.

### Same-individual correspondence survived multiple acute current-context interventions

In the *P. kuhlii* external-masker programme, individual movement/approach bias remained detectable from baseline to masker (K = +4.941 degrees; five of six bats positive; exact p = 0.04028). The separate foam no-masker to foam-plus-masker contrast was also supported (K = +6.779 degrees; five of five positive; p = 0.025).

In *M. daubentonii*, individual flight-time organization remained identifiable over five graded acoustic conditions (A = +0.377542; all three individuals positive; exact identity mapping rank 1 of 1,296; p = 0.00077160). In *E. fuscus*, correct pre/post identity matching of multivariate vocal organization under reversible auditory-midbrain intervention ranked first among 24 assignments (K = +1.004452; four of four positive; p = 0.041667). In the *P. kuhlii* navigation manipulation, all four bats showed positive turning-location identity advantages, with the correct mapping ranked second of 576 allowed assignments across the three contexts (K = +3.695264; p = 0.00347222).

The common inferential result is *retained identity correspondence under changed current conditions* (Fig. 3). Because each programme measured a different behavior with a different null and only three to six biological individuals, these outcomes do not estimate a pooled physiological invariance or species-wide prevalence.

### Coarse personal organization transferred where detailed realized geometry did not

The fixed coarse I/M organization in *R. nippon* retained cross-task individual information (K = +0.55428; five of five positive; p = 0.0001). Applied without refitting the representation to an independent *C. perspicillata* system, the coarse I/M representation remained supported (K = +0.34758; p = 0.0007). By contrast, the fixed detailed scale-free Rhino trajectory-geometry representation did not pass its *Carollia* test (K = -0.0350; p = 0.2144).

These source-level PASS/FAIL outcomes (Fig. 4) support a narrower distinction between portable measured organization and detailed trajectory realization; they are not a formal between-representation K-effect difference test. It does not demonstrate one common neural policy, and nonsupport of the detailed representation does not mean that *Carollia* lacks individually repeatable fine-scale movement in every possible representation.

### Wild individuality persisted after coarse horizontal standardization and absolute-altitude centering

The six admitted wild tracking panels all had identity-matched vertical profiles outperforming their full-pipeline whole-session permutation expectation when self and other profiles were integrated under the same coarse 5-km horizontal occupancy weights. This comparison limits horizontal-occupancy confounding among tested cells; it does not eliminate within-cell spatial fidelity.

More importantly, session-median centering retained calibrated **vertical-distribution shape** identity in all five non-*Tadarida* comparative panels (Fig. 5): *E. helvum* calibrated excess +0.443 nats/fix (p = 0.0002), *H. monstrosus* +0.177 (p = 0.0002), and *P. hastatus* 2022 +0.111 (p = 0.0002), 2023 +0.118 (p = 0.0002), and 2016 +0.574 (p = 0.0076). The motivating *T. teniotis* panel failed the corresponding shape test (calibrated excess -0.022; p = 0.5121), even though absolute vertical-location identity was repeatable. Centering removes constant altitude zero-point shifts exactly but does not establish that every remaining difference is biological rather than state-dependent tag error.

Previously frozen external tests were heterogeneous: the first *N. noctula* primary failed (+0.05175; p = 0.1224), *H. armiger/pratti* failed (-0.04468; p = 0.8616), *M. vivesi* failed under a separate four-individual design (+0.00470; p = 0.4419), and *P. poliocephalus* passed under its separately frozen four-individual design (+0.16873; p = 0.0001). The *Pteropus* excess attenuated strongly after terrain correction (+0.03231; p = 0.0023). These tests define conditional scope; they do not form a representative four-species prevalence sample.

### Terrain-relative fidelity did not require added vertical segregation

In four structurally eligible 500-m panels from *H. monstrosus* and *P. hastatus*, terrain-relative vertical configuration remained individually repeatable after local elevation subtraction: V_rel = +0.042 (p = 0.0001) for *H. monstrosus*, and +0.100 (p = 0.0001), +0.077 (p = 0.0001) and +0.079 (p = 0.0345) for *P. hastatus* 2022, 2023 and 2016. These are **post-outcome ecological diagnostics**, not an independent prospective discovery series.

Yet the terrain-relative added-segregation statistics were -0.0158, -0.0090, +0.0163 and +0.0289 for the same four panels, respectively. None supported positive added segregation under its permutation null. The *H. monstrosus* signal was significant in the **negative** direction (two-sided p = 0.036); the three *P. hastatus* tests were p = 0.364, 0.270 and 0.480.

A separate local co-use analysis found no supported additional synchronous separation in *H. monstrosus*, *P. hastatus* 2022 or *P. hastatus* 2016. The positive 95% upper compatibility endpoints for excess co-use separation were approximately 0.98 m, 1.92 m and 1.13 m in these three panels. *P. hastatus* 2023 was the exception (+3.57 m; phase-shift p = 0.0231), with a 600-s **all-space** encounter definition and wide dyad-level compatibility (-4.63 to +11.73 m). For comparison, *P. hastatus* 2016 also used 600 s but excluded endpoint neighbourhoods, whereas *H. monstrosus* and *P. hastatus* 2022 used 60 s with endpoint exclusion. The three upper endpoints are descriptive compatibility constraints, **not** formal equivalence bounds or evidence of zero interaction. Co-use dyad-bootstrap intervals and the separate S_rel permutation-null envelopes represent different uncertainty objects, as labeled in Fig. 6.

Together, repeatable personal use of terrain-relative airspace and added vertical separation had different empirical outcomes in the same two-species wild panel family (Fig. 6). This is the strongest within-domain evidence that individual differentiation in probability of space use need not produce exclusive spatial layers or routine contemporaneous vertical avoidance.

### Repeatability extended beyond adjacent sessions and broad movement contexts

Post-outcome restrictions to self-training sessions at least one day from the target preserved identity in four of four eligible wild panels; at least three days in three of three; and at least seven days in two of two. Common 500-m location × speed × turning matching preserved calibrated identity in all four eligible panels (calibrated excess +0.087 to +0.451; p = 0.0002–0.0286). Under a 2-km matched place × kinematics test, own history beat same-night conspecific data in three of four panels, with the 2016 outcome unresolved at its frozen threshold (p = 0.0544).

These tests disfavor simple explanations restricted to adjacent sessions, different coarse landscape cells, broad kinematic-state mixtures or common calendar-night responses. They do not identify the persisting biological carrier, and the shared kinematic proxies do not constitute independently observed foraging or social states.

### The frozen laboratory-style carrier did not establish a field mechanism

The previously frozen scalar FlightIntensity bridge required success in at least three of four wild panels. Its observed sequence was **PASS / PASS / FAIL / STRUCTURAL STOP**, yielding two of four and an overall **failed predeclared gate**. Positive post-outcome H/V components remain exploratory. Thus laboratory-style identity organization cannot currently be treated as a demonstrated explanation of the wild vertical shapes or their overlap. The wild distribution and segregation results stand independently of this unsuccessful cross-domain carrier test.

## Discussion

### What persists is individual information, not necessarily a fixed trajectory

The integrated evidence separates persistence of identity-bearing organization from invariance of the measured behavior. Across controlled acute sensory, auditory and navigational interventions, true individual correspondence survived after common context effects were accounted for. The relevant inference is not that bats behaved identically in different environments; it is that some relationship among individuals survived substantial changes in measured behavioral expression. The coarse-I/M versus detailed-geometry transfer contrast provides a related, though independent, representation-level observation: a compact description can remain predictive when a fixed, richly geometric one does not.

These experiments involve different species, behaviors and small biological cohorts; the pattern does not locate a common neural memory or imply that one latent organization is shared across every bat. Still, it motivates treating an animal's immediately observed trajectory as only one possible realization of a more persistent, empirically measurable individual difference. An individual can be predictable without executing one literal route repeatedly.

### Individual differentiation is not the same as spatial exclusion

The ecological payoff comes from the wild evidence. After horizontal standardization and session-median centering, five comparative panels retained individual differences in the **shape** of vertical-use distributions. In the narrower four-panel, two-species terrain-relative family, the measured personal configurations remained repeatable while positive additional vertical segregation was unsupported throughout and additional synchronous separation was supported under only one heterogeneous encounter definition.

This combination matters because individual specialization is sometimes implicitly portrayed as a mosaic of exclusive individual niches. A population can instead contain overlapping, non-exchangeable probabilistic ways of using common airspace. Conspecifics may visit the same places and altitudes but differ in how frequently they use parts of the vertical distribution, how those probabilities persist across sessions, or which parts of repeated movement tasks they emphasize. The wild analyses confirm whole-distribution identity, not separately calibrated upper-tail strategies, prey preferences, energetic payoffs or fitness optimization. "Differentiation" here means repeatable probability-structure differences; it should not be silently upgraded to demonstrated diet or trophic specialization.

Spatial overlap also does not disprove competition. Past competitive sorting could help establish different solutions even if ongoing encounter avoidance is weak. Alternatively, habitual task reuse might arise without strong competition. The supported inference is that **maintenance does not require demonstrated continuing exclusive vertical layers** in the tested panels. The ecological origins and current payoff of these differences remain separate questions.

### Development changes phenotype without yielding a universal individuality-gain rule

The developmental tests keep the synthesis from treating a persistent individual as an invariant object determined at birth. Juvenile personal history became highly predictive later in early movement, but the predeclared monotonic formation rule failed at its across-individual consistency criterion. Randomized enrichment and auditory-feedback treatments affected source-study phenotype yet did not establish a corresponding treatment effect on total multivariate dispersion under the frozen reanalyses. The latter outcome is not equivalent to "development does not matter." Opposing acoustic-feature contributions and coherent but unsupported personality-feature tendencies suggest that the *allocation* or geometry of individual variation might change without a detectable common change in its scalar total.

The causal origin of repeatable movement differences remains weakly identified. Baseline predisposition, identity-specific experiences, biomechanics, social histories and stochastic reinforcement are possible mechanisms, but the current experiments do not eliminate one in favor of another for the wild field patterns. What can be said is that development, current context and ecological consequence cannot be replaced by one interchangeable magnitude of "individuality."

### A failed laboratory-to-field bridge is a boundary, not the connection

Our interpretation does not depend on pretending that the frozen FlightIntensity field bridge passed. It failed the stipulated overall 3/4 threshold, comprising two passes, one failure and a structural stop. This is evidence that the specific laboratory-style scalar carrier has not been shown to account for the wild identity result. It is **not** evidence that no possible laboratory-to-field relationship exists. Nor does it establish that the wild 3D profile of one bat results from the same information carrier preserved during an acute laboratory masker experiment in another species.

The legitimate integration is at the level of **distinguishable empirical propositions**: maintained behavioral correspondence under intervention; partial portability of measured organization across tasks; and maintained, overlapping wild vertical-use distributions. Presenting them together produces a more informative theory of what must and need not accompany persistent individuality, while leaving the direct mechanism bridge open.

### Which ecological mechanisms remain viable?

A simple account consistent with the wild evidence is **reuse of a personal movement solution**, for which stereotyped learned bat flight paths and site fidelity in other flying taxa provide biological plausibility but not direct evidence for the current animals (Barchi et al. 2013; Votier et al. 2017): previous individual history remains predictive when the same general task is encountered again, even in shared space. This is a hypothesis about reuse of measurable structure, not a demonstration of memory or learned optimality. At least two nonexclusive pathways remain: individuals may spend different fractions of their time in behaviorally distinct states (commuting, feeding-site approach, social travel), or they may differ within the same state through biomechanics, micro-resource choice, fine route familiarity or responses to local atmospheric conditions.

Place × speed × turning matching and multi-day restrictions weaken the simplest versions of state-mixture and recent-carryover explanations, but unobserved state identities and sub-500-m environmental contrasts remain. A predeclared ERA5 continuous wind-reaction-norm family stopped before numerical vertical analysis when the *P. hastatus* 2023 panel retained seven evaluable individuals against eight required; it cannot be reported as a negative wind-mechanism test. Individual morphology, energetic costs, fine environmental response and exact resource use were not independently identified as causes. The existing public-data tests locate the mechanism question without settling it.

### Scope, alternative explanations and evidential strength

Four limitations prevent a universal causal interpretation. First, source systems and outcome scales are heterogeneous, and experimental cohorts of three to five bats provide far less biological generality than their exact mapping p-values might imply. The present article is not a conventional pooled meta-analysis and not a prospectively registered multicenter experiment. Second, within the wild source screen the five centered-shape positive panels contrast with negative or inconclusive external tests; even the strongest spatial partitioning analysis is restricted to four panels of two tropical species. Third, horizontal standardization only operates at its chosen spatial resolution, and centered height removes additive offsets rather than heteroscedastic or behavior-dependent tracking error. Seasonal confounding is particularly relevant to the *P. hastatus* 2016 panel, where repeated tracking windows overlap less than in the others. Fourth, terrain/co-use/maintenance analyses are post-outcome diagnostics; null calibration performed before opening their respective later outputs does not retroactively make the entire ecological hypothesis family preregistered.

The relevant negative statements also differ in strength. The frozen first-flight primary **failed a composite sign-and-permutation gate** even though its mean test was positive. Developmental tests **did not establish an increase or difference**, not equivalence. The wild permutation tests **did not support positive added segregation**, not complete absence of resource or spatial partition at all resolutions. Three co-use panels provided positive upper compatibility endpoints of roughly one to two metres, while one panel supported extra separation under a different, coarse encounter definition. Selective omission of these distinctions would manufacture a cleaner but scientifically weaker story.

### A decisive integrated experiment

A future study should connect the levels in the **same identifiable animals**. Measure a preregistered compact movement organization under at least two controlled current contexts; experimentally randomize the availability or novelty of several task solutions; then track the same individuals with calibrated three-dimensional altitude and independently classified behavioral states at known feeding or commuting resources. Test the predeclared predictions separately: whether experimental identity organization survives condition changes; whether it forecasts held-out terrain-relative vertical probability shape; whether conditioning on measured task/resource state removes or preserves that prediction; and whether experimentally increasing solution overlap changes actual dyadic exclusion. Tag switching or stationary references would address instrument error, while morphology and fine airflow would test alternative carriers.

The key causal manipulation is **solution opportunity or history**, not another observational identity-permutation test. Until such data exist, individual information reuse is an explanatory candidate rather than a discovered behavioral law.

## Conclusion

Independent bat experiments repeatedly retain measurable individual behavioral correspondence despite altered current sensory and navigational conditions, while developmental manipulations do not support a simple common rule for changing the total amount of individualization. Coarse organization can be portable where fixed detailed trajectory geometry is not. Most importantly, several wild bat populations exhibit repeatable individual vertical-use distributions after controlling coarse horizontal occupancy and additive altitude level, and four structurally evaluable panels from two species retain terrain-relative individuality without support for positive added vertical segregation. **Persistent individuality and spatial partitioning are empirically distinct; one need not imply the other.** The current evidence does not identify a single carrier that connects laboratory organization to field space use, nor a specific origin mechanism. Treating maintenance, expression and ecological consequence as separate testable questions makes that uncertainty explicit and provides a sharper basis for future causal work.
