# Draft cover letter — Behavioral Ecology, unified bat individuality article

**NOT FOR JOURNAL SUBMISSION UNTIL HUMAN AUTHORS COMPLETE ALL BRACKETED APPROVAL FIELDS**  
**Date:** 14 October 2026  
**Target manuscript:** Persistent behavioral individuality and the limits of 3D niche inference in bats

Dear Editor,

We would like to submit our manuscript, **“Persistent behavioral individuality and the limits of 3D niche inference in bats,”** for consideration as an original research article in *Behavioral Ecology*.

The paper asks a biological question that studies of repeatability and individual specialization sometimes leave implicit: **when does persistent individual flight-height organization accompany added vertical segregation, beyond differences already present in horizontal use?** We integrate source-specific reanalyses of publicly archived sensory and navigation experiments, developmental and task-transfer recordings, and repeated three-dimensional field tracking. These are separately analysed source programmes with distinct source-specific null models; **we do not assume all source panels contain different animals**. In particular, two literal tracking-animal identifiers recur across the 2016/2022 *P. hastatus* panels from La Gruta, and a verified cross-year PIT-tag identity match is still outstanding. We do not pool their effect sizes, treat all panel-indexed profiles as independent animal replication, or claim one experimentally measured neural state directly generated wild altitude use.

Four controlled perturbation programmes retain correct-individual behavioral correspondence after sensory or navigation conditions changed, even though several comprise only three to six biological animals. A predeclared coarse movement representation transfers between task contexts where fixed detailed trajectory geometry does not receive support. Two randomized developmental experiments alter behavioral phenotype in the source publications without supporting a simple change in the total amount of multivariate individualization under our reanalyses; one juvenile formation primary fails its composite, prespecified criterion while a late-history secondary remains predictive.

The ecological center of the manuscript lies in the field evidence. Across six original tracking panels, five comparative panels retain calibrated individual vertical-distribution shape identity after shared coarse horizontal weighting and session-median centering. In the narrower four structurally eligible field panels from **two tropical species**, post-outcome terrain-relative vertical individuality is supported in all four, whereas **none** supports positive additional vertical segregation. **These field analyses are study-panel-level comparisons, not matched-individual paired experiments:** 500-m place×speed-turning matching admitted n=19/23/11/9 bats, the 500-m smoothed joint geometry V/S admitted n=8/5/6/9, and co-use I admitted n=11/6/6/5 (H/P2022/P2023/P2016). Exact bat-ID overlap was not established even where eligible n matched; no individual-level V-versus-I response was tested. A distinct co-use analysis has a **nominal source-level** positive result in one panel under a relatively coarse 600-second/all-space encounter definition (raw p = 0.0231); **this endpoint compares bats' differences in session-median-centered height deviations, not actual physical vertical gaps or avoidance**, and the other three panel tests are unsupported under their distinct definitions. However, a retrospective four-panel within-endpoint Holm sensitivity preserves **V_rel** significance in all four panels (maximum adjusted p = 0.03453), whereas the isolated co-use result becomes p = 0.0924 and the nominal negative-direction *Hypsignathus* S_rel result becomes p = 0.1448. A second, still retrospective twelve-test sensitivity pooled the four V_rel, four two-sided S_rel and four upper-tail co-use I p-values into one illustrative family. Under that *expanded* display family, V_rel remained below 0.05 in three of the four panels (H, P2022 and P2023, each adjusted p = 0.0012), whereas the P2016 V_rel adjusted p rose from 0.03453 under the within-endpoint family to 0.27622; neither S_rel nor co-use passed the expanded correction. Neither after-outcome grouping is a prospective or programme-wide familywise procedure, nor a test of an absence of spatial/resource partitioning. We explicitly describe these analyses as **post-outcome diagnostics**, not as independent preregistered confirmatory field experiments. In source-native 500-m descriptive comparisons, within-individual horizontal probability overlap also exceeds between-individual overlap in all four panels; consequently the paper **does not claim an absence of horizontal partitioning or shared resource differences**. The narrower ecological contrast concerns extra segregation of the originally **smoothed, session-centered relative-height probability distributions**. An exact source-algebra audit also shows that conditional fidelity V and added joint 3D segregation S are **nonidentical but mathematically coupled functions of the same tracking sessions**, with S_native = V_native minus the between-versus-within contrast in nonnegative horizontal-weighting overlap slack. All four source panels satisfy that identity; neither this slack nor the V-versus-S significance-count difference is a separately randomized ecological mechanism or statistically independent replication. The 500-m joint O_XYZ estimates apply α=0.5 pseudocounts to all ten within-cell bins and require 50 shared-support fixes **aggregated across cells**, so positive estimated 3D overlap is not direct evidence of observed shared airspace. Source-native S_native appeared positive in *H. monstrosus* (+0.1029) and *P. hastatus* 2016 (+0.1304), but their paired original DEM-relative results were −0.0158 and +0.0289, respectively, with unchanged horizontal overlap contrast. In the other two source panels, the processing change had the opposite direction, so this is **not a uniform terrain effect**. Individual terrain-relative fidelity remained source-supported despite these changes. This adds a spatially explicit ecological measurement result, while neither proving terrain causes individual strategies nor establishing actual height above ground: original GPS datums mix ellipsoid- and mean-sea-level-referenced fields.

Importantly, a separately frozen scalar laboratory-style carrier-to-wild bridge fails its required overall three-of-four-panel gate (two positive outcomes, one failure and one structural stop). We preserve this negative result and do **not** claim to have discovered the neural, cognitive, genetic or ecological resource mechanism causing the wild individual strategies. We believe the value of the synthesis is instead to make empirically testable distinctions between identity maintenance, behavioral expression and spatial partitioning.

We make **no priority claim** for individual niche differentiation in shared space: Carlson et al. (2021; *Nature Communications*, DOI 10.1038/s41467-021-24826-x) already reported persistent nested/overlapping individual environmental niches in white storks, and Wang et al. (2023; *Movement Ecology*, DOI 10.1186/s40462-023-00394-1) documented spatial specialization and seasonal overlap in bats. Even within our *P. hastatus* source population, Calderón-Capote et al. (2025; *PLOS ONE*, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0313782) had already evaluated 2016/2022 **horizontal foraging-area overlap** and social-group organization, finding approximately 0.20 average 2D foraging-area overlap; this differs fundamentally from our newly plotted 0.80–0.92 overlap of independently median-centered **1D altitude probability shapes**. Neither coefficient represents common three-dimensional habitat volume. We likewise do not claim the first demonstration of behavioral individuality across contexts or phenotype changes without a change in scalar individuality, which have prior art in *Drosophila* and clonal fishes. The more specific candidate advance here is **predictive bat vertical-distribution shape retained under matched coarse horizontal place and movement categories**, together with distinct null-calibrated tests of **centered relative-height-shape segregation** and **co-use residual-deviation association**, integrated with controlled behavioral identity results while **preserving the failed laboratory–field bridge**. The source-matched 500-m place × coarse speed–turning post-outcome stress test retained an individual effect in 4/4 eligible panels, but unobserved task allocation and within-task execution remain indistinguishable without task labels or interventions.
