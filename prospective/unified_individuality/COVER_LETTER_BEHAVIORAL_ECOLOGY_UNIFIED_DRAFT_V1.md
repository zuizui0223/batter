**Date:** 14 October 2026  
**Target manuscript:** Persistent individuality need not entail spatial partitioning in bats

Dear Editor,

We would like to submit our manuscript, **“Persistent individuality need not entail spatial partitioning in bats,”** for consideration as an original research article in *Behavioral Ecology*.

The paper asks a biological question that studies of repeatability and individual specialization sometimes leave implicit: **does persistence of individually predictable behavior require those individuals to occupy separate ecological space?** We integrate source-specific reanalyses of publicly archived sensory and navigation experiments, developmental and task-transfer recordings, and repeated three-dimensional field tracking. These datasets are biologically independent and have distinct null models; we do not pool their effect sizes or claim that one shared experimental neural state was directly measured in wild-tracked bats.

Four controlled perturbation programmes retain correct-individual behavioral correspondence after sensory or navigation conditions changed, even though several comprise only three to six biological animals. A predeclared coarse movement representation transfers between task contexts where fixed detailed trajectory geometry does not receive support. Two randomized developmental experiments alter behavioral phenotype in the source publications without supporting a simple change in the total amount of multivariate individualization under our reanalyses; one juvenile formation primary fails its composite, prespecified criterion while a late-history secondary remains predictive.

The ecological center of the manuscript lies in the field evidence. Across six original tracking panels, five comparative panels retain calibrated individual vertical-distribution shape identity after shared coarse horizontal weighting and session-median centering. In the narrower four structurally eligible field panels from **two tropical species**, post-outcome terrain-relative vertical individuality is supported in all four, whereas **none** supports positive additional vertical segregation. A distinct co-use analysis supports extra vertical separation in one panel under a relatively coarse 600-second/all-space encounter definition; the other three are unsupported under their source-specific encounter definitions. We explicitly describe these analyses as **post-outcome diagnostics**, not as independent preregistered confirmatory field experiments.

Importantly, a separately frozen scalar laboratory-style carrier-to-wild bridge fails its required overall three-of-four-panel gate (two positive outcomes, one failure and one structural stop). We preserve this negative result and do **not** claim to have discovered the neural, cognitive, genetic or ecological resource mechanism causing the wild individual strategies. We believe the value of the synthesis is instead to make empirically testable distinctions between identity maintenance, behavioral expression and spatial partitioning.

The contribution is therefore not the first demonstration of behavioral individuality across contexts or of environmental mean shifts without scalar increases in individuality—both have relevant prior art, including recent work in *Drosophila* and clonal fishes. It is a comparative, provenance-documented examination of how the maintenance and ecological consequences of individuality can come apart, with an unusually explicit boundary on the laboratory–field explanation.
