# Public causal synthesis literature positioning v1

## Status

**CLAIM-BOUNDARY NOTE.**

Purpose:
prevent the public-data bat synthesis from claiming novelty for ideas that are already established in animal-personality / behavioural-syndrome theory.

## What is already established

### Environment-dependent trait covariance is not new

Mitchell & Houslay (2020/2021),
*Context-dependent trait covariances: how plasticity shapes behavioral syndromes*,
Behavioral Ecology,
DOI: 10.1093/beheco/araa115.

Key prior principle:

> environmental context can alter behavioural trait covariances and therefore the apparent structure of behavioural syndromes.

Thus do not claim novelty for:

- "environment changes personality structure";
- "behavioural covariance can depend on context";
- "individual-by-environment interaction can alter multivariate behavioural structure".

### Total among-individual variation and orientation are already distinct objects

White, Pascall & Wilson (2020),
*Towards a comparative approach to the structure of animal personality variation*,
Behavioral Ecology,
DOI: 10.1093/beheco/arz198.

This work explicitly compares among-individual covariance matrices in terms of:
- total variation;
- orientation;
- higher-dimensional shape.

Thus do not claim novelty for the abstract distinction:

> amount of individuality != orientation of individuality.

### Mean plasticity can coexist with stable among-individual structure

Houslay et al. (2018),
*Testing the stability of behavioural coping style across stress contexts in the Trinidadian guppy*,
Functional Ecology,
DOI: 10.1111/1365-2435.12981.

They found:
- strong population-level behavioural plasticity across stress contexts;
- individual-by-environment effects;
- little evidence for changes in multivariate I-matrix structure;
- no cross-context change in total among-individual variation.

Thus the idea that mean expression can move while multivariate individuality remains comparatively stable is already empirically established outside bats.

### Developmental manipulations can alter personality variance/covariance

Goerlich-Jansson et al. (2018),
*Cortisol during adolescence organises personality traits and behavioural syndromes*.

Experimental adolescent cortisol exposure changed:
- mean trait expression;
- repeatability / among-individual variance for some traits;
- correlations among traits.

Thus developmental environment can experimentally reorganize personality structure.

Again, the broad principle is not new.

# What the batter public-data synthesis adds

The contribution is narrower and empirical.

## 1. Independent bat manipulations separate formation from maintenance

Current-context perturbations repeatedly preserve identity mapping:

- external sensory masking;
- graded masking;
- central auditory perturbation;
- navigation-context manipulation.

The individual correspondence survives even when shared behavioural expression moves.

## 2. Developmental manipulations do not obey one scalar rule

Two randomized developmental bat experiments give distinct patterns.

### Broad enrichment

- phenotype affected in the source study;
- frozen individualization D positive across all three laboratory personality traits;
- randomized total-individualization test unsupported.

This is an **expansion-like but unresolved** pattern.

### Developmental auditory feedback

- specific learned vocal phenotype strongly affected in the source study;
- frozen whole-repertoire total individuality unchanged;
- 28-feature decomposition shows 15 positive and 13 negative contributions;
- cancellation ratio = 0.840173.

This is a **reallocation-like** pattern.

Therefore the bat evidence does not support either generic rule:

- `stronger / richer experience → more individuality`;
- `developmental perturbation → less individuality`.

Instead developmental effects appear mechanism-specific in **where** among-individual variation is expressed.

## 3. Same programme also observes history-specific refinement

In the juvenile-history source:
- earliest history is not a robust late-state template;
- recent own history becomes strongly more predictive.

Thus personal organization can be refined through individual history even when group-level developmental manipulations do not predict total individuality amount.

# Defensible novelty statement

Do not write:

> We show for the first time that environments alter the multivariate structure of animal personality.

That is false.

A defensible statement is:

> **Across independent public bat experiments, developmental manipulations and current-context perturbations affect different layers of individuality: developmental history can reshape phenotype and redistribute where individual differences are expressed, whereas established individual correspondence repeatedly persists through acute sensory and navigation perturbations.**

An even more compact framing:

> **Development appears to reorganize individuality; current context mainly reorganizes its expression.**

This is a synthesis claim, not a single-experiment theorem.

---

# Remaining causal gap

The current public data still do not establish the rule that determines:

> **which behavioural dimensions become individualized for a particular individual history.**

The next genuinely valuable public dataset would need:
- randomized or well-identified developmental history;
- repeated multivariate individual measurements;
- enough replication to test reallocation/orientation prospectively;
- preferably an independently specified behavioural solution space.

Another demonstration of repeatability or context-dependent covariance alone has low marginal value.
