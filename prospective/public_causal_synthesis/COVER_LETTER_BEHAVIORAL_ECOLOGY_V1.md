# Cover letter — Behavioral Ecology — draft v1

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Individual organization remains detectable across acute perturbations in bats,”** for publication as an Original Article in *Behavioral Ecology*.

Behavioral individuality is often discussed as a single quantity, yet environmental change can act on several distinct empirical layers: how individual differences emerge, whether identity-bearing organization survives current perturbation, how that organization is expressed in a particular task, and whether it produces ecological niche differentiation. We use provenance-controlled reanalyses of independent public bat experiments to separate these questions.

Across four controlled current-context manipulations—external sensory masking, graded acoustic masking, reversible auditory-midbrain perturbation, and altered navigation conditions—the same biological individual remained detectably identifiable after shared condition shifts were removed. By contrast, two randomized developmental experiments altered behavioral phenotype in their source studies but did not show a simple corresponding change in total multivariate individualization under frozen reanalyses. Cross-task analyses further show that a coarse individual movement representation transfers more broadly than detailed realized geometry, whereas a frozen laboratory-to-wild carrier bridge fails.

We do not present this work as a preregistered multi-study meta-analysis. Public-source discovery and the cross-study synthesis developed iteratively. However, within each source analysis, the endpoint, biological support rule, aggregation hierarchy, and null/randomization model were fixed before the corresponding numerical outcome was opened in this programme. Positive, negative, and failed-gate results are retained in the manuscript.
