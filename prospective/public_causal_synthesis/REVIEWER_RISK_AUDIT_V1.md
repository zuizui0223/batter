# Reviewer-risk audit v1

## Status

**PRE-MANUSCRIPT CLAIM-STRESS TEST.**

Purpose:
identify where the current synthesis is strongest and where a reviewer can correctly accuse it of overclaiming.

---

# Risk 1 — "formation and maintenance are separable" may sound stronger than the design

The programme compares:
- developmental/history datasets;
- acute perturbation datasets;
- cross-task portability datasets;
- wild spatial datasets.

These are not one randomized factorial experiment in the same biological individuals.

Therefore:

> **formation and maintenance are empirically distinguishable layers in the programme**

is defensible.

But:

> **the same individual state was experimentally shown to be formed by one process and maintained by another**

is not established.

### Safer wording

Prefer:

> **formation, maintenance, expression and ecological consequence show different empirical signatures across the analysed bat systems.**

or:

> **the evidence is inconsistent with treating behavioral individuality as one scalar property that responds uniformly to environmental change.**

---

# Risk 2 — several maintenance datasets have very small biological n

Examples:
- Myotis: n=3;
- Eptesicus: n=4;
- Aharon Pipistrellus: n=4;
- Rhinolophus mechanism programme: n=5.

Exact/randomization tests give strong within-archive mapping evidence.

They do not give:
- precise species-level prevalence;
- broad population effect-size estimates;
- independence equivalent to dozens of biological individuals.

### Required manuscript language

Use:

> **strong within-experiment identity correspondence**

not:

> **strong population-level generality**.

Show biological n directly in every perturbation figure.

---

# Risk 3 — exact p-values from tiny n can look more impressive than the biology warrants

Examples:
- Myotis p=1/1296 from condition-wise identity assignments;
- Eptesicus p=1/24;
- Aharon p=2/576.

These p-values quantify:
- extremeness of the true cross-condition identity mapping under the frozen combinatorial null.

They do not imply:
- high statistical power for population prevalence;
- a large population effect.

### Figure rule

Always pair:
- exact p;
- biological n;
- positive-individual count;
- endpoint definition.

Do not plot p-values alone.

---

# Risk 4 — developmental nulls do not prove "development does not affect individuality"

Rachum:
- D = +0.734699;
- p = 0.167785;
- direction is coherent across 3/3 traits.

Elie:
- D = -1.425497;
- p = 0.716667;
- strong opposing feature contributions cancel.

Therefore the correct conclusion is:

> **the tested developmental manipulations do not support a simple effect on the total amount of individuality.**

Not:

> **development has no effect on individuality.**

The descriptive decompositions actually suggest changes in structure/allocation may exist.

---

# Risk 5 — current-context persistence itself is not novel

Direct prior art now includes:
- Mathejczyk et al. 2026: individuality across environmental contexts in Drosophila;
- multiple animal-personality studies of context consistency.

Therefore the manuscript cannot be sold as:

> first evidence that individuality persists across contexts.

The bat contribution is:
- multiple controlled sensory/navigation manipulations;
- exact identity correspondence;
- integration with developmental and ecological-boundary evidence.

---

# Risk 6 — mean shift without individuality-magnitude change is also not novel

Gallagher et al. 2026:
- clonal Amazon mollies;
- developmental predator-cue manipulation;
- mean behavior changes;
- magnitude of individuality unaffected.

Therefore Rachum/Elie should not be sold as discovery of this general principle.

Their value in this manuscript is comparative:

> **two independent bat developmental manipulations produce phenotype effects that do not map simply onto total individualization, while acute perturbations repeatedly preserve identity correspondence.**

---

# Risk 7 — heterogeneous endpoints prevent a conventional meta-analysis

The programme includes:
- movement angles;
- flight time;
- vocal vectors;
- bilateral turning location;
- low-dimensional movement policy;
- personality-trait dispersion.

These statistics are not on one effect-size scale.

Therefore:
- do not compute a pooled standardized effect merely to obtain one grand number;
- do not visually imply K values are comparable across panels.

### Better common object

The common object is qualitative/inferential:

> **same-individual correspondence after removal of shared context effects.**

For acute perturbation panels, report:
- sign;
- positive-individual fraction;
- exact/null rank;
- biological n.

---

# Risk 8 — programme-level dataset selection is not fully prospective

Individual source analyses were frozen before numerical opening.

But the broader programme:
- searched multiple public datasets;
- retained scientifically useful sources;
- evolved the synthesis question over time.

This is not equivalent to a prospectively preregistered multi-study meta-analysis.

### Required transparency

State explicitly:

> **The source-level analyses were prospectively frozen before outcome opening, whereas the cross-study synthesis was developed iteratively as public sources were audited.**

This honesty is essential.

Do not call the whole programme preregistered.

---

# Risk 9 — the wild ecological bridge failed

Laboratory evidence:
stronger.

Wild bridge:
- frozen scalar carrier gate = 2/4 FAIL;
- H/V = post-outcome exploratory.

Therefore ecological relevance must not be manufactured by merging them.

### Correct role

The failed wild bridge is a strength of the paper if presented as a boundary:

> **persistent laboratory individuality does not automatically imply wild spatial partition or niche differentiation.**

This directly motivates separating organization from ecological consequence.

---

# Risk 10 — "one persistent personal organization" may imply an unobserved universal latent state

The programme has:
- movement organization;
- vocal organization;
- navigation organization.

No common latent variable was measured across all systems.

Therefore prefer:

> **individual-specific organization**

over:

> **the individual state**

when discussing cross-species synthesis.

Use singular state only within a specific dataset where it is operationally defined.

---

# Safest central claim

> **Across independent bat experiments, individual-specific organization repeatedly remains detectable after acute sensory or navigational perturbation, whereas developmental manipulations that alter phenotype do not show a simple corresponding change in total individualization. Coarse personal organization is more portable than detailed realization, and its wild ecological consequences remain a separate unresolved layer.**

This is long but fully supported.

---

# Safer title hierarchy

## Best balance

**Acute perturbations preserve individual organization across bat systems**

Pros:
- directly supported;
- no claim of universal formation mechanism;
- strongest evidence is positive.

Cons:
- formation evidence becomes secondary.

## Best conceptual title

**Behavioral individuality occupies separable causal layers in bats**

Pros:
- captures full programme.

Cons:
- "separable" remains a synthesis inference across heterogeneous systems.

## Most conservative

**Persistent individual organization and context-specific expression across public bat experiments**

Pros:
- very defensible.

Cons:
- less striking.

## Avoid

- "Formation and maintenance are controlled by different mechanisms"
- "Individuality is invariant to environment"
- "Development does not change individuality"
- "A universal bat personal policy"
- "Individual specialization persists independently of context"

# Journal ceiling from the present evidence

## Plausible upper target

**Proceedings of the Royal Society B**

Reason:
- comparative animal-behavior question;
- multiple controlled experiments;
- mechanistic framing;
- strong provenance;
- mixed positive/negative evidence.

Main risk:
heterogeneous small-n public reanalyses may be judged as synthesis rather than one decisive experiment.

## Strong fit

**Behavioral Ecology**

Reason:
- individuality/personality;
- development × environment;
- context dependence;
- ecological consequence boundary.

## Also plausible

**Animal Behaviour**
or
**Royal Society Open Science**.

## Not currently justified without a new decisive formation result

- Nature Ecology & Evolution;
- Ecology Letters as a new general principle;
- Nature Communications;
- PNAS.

Recent 2026 prior art makes those broad novelty claims especially difficult.

---

# Decision

Proceed with the manuscript only if its purpose is:

> **a rigorously provenance-controlled comparative decomposition of behavioral individuality across bat experiments.**

Do not proceed if the intended sales pitch requires:

> **discovery of a universal new principle of individuality.**

The current data support the first, not the second.
