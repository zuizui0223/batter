# Cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology — v0.3.5

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Repeatable vertical identity in bat airspace persists
after coarse horizontal occupancy is standardized,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

The ecological problem is simple but under-tested: individual differences in vertical space use
can be generated by repeated use of different horizontal patches or central-place routes. We ask
whether individual identity still predicts held-out vertical state after self and conspecific
profiles are evaluated under the same coarse horizontal occupancy distribution.

Across six public tracking panels from four bat taxa, identity-matched vertical profiles exceed
panel-specific whole-session exchangeability expectations in all six datasets. We then froze and
ran two additional reviewer-facing audits before revising the manuscript. First, the same
x-y/time-only 1-km endpoint-neighbourhood exclusion was extended prospectively to the five
comparative panels. Four pass the predeclared rule; *Eidolon helvum* retains a strong calibrated
signal but fails the frozen minimum-evaluable-individual gate, while the earlier focal *Tadarida*
test remains an inferential failure. Thus endpoint-associated structure does not generally erase
the signal, but central-place effects cannot be declared absent.

Second, we calibrated the two intuitive biological effect translations against their own
finite-sample nulls. Pairwise self-identification exceeds the panel-specific permutation baseline
in five of six panels; the 2016 *Phyllostomus* panel does not. In focal *Tadarida*, the raw
256-m expected-AGL separation is compared with a 134-m exchangeability baseline, leaving a
123-m calibrated excess (p=0.0297).

The analytical history is fully retained. Earlier architecture language was withdrawn after a
frozen estimator audit showed that intuitive zero baselines and ordinary marginal scores were
misleading. No failed endpoint has been rescued by retuning. The source universe remains closed,
and negative or attrition-limited tests are reported in the main manuscript.

We believe the paper contributes both ecological and methodological insight: repeatable vertical
individual structure persists beyond coarse horizontal occupancy in multiple bat systems, while
prediction-based individuality statistics require calibration of the complete analysis pipeline
rather than assumed zero or 0.5 nulls.

All tracking data are publicly archived. No new animal handling was conducted.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
