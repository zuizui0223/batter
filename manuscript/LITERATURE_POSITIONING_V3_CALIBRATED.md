# Literature positioning v3 — calibrated vertical identity

Checked: 2026-09-27

## Positioning after estimator calibration

The paper should **not** claim that individual vertical specialization, vertical niche
partitioning, or individual differences in flight altitude are new phenomena.

The literature already contains all three ingredients separately:

1. long-term individual specialization in vertical diving behaviour;
2. repeatable three-dimensional foraging habitat use;
3. individual differences in the flight altitude of free-flying birds.

The defensible novelty is narrower:

> **The study tests whether individual identity in vertical airspace remains repeatable after
> self and other individuals are standardized to the same horizontal space-use distribution,
> using held-out self-versus-other prediction across six tracking panels from four bat taxa.**

This is a test of vertical non-exchangeability **beyond horizontal fidelity**, not a claim to
have discovered the vertical niche or individual vertical differences.

## 1. General individual-specialization framework

### Bolnick et al. 2003

Bolnick, D.I. et al. *The ecology of individuals: Incidence and implications of individual
specialization*. The American Naturalist 161:1–28.
DOI: 10.1086/343878.

Role in the paper:
- population niches can be mixtures of non-exchangeable individuals;
- motivates separating population-level niche width from within-individual consistency.

### Bat horizontal-space specialization

Kerches-Rogeri et al. 2020 showed individual specialization in horizontal space use by
frugivorous bats (Journal of Animal Ecology; DOI 10.1111/1365-2656.13339).

Wang et al. 2023 showed that spatial individual specialization in the great evening bat
*Ia io* changed between summer and autumn (Movement Ecology 11:32;
DOI 10.1186/s40462-023-00394-1).

These studies establish that bat space use can be individually structured. The present study
must therefore explain why its vertical test is not merely another home-range specialization
analysis.

## 2. The vertical niche is already an established ecological concept

### Gámez & Harris 2022

Gámez, S. & Harris, N.C. *Conceptualizing the 3D niche and vertical space use*.
Trends in Ecology & Evolution 37:953–962.
DOI: 10.1016/j.tree.2022.06.012.

This work explicitly treats vertical stratification as an additional ecological niche axis and
argues that species and individuals can use vertical space to access resources, avoid
competitors and predators, and respond to microclimatic gradients.

### Xing et al. 2023

Xing, S. et al. *Ecological patterns and processes in the vertical dimension of terrestrial
ecosystems*. Journal of Animal Ecology 92:538–551.
DOI: 10.1111/1365-2656.13881.

This review places vertical structure directly within animal ecology and JAE's conceptual
territory.

Implication:
**“vertical space matters” is background, not novelty.**

## 3. Individual vertical specialization is already known in diving animals

### Woo et al. 2008 — Brünnich's guillemots

Woo, K.J. et al. *Individual specialization in diet by a generalist marine predator reflects
specialization in foraging behaviour*. Journal of Animal Ecology 77:1082–1091.
DOI: 10.1111/j.1365-2656.2008.01429.x.

Individuals specialized in dive depth, dive shape and flight time; behavioural differences
persisted from hours to years.

### Ratcliffe et al. 2013 — South Georgia shags

Ratcliffe, N. et al. *The roles of sex, mass and individual specialisation in partitioning
foraging-depth niches of a pursuit-diving predator*. PLOS ONE 8:e79107.
DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0079107.

About 45.5% of dive-depth variance occurred among individuals in the full population model, and
the study explicitly interpreted depth selection as individual specialization of a vertical
foraging niche.

Importantly for the present study, that paper also notes that individual depth specialization
can be confounded with repeated use of patches of different bathymetry, while some systems show
different depth strategies over the same foraging patch.

That is the closest conceptual analogue to our horizontal-standardization problem.

### Southern elephant seals

McIntyre, T. et al. 2017. *Slow to change? Individual fidelity to three-dimensional foraging
habitats in southern elephant seals, Mirounga leonina*. Animal Behaviour 127:91–99.
DOI: 10.1016/j.anbehav.2017.03.006.

The study documented inter-migration fidelity to spatial areas, vertical depth layers and thermal
conditions in a true 3-D foraging framework.

Implication:
**the paper cannot claim the first repeated 3-D or vertical individual niche.**

## 4. Individual flight-altitude differences are also not new

### Dreelin et al. 2018 — aerial insectivorous birds

Dreelin, R.A., Shipley, J.R. & Winkler, D.W. *Flight Behavior of Individual Aerial
Insectivores Revealed by Novel Altitudinal Dataloggers*. Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution
6:182.
DOI: 10.3389/fevo.2018.00182.

Barometric loggers recorded breeding Tree Swallows, Barn Swallows and Purple Martins over
multiple days. Individual identity was significant in models of flight-bout altitude, alongside
species, time and atmospheric conditions.

Therefore:
- individual variation in airborne flight altitude has already been demonstrated;
- our novelty cannot be “first individual differences in flight altitude”.

The important distinction is that Dreelin et al. estimated individual effects on altitude,
whereas batter tests **held-out identity transfer** and explicitly removes differences in
horizontal cell-use weighting.

### Flight-height telemetry is measurement-sensitive

Cook et al. 2023 showed that GPS and barometric altimeters can yield systematically different
flight-height distributions in Lesser Black-backed Gulls and that the magnitude of disagreement
can vary among individuals.

Cook, A.S.C.P. et al. *Flight heights obtained from GPS versus altimeters influence estimates
of collision risk with offshore wind turbines in Lesser Black-backed Gulls Larus fuscus*.
Movement Ecology.
DOI: 10.1186/s40462-023-00431-z.

This supports keeping the source-specific vertical reference caveat prominent and avoiding
absolute cross-taxon height comparisons.

## 5. Repeatable movement identity is broadly established

Franklin et al. 2022 synthesized 177 repeatability estimates from 47 bird species and found mean
repeatability of migratory timing around 0.414.

Franklin, K.A. et al. *Individual repeatability of avian migration phenology: A systematic
review and meta-analysis*. Journal of Animal Ecology 91:1416–1430.
DOI: 10.1111/1365-2656.13697.

Multiannual tracking studies also report repeatable migration routes, wintering areas and
timing in birds.

Implication:
repeatability by itself is not the contribution. The contribution is the **dimension and
counterfactual control** used to establish it.

## 6. Precise novelty boundary

### Do not claim

- first evidence of individual vertical specialization;
- first evidence of repeated 3-D individual niches;
- first evidence that flying animals differ in altitude;
- first evidence that bats show spatial individual specialization;
- a universal vertical strategy or personality trait.

### Preferred novelty

> **We ask whether repeatable individual information exists specifically in the vertical
> component of bat airspace use after horizontal space-use fidelity is held constant.**

The empirical advance has four parts:

1. **horizontal-standardization:** self and other vertical profiles are integrated under the
   same horizontal cell-use weights;
2. **held-out identity test:** another session from the same individual is evaluated against
   other individuals on unseen target data;
3. **pipeline-calibrated inference:** whole-session identity permutations estimate the
   panel-specific finite-sample null rather than assuming a zero log-score baseline;
4. **multi-panel replication:** the same test is applied to six pre-existing panels spanning four
   bat taxa, without reopening the source search after outcomes.

This combination is the paper's novelty.

## 7. Ecological contribution

The manuscript should lead with a biological alternative hypothesis:

> Apparent vertical individuality may be generated trivially if individuals repeatedly use
> different horizontal locations that differ in terrain or vertical opportunity.

The calibrated result answers that alternative directly:

> **Individual vertical identity remains detectable after horizontal use is standardized.**

That result means a pooled 3-D population niche can contain a repeatable individual component
along the vertical axis that cannot be reduced to horizontal home-range fidelity alone.

The focal *Tadarida* AGL analysis strengthens the vertical interpretation at 2.5–5 km, while the
predeclared endpoint-proxy exclusion and 10-km failures prevent overstatement about central-place
independence or scale invariance.

## 8. Recommended introduction sequence

1. Populations can be mixtures of individual specialists.
2. Space-use specialization has mostly been framed horizontally in bats.
3. Vertical niche specialization is known in diving vertebrates and airborne altitude
   differences are known in birds.
4. The unresolved problem is **confounding between vertical individuality and horizontal
   fidelity**.
5. State the test: hold horizontal cell-use weights common, then ask whether individual vertical
   profiles still predict held-out sessions.
6. Introduce the outcome-blind six-panel bat comparison.
7. State the focal claim ceiling: no fixed route map, no central-place independence, no universal
   scale invariance.

## 9. Recommended title language

Strong options:

- **Individual vertical identity in bats persists beyond horizontal space-use fidelity**
- **Repeatable bat vertical airspace use persists after horizontal standardization**
- **Bat vertical individuality persists after controlling horizontal space use**

Avoid:
- “multiple predictive architectures”;
- “first vertical specialization”;
- “3-D personality”;
- “individual flight-height strategies” unless clearly qualified as predictive rather than
  mechanistic.

## 10. Journal positioning

### Journal of Animal Ecology

The recalibrated story is more naturally ecological than the superseded architecture story.

JAE framing:
- individual specialization and population niche structure;
- vertical niche as an ecological dimension;
- direct control of the most natural alternative explanation (horizontal fidelity);
- multiple bat systems;
- retained focal negative mechanism/robustness results.

The paper should avoid leading with estimator correction. Calibration is necessary methodology,
but the biological headline is **vertical individuality beyond horizontal fidelity**.

### Movement Ecology

Still the strongest backup because the study is built from 3-D tracking, held-out movement
prediction and spatial-scale sensitivity.

The difference in framing:
- JAE: individual niche structure;
- Movement Ecology: 3-D movement repeatability and predictive transfer.

## Bottom line

The literature makes the novelty narrower but stronger:

> **Vertical individual specialization is not new. What is new in this study is evidence that
> repeatable vertical identity in free-flying bats survives an explicit standardization of
> horizontal space use, repeatedly across a closed multi-taxon tracking panel.**
