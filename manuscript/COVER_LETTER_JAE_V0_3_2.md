# Anonymous cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Individual vertical identity has multiple predictive architectures across bat systems,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Individual specialization is commonly treated as a difference in niche breadth or specialization magnitude. Our manuscript asks a different question: **where is repeatable individual information spatially organized in three-dimensional airspace?** We separate cross-night predictive identity carried by an animal-wide vertical distribution from additional identity that appears when vertical state is conditioned on horizontal place.

The study combines a focal three-dimensional bat dataset with an outcome-blind public-data screen and prospectively frozen comparative tests. Two independent species reproduce conditional-dominant vertical identity, whereas a third independent panel is marginal-dominant. Within that third species, an untouched prospective temporal test fails to reproduce the earlier architecture, ruling out a simple seasonal explanation.

We believe three features make the study particularly relevant to *Journal of Animal Ecology*. First, the result concerns a broad principle of individual ecology: pooled population distributions can combine non-exchangeable individuals whose identity is organized through different spatial architectures. Second, the comparative panel was structurally selected before numeric vertical outcomes were used for admission. Third, we retain two important negative results in the main argument: a stronger focal residual cell-by-height stability test does not pass, and a prospective within-species architecture prediction fails. These failures place explicit limits on mechanism while sharpening the ecological conclusion.

The manuscript therefore does not claim personality, learned routes, adaptive specialization or a universal mechanism. Its central conclusion is narrower: **repeatable vertical identity can be conditional-dominant or marginal-dominant, and that predictive architecture can vary across ecological contexts.**

All analysed tracking data are publicly archived, and the complete source-screen provenance, frozen contracts, analysis code and figure generation are openly available. No new animal handling was conducted for this reanalysis.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
