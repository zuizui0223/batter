# Cover letter — Movement Ecology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research manuscript, **“Repeatable individual signatures limit exchangeability of vertical movement-state maps in European free-tailed bats,”** for publication in *Movement Ecology*.

Movement ecology often describes population-level movement patterns by pooling tracked individuals, while individual-specialization research shows that conspecifics may use different subsets of a population niche. We connect these ideas in a three-dimensional setting by asking a predictive question: **can a vertical movement-state map learned from one individual be exchanged for another individual's map without loss of held-out prediction?**

Our study reanalyses a public GPS dataset from eight European free-tailed bats (*Tadarida teniotis*). A preceding frozen analysis established a vertically thick population-level state space—approximately 4.02 effective altitude states after horizontal location was known—but the pooled location-conditioned vertical distribution failed to transfer to two independently held-out bats.

We then divided every individual's track chronologically and constructed a complete 8×8 early-to-late transfer matrix. The correctly identity-matched maps improved conditional vertical-state log score by +0.168 nats/event on average relative to the population cell-specific model. Across all 8! identity assignments, only seven assignments were at least as strong as the observed matching (P=0.000174), and six of eight bats had positive own-map gains.

Sequential mechanism tests constrained the interpretation. The signal did not reduce to a robust individual-by-location vertical interaction once marginal altitude preference was controlled, nor did marginal altitude or horizontal space use alone generally outperform population distributions. We therefore conclude that the population's three-dimensional movement niche contains **repeatable but composite individual signatures**, rather than eight isolated vertical strategies.

We believe this is a strong fit for *Movement Ecology* because organismal movement is the central ecological object, and the paper addresses a general problem in tracking studies: the distinction between the breadth of a pooled movement-state distribution and the exchangeability of that distribution among individuals. The result also complements the original environmental-mechanism study of this dataset rather than re-testing it.

The source tracking data are publicly archived in the Movebank Data Repository (DOI 10.5441/001/1.52nn82r9), and the complete analysis contracts, code and frozen result records are publicly available in the analysis repository. A permanent code/archive DOI will be added before submission.
