# Anonymous cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology — v0.3.3

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Individual vertical identity has multiple predictive architectures across bat systems,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Individual specialization is commonly treated as a difference in niche breadth or specialization magnitude. Our manuscript asks a complementary question: **where is repeatable individual information spatially organized in three-dimensional airspace?** We separate cross-night predictive identity carried by an animal-wide vertical distribution from additional identity that appears when vertical state is conditioned on horizontal place.

The study combines a focal three-dimensional bat dataset with an outcome-blind public-data screen and prospectively frozen comparative tests. Two independent species reproduce conditional-dominant vertical identity, whereas a third independent panel is marginal-dominant. Within that third species, an untouched prospective temporal test fails to reproduce the earlier architecture, ruling out a simple seasonal explanation.

We believe the study fits *Journal of Animal Ecology* because it advances a broad question in individual ecology rather than a bat-specific movement metric. Pooled population distributions can combine non-exchangeable individuals whose identity is organized through different spatial architectures. The comparative source universe was structurally selected before numeric vertical outcomes were used for admission, and we retain negative results that delimit interpretation rather than discarding them.

In particular, the focal dataset strongly supports repeatable conditional identity but a stronger preregistered residual cell-by-height stability test does not pass. We therefore do not claim a fixed individual route map. Similarly, a prospectively frozen within-species architecture prediction fails. These results sharpen the ecological conclusion: **repeatable vertical identity can be conditional-dominant or marginal-dominant, and that predictive architecture can vary across ecological contexts.**

The manuscript is within the journal's Research Article word limit and uses a numbered abstract. All analysed tracking data are publicly archived, and source-screen provenance, frozen contracts, analysis code and figure generation are openly available. No new animal handling was conducted for this secondary analysis.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
