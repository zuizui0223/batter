# Cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology — v0.3.4

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Repeatable vertical identity in bat airspace persists
after horizontal occupancy is standardized,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Individual specialization in vertical behaviour is already known from diving and flying animals.
A persistent ecological ambiguity, however, is whether apparent vertical specialization simply
reflects repeated use of different horizontal locations that differ in terrain or vertical
opportunity. Our manuscript tests that alternative directly.

Across six public tracking panels from four bat taxa, we evaluate held-out target sessions using
vertical profiles learned from the same individual and from conspecifics, while integrating both
profiles over identical horizontal-cell weights. Whole-session identity permutations preserve
within-session movement, sampling and horizontal coverage and provide a panel-specific
finite-sample exchangeability baseline. Identity-matched vertical scores exceed that expectation
in all six panels.

The magnitude is also biologically interpretable. Under common horizontal weighting, the same
individual's vertical profile outpredicts a specific alternative individual in approximately
77–86% of pairwise comparisons in five of six panels. In focal *Tadarida teniotis*, terrain-
relative vertical identity remains detectable at 2.5 and 5 km, while a 10-km test and a
predeclared night-endpoint-neighbourhood exclusion do not pass. We retain these negative controls
in the main manuscript because they define the claim ceiling: the study demonstrates vertical
individual structure beyond occupancy among the tested coarse horizontal cells, not independence
from all fine-scale horizontal or central-place structure.

The analytical history is fully disclosed. An earlier conditional-versus-marginal decomposition
was superseded after a frozen diagnostic calibration showed that its finite-sample null was not
zero and that horizontal occupancy affected the marginal score. The focal calibration was frozen
before output; a common cross-panel calibration contract was then frozen before any non-focal
calibration outputs were opened. The source universe remained closed throughout.

We believe this combination of individual-specialization ecology, explicit control of an
important spatial confound, held-out prediction and retained negative tests fits the broad
animal-ecology scope of the journal. All tracking data are publicly archived; analysis contracts,
source provenance and code are openly available. No new animal handling was conducted.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
