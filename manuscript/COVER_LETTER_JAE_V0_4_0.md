# Cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology — post-freeze maintenance revision

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Persistent individual vertical strategies need not partition three-dimensional space in two tropical bat species,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Individual differences in flight altitude are well documented. We ask a stricter ecological question: once individual vertical strategies exist, what aspects of them persist when obvious spatial and measurement explanations are removed, and does their maintenance require individuals to occupy mutually exclusive parts of three-dimensional space?

Across six public bat tracking panels, identity-matched vertical profiles exceed panel-specific session-label exchangeability expectations after self and conspecific profiles are evaluated under identical coarse horizontal weights. Five comparative panels retain calibrated vertical-distribution shape identity after every session is translated to zero median, removing any additive constant altitude offset.

Post-outcome diagnostics then separate persistence from spatial partitioning. In four structurally evaluable panels from *H. monstrosus* and *P. hastatus*, individual vertical strategy remains detectable after local DEM elevation is removed within shared 500-m cells, yet none shows permutation-supported positive terrain-relative segregation. Additional vertical separation during synchronous local co-use is unsupported in three of four panels; the fourth is a heterogeneous 2023 exception under a coarser encounter design. Thus persistent individual specialization need not correspond to exclusive vertical layers or contemporaneous avoidance.

Additional stress tests localize what is maintained. Same-individual history remains predictive when training sessions are separated from targets by multiple days, including at least seven days in the two systems with sufficient week-scale support. Individuality also survives simultaneous 500-m place × speed × turning matching in all four evaluable *H. monstrosus*/*P. hastatus* panels, and same-individual history generally outpredicts contemporaneous conspecifics after 2-km place × kinematic matching. Together these results motivate an origin-versus-maintenance distinction: processes such as competition may generate individual specialization, whereas continued expression can persist through reusable personal movement solutions without ongoing spatial exclusion.

We deliberately keep the mechanistic claim bounded. The data do not demonstrate memory, learning or adaptation. Sub-500-m resource or route fidelity, stable flight performance and fine environmental reaction norms remain viable proximate carriers. A predeclared ERA5-wind reaction-norm analysis was stopped before any vertical response was opened when its continuous-slope structural gate failed by one individual in the 2023 panel; thresholds were not relaxed and no reduced-panel rescue was used.

Previously frozen external tests also constrain generality. The first-primary *Nyctalus noctula*, *Hipposideros armiger/pratti* and *Myotis vivesi* tests did not meet their frozen criteria, whereas *Pteropus poliocephalus* did under a separate four-individual design and was strongly terrain-sensitive. These failures are retained rather than treated as inconvenient non-replications.\n\nThe motivating *Tadarida teniotis* dataset remains an explicit boundary case: its repeatable absolute vertical-location component does not survive removal of absolute altitude level. The study also retains a methodological contribution—finite prediction pipelines require pipeline-specific exchangeability calibration rather than intuitive null values.

Each post-outcome diagnostic was specified before its own result was opened, failed analyses and structural stops are retained, all tracking data are publicly archived, and no new animal handling was conducted.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
