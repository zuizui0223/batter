# Cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology — v0.3.8

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Repeatable individual shapes of vertical space use persist beyond coarse horizontal occupancy in bats,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Individual differences in flight altitude are not new. The ecological question we address is stricter: when bats are compared under the same coarse horizontal occupancy, and when each session's absolute altitude level is removed, do individuals still retain repeatable differences in the **shape** of vertical space use?

Across six public three-dimensional bat tracking panels, identity-matched vertical profiles exceed panel-specific whole-session exchangeability expectations after self and conspecific profiles are evaluated under identical 5-km horizontal-cell weights. The strongest result comes from five comparative panels spanning *Eidolon helvum*, *Hypsignathus monstrosus* and three *Phyllostomus hastatus* datasets. All five retain calibrated vertical-distribution shape identity after every session is translated to zero median, which exactly removes an additive altitude zero point.

We additionally reconstruct the already-tested leave-one-session-out self profiles to show what this individuality means biologically. Individuals within the same panel differ in how tightly vertical use is concentrated around the session median and in how much probability extends into upper and lower tails. For example, upper-tail mass at residual heights >=100 m ranges from about 2–22% among *Eidolon*, 1–15% among *Hypsignathus*, 4–16% in *P. hastatus* 2022 and 4–25% in the 2023 panel. No new clustering or hypothesis test is introduced; the figure visualizes the content of the already-completed centered-shape audit.

The motivating *Tadarida teniotis* dataset is an explicit boundary case. It retains repeatable absolute vertical-location identity, but its centered-shape test fails (p=0.5121), as does a predeclared endpoint-neighbourhood exclusion. We therefore do not treat its raw metre-scale height difference as independent evidence of device-free biological shape individuality.

The second contribution is methodological. The study shows empirically that intuitive references are not universal nulls for finite prediction pipelines: conditional-minus-marginal contrasts can have negative exchangeability expectations, pairwise self-identification nulls range from about 0.50 to 0.58, and absolute separation statistics can have positive null means. We therefore calibrate the complete prediction statistic under biologically appropriate session-label exchangeability.

All inferential robustness analyses were frozen before their outcomes were opened. Failed tests are retained, the final tag/device audit was declared in advance as the stopping point for new scientific inference, and the new individual-profile figure is descriptive only. All tracking data are publicly archived, and no new animal handling was conducted.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
