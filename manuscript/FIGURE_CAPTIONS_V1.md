## Figure 1

**Title:** Population breadth and individual exchangeability in vertical movement

**Legend:** The study separates three properties that can otherwise be conflated. The previously frozen population analysis found substantial vertical thickness within 18 five-kilometre horizontal cells, equivalent to 4.02 effective fixed altitude states. Yet the population-level location-conditioned vertical distribution did not improve prediction for either of two independently sealed bats relative to the population marginal altitude distribution. The present follow-up therefore tests individual exchangeability directly: each of eight bats is divided chronologically into early and late observations, and maps learned from early observations are scored on every bat's later observations. The identity-matched diagonal produced a mean gain of +0.168 nats per event and an exact 8! assignment P=0.000174. Altitude is GPS height above mean sea level; the figure does not represent height above ground.

## Figure 2

**Title:** Identity-matched transfer matrix of conditional vertical-state maps

**Legend:** Cross-individual transfer of early individual conditional vertical distributions to late observations. Rows are source individuals whose early observations define an individual-specific P(z|cell); columns are late target individuals. Each cell is mean log-score gain relative to the population cell-specific vertical distribution, in nats per target event. Thick borders mark identity-matched early-to-late comparisons. The identity-matched diagonal mean was +0.168 nats/event. Six of eight diagonal gains were positive, and for five targets the correct early individual map was the strict best of all eight source maps. Across all 40,320 permutations assigning early maps to late identities, the exact one-sided P value was 0.000174. Greyscale is used only to aid reading; numerical values define the result.

## Figure 3

**Title:** Decomposition of the individual identity signal

**Legend:** Observed identity-matched statistics (points) relative to the 5th–95th percentile interval of each exact 8! identity-permutation null (horizontal bars). The full conditional identity test passed its frozen support rule (filled point). The individual-by-location vertical interaction after controlling individual marginal altitude preference did not pass (P=0.160). Marginal altitude identity and horizontal cell-use identity were each matched more closely than random identity assignments (P=0.0224 and 0.00233), but only four of eight diagonal gains exceeded the corresponding population baseline, so neither component passed its full support rule (open points). The decomposition therefore supports a repeatable but composite identity signature rather than one simple individual-specialization mechanism.
