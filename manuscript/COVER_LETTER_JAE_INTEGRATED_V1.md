# Cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology — integrated submission v1

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Repeatable vertical individuality in bats persists beyond horizontal occupancy but varies among ecological systems,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Individual differences in flight altitude are well known, but they can arise because animals repeatedly use different horizontal places, because terrain converts horizontal fidelity into apparent altitude structure, or because tracking devices differ in altitude reference. We ask a stricter ecological question: after these alternatives are reduced, does individual identity still organize vertical space use, and is that organization a general property of bats or contingent on ecological system?

We first analyse a closed comparative archive of six public three-dimensional bat tracking panels. After same- and other-individual vertical profiles are evaluated under identical 5-km horizontal-cell weights and every session is translated to zero median, all five comparative panels retain calibrated identity in vertical-distribution shape; the motivating *Tadarida teniotis* panel is an explicit boundary case. Frozen secondary stress tests further show that, where centered individuality occurs, broad movement-state composition, 250–500-m place × state structure and short temporal separation do not generally absorb the signal.

Independent external tests then reveal a sharper boundary. *Nyctalus noctula*, *Hipposideros armiger/pratti* and *Myotis vivesi* fail their frozen primary criteria, whereas *Pteropus poliocephalus* passes under a separately frozen four-individual programme. Because that source combines very strong horizontal individuality with MSL altitude, we froze a post-outcome terrain audit before decoding digital-elevation values. Terrain use itself is strongly individualized, and subtracting DEM elevation attenuates the calibrated centered effect from +0.169 to +0.032, but the terrain-adjusted endpoint remains above its frozen permutation null (p=0.0023). We therefore report the confound as real and substantial, rather than treating *Pteropus* as an unqualified replication.

The broader contribution is that vertical individuality is recurrent but not universal across the tested systems. The opened contrast generates a falsifiable ecological hypothesis: stable centered vertical individuality may be strongest when animals repeatedly solve vertically structured foraging problems around persistent spatial resources, and weaker where prey is mobile or feeding is constrained near a surface. We explicitly present this as hypothesis generation rather than a confirmed cross-species effect, and we have frozen a future-data contract that excludes all currently opened systems from confirmation.

The manuscript retains negative external tests, structural stops and failed robustness tests, and preserves the chronological distinction between original confirmation, outcome-informed mechanism localization, prospective external tests and post-outcome ecological synthesis. No new animal capture, handling or instrumentation was conducted; all tracking data are publicly archived.

We believe the manuscript fits *Journal of Animal Ecology* because it uses a comparative movement-ecology problem to advance a broader ecological principle: the observable expression of individual specialization may depend not only on individual attributes, but also on the ecological opportunities repeatedly presented by a system.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
