# Cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology — integrated submission v1

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Repeatable vertical individuality in bats persists beyond horizontal occupancy but varies among ecological systems,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Individual differences in flight altitude can arise from stable behaviour, repeated horizontal-area use, terrain or tracking-device altitude reference. We ask a stricter ecological question: after these alternatives are reduced, does individual identity still organize vertical space use, and is that organization general across bats or contingent on ecological system?

In a closed comparative archive of six public three-dimensional bat tracking panels, all five comparative panels retain calibrated identity in session-centered vertical-distribution shape after same- and other-individual profiles are evaluated under identical 5-km horizontal-cell weights; the motivating *Tadarida teniotis* panel is an explicit boundary case. Frozen secondary stress tests show that broad movement-state composition, 250–500-m place × state structure and short temporal separation do not generally absorb the signal where it occurs.

Independent external tests then reveal strong heterogeneity. *Nyctalus noctula*, *Hipposideros armiger/pratti* and *Myotis vivesi* fail their frozen primary criteria, whereas *Pteropus poliocephalus* passes under a separately frozen four-individual programme. Because that source combines strong horizontal individuality with MSL altitude, we froze a post-outcome terrain audit before decoding digital-elevation values. Terrain use itself is individually repeatable; subtracting DEM elevation attenuates the calibrated centered effect from +0.169 to +0.032, but the terrain-adjusted endpoint remains above its frozen permutation null (p=0.0023). We therefore treat the confound as real and substantial rather than presenting *Pteropus* as an unqualified replication.

The resulting ecological conclusion is that vertical individuality is recurrent but not universal. The opened contrast generates—not confirms—a falsifiable hypothesis: stable centered vertical individuality may be strongest when animals repeatedly solve vertically structured foraging problems around persistent spatial resources, and weaker where prey is mobile or feeding is constrained near a surface. A frozen future-data contract excludes all currently opened systems from confirmation.

The manuscript retains negative external tests, structural stops and failed robustness tests, and keeps original confirmation, outcome-informed mechanism localization, prospective external testing and post-outcome synthesis distinct. No new animal capture, handling or instrumentation was conducted.

We believe the work fits *Journal of Animal Ecology* because it uses comparative movement ecology to advance a broader question: whether the observable expression of individual specialization depends not only on individual attributes, but also on ecological opportunity.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
