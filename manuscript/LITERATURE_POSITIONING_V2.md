# Literature positioning v2 — JAE submission frame

Checked: 2026-09-27

## Ecological antecedents

### Kerches-Rogeri et al. 2020 — Journal of Animal Ecology

**Individual specialization in the use of space by frugivorous bats.**
DOI: 10.1111/1365-2656.13339.

This study established that bat populations can contain strong individual specialization in
horizontal space and habitat use.

Our distinction:
- the response is vertical state;
- repeatability is tested by held-out self-versus-other prediction;
- we ask where predictive identity resides: marginal height versus location-conditioned height.

### Mordue et al. 2023 — European Journal of Wildlife Research

**Foraging fidelity and individual specialisation in a temperate bat *Myotis nattereri*.**
DOI: 10.1007/s10344-023-01744-5.

This provides strong bat evidence for repeated individual foraging-site fidelity and habitat
specialization.

Our distinction:
- the present paper is not a foraging-state study;
- it explicitly separates marginal vertical identity from additional conditional information.

### Wang et al. 2023 — Movement Ecology

**Linking changes in individual specialization and population niche of space use across seasons in
the great evening bat (*Ia io*).**
DOI: 10.1186/s40462-023-00394-1.

This is especially relevant because it shows that spatial individual specialization can vary
across seasons within a bat population.

Our contribution goes one step sideways rather than simply repeating that result:
- the focal dimension is vertical airspace;
- the changing object is not only specialization magnitude but the **predictive architecture**
  carrying individual identity.

### O'Mara et al. 2021 — Current Biology

**Bats use topography and nocturnal updrafts to fly high and fast.**
DOI: 10.1016/j.cub.2020.12.042.

This is the ecological/mechanistic source study for the focal *Tadarida teniotis* tracking panel.

The present analysis does not re-claim the source mechanism. It asks whether vertical-state
organization is exchangeable among individuals and how individual information is predictively
organized.

### Abrahms et al./three-dimensional fidelity precedent outside bats

Long-term three-dimensional habitat fidelity has been demonstrated in other mobile animals,
including southern elephant seals using repeated horizontal migration and diving habitat.

This broader literature prevents an overclaim that repeatable 3-D individual use is itself novel.

## Precise novelty

Avoid:

> This is the first evidence of three-dimensional individual specialization.

Preferred:

> This study separates two predictive architectures of vertical individual identity and shows,
> across a frozen multi-taxon public-data panel, that location-conditioned and marginal vertical
> information do not have a universal balance.

Three contributions matter together:

1. **estimand separation** — conditional versus marginal predictive identity;
2. **claim-boundary test** — the focal residual-map endpoint is retained as a negative result,
   preventing conditional dominance from being re-labelled as a stable route mechanism;
3. **comparative empirical contrast** — independent bat panels include both
   conditional-dominant and marginal-dominant architectures, with temporal instability within
   *Phyllostomus*.

## Journal route

### First shot: Journal of Animal Ecology

Current fit is substantially stronger than in the original eight-bat version.

The paper now addresses a broad ecological principle:
**how within-population individual heterogeneity is organized in a third spatial dimension**.

JAE is a plausible first shot because the journal explicitly seeks work that develops, tests and
advances broad principles in animal ecology, including individual and behavioural ecology.

The manuscript should therefore lead with:
- population niche as a mixture of non-exchangeable individuals;
- predictive architecture as a general property of individual specialization;
- prospective independent panels and retained negative predictions;
- implications for interpreting pooled animal-space distributions.

Do not lead with:
- ODSP;
- a new metric;
- bat-specific flight mechanics.

### Backup: Movement Ecology

Excellent fit if editorial feedback views the cross-taxon ecological principle as too narrow for
JAE.

Movement Ecology should receive a version that leads with:
- 3-D organismal movement;
- cross-night predictive identity;
- conditional versus marginal vertical state organization;
- scale dependence.

## Main desk-risk remaining for JAE

The broad principle is supported by only four admitted taxa, and vertical reference systems differ
among sources. Therefore the paper should avoid claiming a phylogenetic comparative result.

The strongest generality claim is:
**multiple predictive architectures exist and can change across contexts**, not that a particular
architecture is tied to a species trait.
