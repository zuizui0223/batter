# Cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology — v0.3.6

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Repeatable vertical identity in bat airspace persists
after coarse horizontal occupancy is standardized,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Individual differences in vertical space use can arise for several reasons that are not themselves
vertical biological specialization: individuals may repeatedly occupy different horizontal areas,
follow different central-place routes, or carry tracking devices with different altitude zero
points. Our manuscript tests these alternatives sequentially while retaining failed robustness
tests rather than retuning them.

Across six public three-dimensional tracking panels from four bat taxa, identity-matched vertical
profiles exceed panel-specific whole-session exchangeability expectations after self and
conspecific profiles are integrated under identical 5-km horizontal-cell weights. A prospectively
frozen 1-km endpoint-neighbourhood audit retains calibrated identity in four of five comparative
panels; the fifth retains a strong signal but fails its predeclared post-exclusion sample-size gate.
The earlier focal *Tadarida teniotis* endpoint test remains unsupported.

We then performed one final frozen audit of additive tag/device altitude bias. Every retained
session was translated to zero median before vertical binning, which removes any constant additive
altitude offset exactly. Five of six panels retain calibrated identity in vertical-distribution
shape after this transformation. Focal *Tadarida* is the explicit exception (p=0.5121), so its
absolute vertical-location component remains inseparable from possible device bias and its raw
256-m AGL translation is no longer presented as headline biological evidence.

Before opening stationary heights, an x-y/time-only structural gate identified only two panels
with sufficient shared stationary support for empirical altitude-offset correction. Both retain
calibrated identity after correction (p=0.0002 in each). Tracking-window overlap is substantial in
most panels but weaker in the 2016 *Phyllostomus* panel, which we retain as a temporal-context
limitation rather than opening another outcome-driven analysis family.

The analytical history is fully disclosed. An earlier conditional-versus-marginal architecture
claim was withdrawn after pipeline-specific null calibration showed that intuitive zero baselines
were invalid. Pairwise self-identification and absolute metre-scale separation were subsequently
calibrated against their own finite-sample nulls. The final tag-bias audit was also frozen before
output, and it is the declared stopping point for new scientific analyses before submission.

We believe the paper contributes both ecological and methodological insight: repeatable vertical
individual structure persists beyond coarse horizontal occupancy and, in five systems, beyond
additive altitude zero point; at the same time, prediction-based individuality statistics require
calibration of the complete analysis pipeline rather than assumed zero or 0.5 nulls.

All tracking data are publicly archived. No new animal handling was conducted.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
