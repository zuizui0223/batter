# Cover letter — Journal of Animal Ecology — v0.3.7

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Repeatable vertical identity in bat airspace persists after coarse horizontal occupancy is standardized,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

The paper asks a narrower question than whether animals differ in flight altitude. Vertical individuality is already well documented. Our question is whether individual identity still predicts vertical use after two simpler alternatives are removed: differences in coarse horizontal occupancy and additive altitude zero point.

Across six public three-dimensional bat tracking panels, identity-matched vertical profiles exceed panel-specific whole-session exchangeability expectations after self and conspecific profiles are evaluated under identical 5-km horizontal-cell weights. The strongest ecological result comes from the five comparative panels—*Eidolon helvum*, *Hypsignathus monstrosus* and three *Phyllostomus hastatus* datasets. All five retain calibrated individual identity in vertical-distribution shape after every session is translated to zero median, a transformation that removes any additive constant tag/device altitude offset exactly (p=0.0002–0.0076).

A separate outcome-blind stationary preflight identified only two comparative panels with sufficient shared stationary support for empirical altitude-offset correction. Both retain calibrated identity after that correction (p=0.0002 in each). A fixed endpoint-neighbourhood exclusion also retains calibrated identity in four of five comparative panels; the fifth, *Eidolon*, retains a strong signal but falls below its predeclared post-exclusion sample-size gate.

The motivating *Tadarida teniotis* dataset is deliberately treated as a boundary case rather than the centre of the comparative claim. Its repeatable absolute vertical-location identity does not survive session centering (p=0.5121), and its endpoint-neighbourhood test also fails. We therefore do not present its raw 256-m AGL translation as independent biological evidence.

The second contribution is methodological. The analysis showed that intuitive baselines were not universal: the original conditional-minus-marginal score had a negative finite-sample exchangeability expectation, pairwise self-identification nulls ranged from about 0.50 to 0.58, and an absolute separation statistic had a positive null mean. We therefore calibrate the complete prediction pipeline under biologically appropriate session-label exchangeability rather than treating zero or 0.5 as automatic nulls.

All robustness analyses were frozen before their outcomes were opened, failed tests are retained, and the final tag/device audit was declared in advance as the stopping point for new scientific analysis. Detailed amendment history is provided in Supporting Information rather than made the narrative focus of the manuscript.

All tracking data are publicly archived, and no new animal handling was conducted.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
