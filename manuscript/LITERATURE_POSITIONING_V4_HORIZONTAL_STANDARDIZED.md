# Literature positioning v4 — horizontal-standardized vertical identity

Checked: 2026-09-27

## Positioning

The paper must not claim that vertical individual specialization, repeated 3-D niche use, or
individual differences in flight altitude are new.

Relevant precedents already establish:

- persistent individual specialization in diving depth and behaviour (Woo et al. 2008);
- individual partitioning of foraging-depth niches and the possibility that depth differences
  arise from repeated use of horizontally distinct bathymetric patches (Ratcliffe et al. 2013);
- long-term individual fidelity to three-dimensional foraging habitats in southern elephant
  seals (McIntyre et al. 2017);
- individual effects on flight altitude in free-flying aerial insectivorous birds
  (Dreelin et al. 2018);
- the vertical niche as an established ecological dimension (Gámez & Harris 2022;
  Xing et al. 2023).

The novelty is therefore the **counterfactual control**, not the existence of vertical
individuality.

## Preferred novelty statement

> We test whether held-out individual identity remains predictive of vertical airspace use when
> self and other vertical profiles are evaluated under the same coarse horizontal occupancy
> distribution, and calibrate that prediction against a panel-specific whole-session
> exchangeability null.

Four components matter together:

1. **held-out identity transfer** rather than descriptive between-individual variance;
2. **common horizontal weighting** that removes differences in occupancy among the tested cells;
3. **whole-session permutation calibration** rather than assuming a zero finite-sample score null;
4. **closed multi-panel replication** across six datasets from four bat taxa.

## Closest conceptual precedent: depth versus patch choice

Ratcliffe et al. (2013) is particularly important because it articulates the same ecological
confound in a diving system: apparent individual specialization in depth can arise if individuals
repeatedly use patches with different bathymetry. The study also discusses systems in which
individuals differ in depth use over the same foraging patch.

The present bat analysis asks the airborne analogue at a coarse horizontal grain:
does individual vertical identity remain after differences in occupancy among horizontal cells
are removed?

This is a stronger ecological framing than the superseded “conditional-dominant versus
marginal-dominant architecture” classification.

## Necessary limitation

Common-cell weighting standardizes occupancy among 5-km cells; it does **not** make individuals
occupy identical continuous x-y positions within those cells.

Accordingly, use:

- “after horizontal occupancy among the tested cells is standardized”;
- “beyond coarse-grained horizontal occupancy at the tested scale”;
- “not reducible to differences in occupancy among tested cells alone”.

Avoid:

- “horizontal fidelity is completely controlled”;
- “independent of horizontal space use”;
- “pure vertical specialization”.

The focal *Tadarida* controls reinforce this boundary: AGL identity passes at 2.5 and 5 km but not
10 km, and the predeclared night-endpoint-neighbourhood exclusion fails. Fine-scale horizontal or
central-place structure therefore remains a viable contributor.

## Journal positioning

### Journal of Animal Ecology — first shot

The JAE argument is now ecological:

- individual specialization and population niche structure;
- an established vertical-niche literature;
- a direct test of the most natural ecological alternative explanation;
- replication across a closed set of bat tracking panels;
- retained negative focal controls that define the claim ceiling.

Lead with the biology, not estimator correction. Calibration belongs in Methods and in the
transparency paragraph explaining why the original architecture interpretation was superseded.

### Movement Ecology — backup

Movement Ecology remains an excellent backup because the work uses held-out 3-D movement
prediction, spatial-grain sensitivity and explicit movement-data calibration.

## Bottom line

> Vertical individual specialization is already known. The contribution here is evidence that
> repeatable vertical identity in free-flying bats survives explicit standardization of
> coarse horizontal occupancy in a held-out, pipeline-calibrated, closed multi-panel test.
