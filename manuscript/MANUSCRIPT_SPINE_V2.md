# Repeatable individual signatures in the vertical niche of European free-tailed bats

**Working target:** Journal of Animal Ecology  
**Alternative:** Movement Ecology  
**Status:** ecological manuscript spine v2

## Core question

Can a population-level vertical niche be descriptively thick while its fine organization remains partly individualized and therefore weakly transferable among conspecifics?

## Empirical starting point

The original frozen ODSP endpoint for *Tadarida teniotis* showed:

- H(Z|X,Y) = 1.39186 nats;
- 4.022 effective vertical states;
- both independently sealed bats had non-positive gain from the population-level location-conditioned vertical distribution.

Thus the population-level vertical representation was thick but did not transfer.

## New individual-identity analysis

Using all eight tracked bats, the same 18 frozen 5-km cells and the same fixed MSL-altitude bins were retained.

Each individual was split chronologically into early and late observations. Early individual maps were then scored against every individual's late observations.

### Result 1 — conditional vertical-use signatures are identity matched

The identity-matched diagonal mean gain over the population cell-specific vertical model was +0.16822 nats/event.

Across all 8! = 40,320 identity assignments:

- permutation null mean = -0.03666;
- 5th–95th percentile = -0.15655 to +0.07073;
- exact one-sided P = 0.000174.

Six of eight individuals had positive own-map gain over the population cell-specific model, and the own early map was the strict best of eight maps for five individuals.

This is strong evidence that individual identity carries repeatable information about later vertical-state use.

## Mechanism decomposition

The identity signal was then decomposed prospectively.

### Result 2 — no robust individual × location vertical interaction

After each individual's marginal altitude preference was absorbed into the baseline, adding an individual-specific cell-by-altitude interaction did not pass the frozen rule:

- diagonal residual gain = +0.0240;
- exact permutation P = 0.160;
- 5/8 individual residual gains positive.

Sensitivities:
- lambda=5: P=0.248;
- lambda=50: P=0.065.

Therefore the data do not support the stronger claim that each bat has a distinct, temporally stable location-specific vertical reaction map.

### Result 3 — altitude and horizontal identity signatures are relatively repeatable but do not independently beat the population baseline for most bats

Marginal altitude identity:
- matched diagonal mean gain relative to the species marginal = -0.0311;
- random identity assignments are substantially worse on average (null mean -0.2197);
- exact identity-assignment P = 0.0224;
- only 4/8 bats had positive diagonal gain over the species marginal.

Horizontal cell-use identity:
- matched diagonal mean gain relative to the species cell distribution = -0.2980;
- random identity assignments are much worse on average (null mean -1.2793);
- exact identity-assignment P = 0.00233;
- only 4/8 bats had positive diagonal gain over the species distribution.

Under the predeclared component support rule, neither component alone qualifies as a generally superior individual predictor.

## Ecological synthesis

The simplest interpretation is **partial individual specialization rather than eight isolated individual niches**.

Three levels coexist:

1. **Population thickness.** The population occupies a broad vertical state space.
2. **Identity repeatability.** Correctly matched early individual maps predict later conditional vertical states much better than maps assigned from other individuals.
3. **Population robustness.** The pooled population distribution remains a strong predictor and often outperforms an individual marginal or horizontal model.

The individual signature is therefore real but composite. It is not reducible to one stable altitude offset, one horizontal home-range distribution, or one individual-by-location vertical interaction.

This provides a plausible reason why the original population-level x-y-conditioned vertical map did not transfer cleanly to new bats: conspecifics are not fully exchangeable in how horizontal and vertical movement dimensions combine.

Because the follow-up uses the already-open original dataset, this mechanism interpretation is not an independent replication and should be tested in a new colony or tracking dataset before generalizing to the species.

## Connection to prior ecology

Existing bat studies have documented individual specialization in horizontal space use, foraging areas and habitat composition. The original *T. teniotis* study demonstrated predictable use of topography and nocturnal uplift for high-altitude ascents.

The present analysis adds a different level of organization: whether **an individual's vertical-state distribution can be transferred to another conspecific after horizontal position is represented explicitly**.

This places vertical movement within the individual-specialization framework without claiming that MSL altitude is height above ground or that individual differences identify behavioral causes.

## Proposed title options

1. **Population-level vertical niche structure masks repeatable individual signatures in European free-tailed bats**
2. **Repeatable individual signatures limit transfer of vertical niche structure among European free-tailed bats**
3. **A thick vertical niche is not an exchangeable niche: individual signatures in three-dimensional bat movement**

## Claim ceiling

Supported:
- population vertical thickness;
- failed species-average transfer from the prior frozen endpoint;
- strong identity-matched conditional prediction in the follow-up;
- lack of robust evidence for a pure individual-by-location vertical interaction;
- relative repeatability of altitude and horizontal identity components.

Not supported:
- eight fully distinct vertical strategies;
- stable individual personality;
- learning;
- causal resource partitioning;
- height above ground;
- adaptive value;
- universal species-level individual specialization.
