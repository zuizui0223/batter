# Literature positioning v1

## Closest ecological antecedents

### Kerches-Rogeri et al. 2020 — Journal of Animal Ecology

*Individual specialization in the use of space by frugivorous bats.*

This paper established that conspecific bats can differ substantially and consistently in horizontal space and habitat use. It is the closest conceptual precedent for treating individual bats as potentially occupying subsets of a population spatial niche.

Difference here:
- the focal added dimension is vertical state;
- the question is predictive identity transfer rather than a spatial-specialization index;
- horizontal cell is explicitly represented while vertical state is scored.

### Mortimer et al. 2023 — European Journal of Wildlife Research

*Foraging fidelity and individual specialisation in a temperate bat Myotis nattereri.*

This study showed repeated use of individual foraging areas and individual habitat specialization.

Difference here:
- our analysis asks whether an early 3D state map predicts the same individual later better than another individual's map;
- the focal issue is exchangeability of population-level vertical niche structure.

### Seasonal individual specialization in Ia io — Movement Ecology 2023

Work on great evening bats linked individual and population spatial-niche breadth across seasons.

Difference here:
- our state variable is vertical flight altitude, not only planar home-range breadth;
- we separate population thickness, identity matching and transferability.

### O'Mara et al. 2021 — Current Biology

*Bats use topography and nocturnal updrafts to fly high and fast.*

This is the source study/data architecture for the current *Tadarida teniotis* tracking data. It showed that topography and nocturnal uplift predict high-altitude ascents.

Our analysis must not compete with or overwrite that mechanism result. Instead it asks a downstream question that the original paper did not target:

> Are the resulting vertical-state patterns exchangeable among tracked individuals?

## Novelty statement

The novelty is not the general existence of individual specialization in bats.

The narrower contribution is:

> **individual specialization can be tested as cross-individual transferability of a vertical-state map, revealing a distinction between population-level vertical niche thickness and exchangeability of its organization among individuals.**

## Journal positioning

### Journal of Animal Ecology

Best fit if the manuscript leads with:
- individual specialization as population niche structure;
- within- versus among-individual heterogeneity;
- implications for representing animal niches with pooled tracking data.

Main desk risk:
- only eight individuals and one source population;
- sequential mechanism follow-up uses a previously analysed public dataset.

### Movement Ecology

Best fit if the manuscript leads with:
- three-dimensional movement;
- identity transfer matrices;
- repeatability versus exchangeability of movement-state distributions.

This is likely the safer first route if the paper remains tightly focused on the bat system.
