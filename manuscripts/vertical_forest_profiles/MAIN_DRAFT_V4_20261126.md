# Predictive value and limits of vertical forest profiles for recorded bat species sorting

**Internal manuscript V4 — 26 November 2026 JST.** Standalone research using our harmonized occurrence–trait–environment database. Not a calibrated 3D SDM or submission-ready manuscript. The response is recorded species-label allocation, not biological occupancy. The four core model analyses have passed a clean, checksum-verified replay. A distinct historical Danish airborne-laser structural comparison is now complete; the primary Dutch comparison remains unestablished. Authorship, bibliography, licensing, visual review and independent biological validation remain release considerations.

## Abstract

Forest cover and canopy height compress vertical habitat architecture into scalar descriptors. We asked whether relative vertical return-height information improves species-specific prediction beyond these descriptors and whether the gain transfers across sampling contexts. We assembled a bat occurrence, wing-trait, climate, land-cover and remotely predicted height-profile database on a common equal-area 1-km grid. A conditional comparison of two recorded species labels and two observed cells within a provider, year and 200-km block avoids interpreting unrecorded labels as absences. Among 14 taxa, adding relative median return height to species-specific baseline slopes improved block-held-out log score by 0.01440 nats per composite comparison in all five folds. A stricter provider-and-block holdout retained a gain of 0.01297. A fixed August 2026 GBIF snapshot yielded +0.00984 in all five folds, but 47/50 evaluated blocks overlapped the pilot and a prospective novel-block check lacked support. A provider- and geographically-disjoint northern sample yielded −0.00259 for known species, where the structural product was also extrapolative. Trait-only prediction to held-out taxa did not establish a transferable profile-specific benefit, even with combined flight and acoustic descriptors. In a separate historical Danish airborne-laser comparison (375 cells, 31 spatial blocks), the profile proxy correlated with the measured all-return mean-height/vegetation-P95 ratio (r = 0.498) and reduced held-out ratio RMSE by 0.00364 at the predeclared 70% common-area threshold; the gain was negative under a 90% support sensitivity. Thus vertical environmental information improved recorded-label prediction within the sampled domain but did not establish transferable habitat filtering or actual three-dimensional distribution.

## 1. Introduction

Equal tree cover and canopy height need not imply equal vertical forest structure. Relative vegetation height may therefore supply information lost when habitat is described only on a horizontal plane. Flying animals are a useful system in which to distinguish scalar habitat descriptors, vertical environmental descriptors and actual use of airspace. Existing aerial-niche, bat habitat-use, lidar and trait-environment research is already substantial (initial prior-art anchors in GVSM_AMENDMENT_V1.md and PROSPECTIVE_CONTRACT_V1.md). The contribution sought here is not the invention of vertical remote sensing or the assertion that bat habitat use differs among species. It is the construction of a joint database and an out-of-sample contrast that separates additional vertical information, trait-based sharing and the boundary of model transfer.

Presence-only data conflate ecological use and observation. We therefore condition on recorded labels and their observed locations rather than treat an unreported taxon as absent. Common source and time support reduces some sampling differences but does not prove identical detection. We ask (i) whether vertical information improves blocked allocation of recorded species labels beyond climate, cover and height; (ii) whether the improvement persists after withholding providers and changing the source snapshot; and (iii) whether it transfers to a disjoint northern sample or to taxa excluded from training. The initial pilot was exploratory; later snapshot and northern validation rules were frozen before their outcomes. The original 20-taxon native-PAVD SDM remains nonadmitted.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Independent data construction

Initial live-search data: 26 completely retrieved physical-trait taxa in [−12°,35°,30°,51.6°], without a country filter, including neighboring non-European land. The 20,784 records represented 4,854 species-cell combinations; coordinate uncertainty >0 and ≤500 m, observed detection, observation record types, 17 April 2019–16 March 2023. One high-volume taxon and one count failure were excluded from the completed acquisition, not treated as absent. Source query and eligibility receipts preserve these limits.

Fixed source: the official experimental GBIF Darwin Core Archive Parquet snapshot of 1 August 2026 (reported source DOI 10.15468/dl.8yrbe7). The order Chiroptera was extracted from eight inventoried Mammalia remainder objects, with the frozen country whitelist, [−12°,35°,32°,71°] bounds, precision/date/record-type/geospatial filters and parseable species name, snapshot key and GBIF ID. All 219,960 extracted records have distinct GBIF IDs; 39 archived names, 240 providers, 23,489 species-cell combinations, 11,006 distinct cells. These names are not an independently verified regional fauna. The new alphanumeric snapshot keys are not joined directly to old numeric GBIF keys. Use a one-to-one exact-name bridge, excluding ambiguous cases; unresolved taxonomy remains explicit. The August and November occurrence archives are never pooled. This is a source DOI, not a newly minted DOI for our extraction.

Physical AR, WL and mass came from doi:10.5281/zenodo.7226367; compiled call-frequency geometric midpoint, mean duration and the exploratory maternity-roost category use the verified physical/EuroBaTrait manifests (doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.21777161.v2). No outcome-based trait imputation; acoustic midpoint is not peak frequency. Previously modelled habitat affinities are excluded from functional-trait predictors.

### 2.2 Environment and observation support

GVSM 2020 predicted mean return-height quantiles RH10,25,50,75,90,98 (doi:10.31223/X5M22F) are area-weighted on EPSG:6933 zero-origin 1-km cells using their common finite native-pixel support (≥95%). Negative low quantiles are retained. The product's 51.6°N GEDI training-latitude boundary distinguishes wholly southern and wholly northern cells; crossing cells are excluded in side-specific tests. This is a latitude-support proxy, not proof of unbiased southern predictions. CHELSA v2.1 1981–2010 bio1,4,5,6,12,15 use declared source conversions and common area-weighted coverage. ESA WorldCover 2021 v200 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.7254221) uses equal-area source-pixel center assignment; accept 95–105% valid cell area. Forest support requires tree fraction ≥0.3, finite ordered quantiles and RH98>5 m. The vertical descriptor R50 = area-mean RH50 / area-mean RH98 is not measured foliage density, open-air volume or bat flight height.

Pilot integration yielded 2,980 common environmental cells, 1,850 forest cells, 8,811 forest records and 2,977 species-cells. Fourteen taxa met the fixed geographic gate of ≥30 unique cells and ≥4 separate 200-km blocks each containing ≥5 cells. Fixed-source integration yielded 10,983 common cells, 6,775 forest cells, 56,847 forest records and 14,124 species-cells for the target group. Twenty exact-name trait taxa passed the all-latitude forest geographic gate; only 16 within the training latitude.

Fixed source metadata: 127,033 records have sampling-protocol text, 143,476 event identifiers; establishment field missing throughout. Protocol/remarks/behavior screening flagged 684 roost-context records and zero explicit captive records. Later fixed tests subtract flagged roost records from grouped counts, retaining labels with ≥1 unflagged record. Flags are incomplete; unflagged records are not necessarily wild or flight observations. Earlier pilot has no comparable record-level screen. No claim of calibrated survey effort.

### 2.3 Conditional recorded-label prediction

For each dataset-year-200km-block stratum, deduplicate species-cell labels, ignoring record multiplicities. For each unordered species pair s,t, take all pairs of recorded cells x,y bearing the respective labels, excluding x=y. Cells with both labels may contribute both orientations. Deterministically sample up to 128 comparisons per species-pair-stratum uniformly without replacement (seed 20261125). Equal total block weight, equal group weights within blocks, equal comparison weights within groups. Comparisons share underlying observations and are not independent biological replicates.

Given the observed labels and cells, the orientation log odds is Delta = f_s(x) − f_t(x) − f_s(y) + f_t(y). The composite log score is log sigmoid(Delta). Neutral B0 score = −log(2). B1 uses six climate variables, five land-cover fractions (tree, shrub, grass, cropland, built-up) and RH98, with species slopes parameterized along standardized AR plus centered residual slopes orthogonal to AR. B2 adds species-specific R50 slopes. B3 instead parameterizes the R50 slopes along AR plus orthogonal residuals. B2 and B3 span identical species contrasts; their difference reflects different ridge shrinkage, not a distinct larger response space. Use training-only feature scaling, fixed ridge 0.1 times squared coefficient norm, analytic gradients and L-BFGS-B. The pilot numerical checks and all reported pilot/snapshot fold optimizations passed. Composite scores are not an unconditional occurrence likelihood.

### 2.4 Identifiable prediction contrasts

The score is invariant to adding a species intercept or a cell-specific term common to all species: both vanish in the double difference. A vertical-profile predictor that contributes identically to all species also cancels. Consequently, the incremental profile score tests differential recorded-label associations rather than a universal improvement in cell suitability.

For an idealized independent-cell label model, let p_s(x) be the marginal probability of a recorded label. The expected orientation weight ratio is p_s(x)p_t(y)/[p_t(x)p_s(y)]. If p_s(x)=A_s B(x) U_s(x), with positive factors yielding valid probabilities, the ratio depends only on the biological component U. This separability is not established by matching provider, year and region. In particular, deduplicating repeated detections creates an at-least-one-record response; even separable Poisson recording intensities can yield nonseparable label probabilities. The ratio of expected orientation weights is an interpretation under stated assumptions, not a joint likelihood for the weighted and capped composite sample. See [Supplement S1](SUPPLEMENT_S1_OBSERVATION_IDENTIFIABILITY.md) for the exact invariance, counterexample and altitude identifiability limits.

### 2.5 Validation

Pilot: five fixed outer block folds (hash-sorted 200-km block keys, round robin). Preserve the frozen combined B3−B1 screen and separate B2−B1/B3−B2 contrasts. The profile increment became the paper's focus after pilot evaluation. Post-pilot diagnostics independently hash providers into five folds, exclude all training observations from either held-out provider or held-out block fold, and score supported intersections; separately hold out both species and the spatial fold to evaluate trait-only prediction. W denotes AR, log WL and log mass; WA adds log call-frequency midpoint and log duration; WAR adds maternity-roost category. Complete-case subsets and unknown categories are reported rather than silently pooled.

Fixed snapshot A: same 14 eligible pilot taxa, screened source, within training latitude, identical predictors and five block folds. Frozen descriptive screen: positive overall B2−B1, ≥4/5 positive folds and all single-block omission means positive. B: compare genuinely new blocks relative to the exact pilot pair construction, requiring ≥10 test blocks and ≥10 test taxa with training support; stop without estimation if unsupported.

Northern C: train only on old pilot, test only wholly northern fixed-source cells outside BOTH the entire prior archive's providers and blocks. Known-species model evaluates pairs with both taxa in training. Cold-start models exclude both target taxa from old training and use the same complete-case comparisons for AR, W and WA. Conditional test-block bootstrap resamples blocks with trained models fixed; interval is descriptive, not population-level inference. Changes in structural extrapolation, geography and observation cannot be separated.

### 2.6 Independent historical environmental comparison

The separately frozen EcoDes-DK15 analysis (Assmann et al. 2022, DOI 10.5194/essd-14-823-2022; data DOI 10.5281/zenodo.5752926) uses 2014–2015 ALS at 10 m, not contemporaneous 2020 measurements. All 909 existing forest-profile-supported candidate cells in the specified Danish bounding rectangle were selected before examining ALS values. Actual source tile extent, land masks, absence of building returns, finite physical-value rules and joint native support delimit the valid comparison. Five source archive MD5 checks passed; clipped native tiles are indexed by actual transforms and extents, and conflicting overlaps are excluded. Preserve negative valid normalized mean heights and zero valid canopy heights at native resolution. GDAL area-average aggregation maps shared valid areas onto the common EPSG:6933 1-km cells. Retain area-mean vegetation P95 greater than 5 m and at least 70% shared valid area (50% and 90% sensitivity). The distinct ALS response is ratio of mean all-return normalized height to mean vegetation P95 (not the GVSM waveform-energy quantile ratio). The secondary openness measure is ground/water return fraction. Pearson and Spearman correlations are descriptive. Five hashed 50-km block folds compare a ridge model (alpha = 1) of the ALS ratio on GVSM RH98 and tree fraction with an otherwise identical model adding GVSM R50. Train-only scaling and the same rows within each comparison are retained. The RMSE is cell-weighted, not the bat-model block-weighted composite score. This is a northern-only historical environmental check, not a biological replicate or a direct south/north comparison.

## 4. Discussion

The principal descriptive result is a small, repeatable within-domain improvement in recorded-label prediction after adding relative return-height information beyond climate, land cover and scalar height. It survived a stricter provider-and-block holdout and a changed, substantially overlapping source snapshot. That information did not survive disjoint northern transfer. The contrast is a limit of this fitted prediction system, not identification of a causal latitude threshold. Ecology, observation and extrapolation of the structural product change simultaneously. The Danish historical comparison provides limited evidence that the extrapolative profile proxy retains some environmental information in the north, despite the failed northern bat-label transfer. Its 90%-support sensitivity, historical timing and different return metrics preclude using it to partition the causes of failed transfer.

Flight, acoustic sensing, roost and resource access may influence differential vertical habitat responses. However, AR sharing did not improve the within-domain model and the profile-specific benefit did not persist for trait-based cold start in the north. Acoustic traits may also predict detectability. We do not attribute any predictive differences to hearing, flight cost, competition or a realized niche mechanism.

The exact intercept invariance of the score is useful for comparing species-differential recorded associations. It does not establish that the observation process itself is separable. In particular, removing duplicate records can retain detection saturation effects (Supplement S1). Shared dataset-year-block support is not equal observation effort. The observed intensity is a product of biological use and a species- and context-specific observation process; fixing labels and locations does not identify either factor. Our outcome is a conditional, composite predictive score on observed labels, not occurrence probability, habitat selection, abundance or biological coexistence. The negative northern gain and stopped novel-block gate are substantively different: the former is evaluated performance, the latter lack of support.

A vertically resolved environmental predictor is not a vertically resolved distribution. For any horizontal intensity Lambda_s(x), infinitely many normalized altitude distributions q_s(z|x) give lambda_s(x,z)=Lambda_s(x)q_s(z|x) with the same marginal. The present study therefore does not establish flight-height probability or a 3D functional niche. The old tracking/feeding-mark results motivate this distinction but are not pooled here. Independent structural measurements and height-specific detection, plus terrain, roost and effort controls, would be needed to bridge these estimands.

### 4.1 Environmental-proxy validation boundary

A prospective independent environmental comparison was defined before any AHN4 structural outcomes using Dutch airborne laser-scanning metrics (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.15261042). The fixed candidate selection contains 636 previously supported 1-km forest cells in the Netherlands bounding rectangle (318 wholly within the GVSM GEDI training latitude, 312 wholly north, six boundary cells). The comparison targets correspondence of GVSM relative return height with a distinct ALS-derived height ratio, not exact waveform-energy or PAVD calibration. The first run 45599899517 stopped with HTTP 500 during public mask archive member retrieval; it produced no matched measurements or structural effect. A secondary attempt using the original 2023 release (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.8422129) verified the full revised mask archive by MD5 and both required mask members by ZIP CRC, but timed out before retrieving any metric values. This is an acquisition failure, not a negative structural validation. The primary repair (45601066021) retrieved all five required raster members with checked CRCs, but stopped on a noninteger source-grid origin offset before producing structural metrics. A geometry-only correction (45622383530) passed seven synthetic checks and retrieved identical member hashes, then stopped because a hardcoded expected mask hash was inadvertently truncated. The correction now reads the complete expected hashes directly from the verified acquisition receipt. Runs 45642032490 and 45642146713 were initiated on the corrected path; no admitted Dutch structural metrics are available at this revision. These are engineering failures, not ecological or structural nulls. See [transport stop and repair](../../prospective/gedi_trait_macro_sdm/AHN4_TRANSPORT_STOP_RECEIPT_V1.md). Even successful proxy validation would not establish the biological cause of the failed north transfer.
