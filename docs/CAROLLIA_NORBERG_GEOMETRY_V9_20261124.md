## Conditional theorem: relative wind vulnerability is not absolute accessibility
With constant m and area S, fixed coefficients and common positive feasible speed set, increasing span reduces A and leaves B unchanged. Pointwise P at every speed decreases. Thus independently minimizing round-trip leg energy over speeds, for fixed E, cannot reduce absolute accessible radius in any direction in this reduced model, regardless of uniform steady wind. Verified numerically in JS on 361 headings, speed 5–7 m/s (2001 grid speeds), w=4, P. nathusii inputs, +20% span: absolute radius ratio 1.07578–1.10660 (exact calculation max 1.1068166). This is conditional mathematical dominance, not empirical individual evidence; wing span changes may affect feasibility, maneuverability, drag, physiology or speed policy in real animals.
If instead each individual is forced to maintain its own calm maximum-range speed u0=(A/B)^.25, then u0 ∝ span^-1/2 and calm radius ∝ span. The absolute wind area at same E is proportional to span²(1-c*span)^(3/2) for c*span=(w/u0)^2<1. Differentiation yields the interior maximum at (w/u0)^2=4/7, i.e. w/u0≈.756. Relative area declines with span under this fixed calm-speed rule. Any apparent absolute reversal is conditional on that restrictive speed policy and disappears under the shared feasible-speed optimization assumptions above. This distinguishes true absolute accessibility from the fraction of calm accessibility. No empirical claim, no new study submission, no merge.
