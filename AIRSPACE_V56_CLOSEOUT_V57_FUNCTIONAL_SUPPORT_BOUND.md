Let P be an explicitly fixed reference distribution of recorded movement opportunities, F the latent relative rate distribution of target functional events on exactly that support (conditional on effort/behavior definitions), and Q the expected detected-event distribution. Assume correct spatial/temporal attribution, no false positives or inter-cell localization, and Q_j=q_j F_j/sum q_k F_k, strictly positive q_j on support with max(q)/min(q)<=Gamma, Gamma>=1. Reference P must itself be appropriate/known; an observed movement distribution is not population true occupancy. If marks can arise from unrelated animals, missing support q=0, or location errors, the bounds below do not apply directly.
For any fixed subset S with q=Q(S), f=F(S), p=P(S), odds(q)=odds(f)*E_F[detection|S]/E_F[detection|S^c]. The conditional means ratio belongs to [1/Gamma,Gamma]. Thus sharp lower L=q/(q+Gamma*(1-q)), upper U=Gamma*q/(Gamma*q+1-q). For p,q in (0,1), robust strict enrichment L>p iff odds(q)/odds(p)>Gamma; robust depletion U<p iff odds(p)/odds(q)>Gamma. Gamma is sensitivity parameter, not estimated from V48/49 or V51/54. For q=0,1 and finite positive bounded detection the endpoints are point-identified in the population idealization, not finite-sample proof of absence. Bounds attained by two-group constant detection ratios with probabilities <=1. General pairwise rate ordering also requires observed odds-ratio contrast above Gamma when compared on the same fixed reference support. A separate sharp bound on total variation TV(F,Q)<=(sqrt(Gamma)-1)/(sqrt(Gamma)+1) can be proved by maximizing the two-group difference over q or f.
