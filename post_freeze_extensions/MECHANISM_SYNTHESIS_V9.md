## 2. What the original archive localizes mechanistically

These are post-freeze mechanism-localization/stress-test results, not independent replications.

### Behavioural/kinematic mixture

Identity remains after:
- broad speed-state conditioning: **5/5** comparative panels;
- speed × turning conditioning: **5/5**.

Thus different fractions of time in broad kinematic states are insufficient as a general explanation.

### Horizontal route/patch scale

Where cross-individual support permits, identity remains after:
- 2-km place × state: **4/4**;
- 500-m place × state: **4/4**;
- 250-m place × state: **3/3**.

A common 100-m test is structurally unavailable.

Thus the unresolved spatial mechanism is pushed below roughly 250–500 m in the evaluable systems.

### Temporal persistence

Identity remains with self-history separated from the target by:
- >=1 day: **4/4**;
- >=3 days: **3/3**;
- >=7 days: **2/2**.

This supports a stable multi-day component and, in two systems, a week-scale component. It does not identify memory, personality or genetic determination.

### Broad shared night context

At 2-km place × state, same-individual history remains more informative than same-night other individuals in **3/4** evaluable panels.

Thus broad calendar-night context is not a sufficient general explanation.

## Provisional general principle

The evidence now points to a distribution-level principle rather than one simple mechanism:

> **Individuals can carry persistent information in how they organize vertical space that is not reducible to coarse place occupancy or broad movement state, and in several systems persists across sub-kilometre spatial matching and multiple days.**

The mechanism may be generated at finer spatial/resource/environmental scales or by stable individual traits and learned responses. The final source-RSF attribution determines whether measured roost/habitat allocation accounts for a detectable share of the Nyctalus signal.
