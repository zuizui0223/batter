# Integrated figure and manuscript patch plan v1

## Design goal

The integrated paper must read as one ecological argument, not a frozen manuscript followed by a post-freeze appendix.

Core sequence:

**phenomenon -> confound control -> mechanism localization -> external boundary -> ecological hypothesis**

## Manuscript section transformation

### Abstract
Replace “all five comparative panels retain shape” as the endpoint with a four-stage abstract:

1. confound problem;
2. original centered-shape support;
3. post-freeze localization plus external heterogeneity;
4. resource-anchoring × vertical-opportunity hypothesis explicitly marked as generated, not confirmed.

### Introduction
Delete the current ending that makes state conditioning only a future study.

Instead frame two questions:
1. does centered vertical individuality survive spatial/device alternatives?
2. once detected, is it a general property of bat movement or does its expression vary among ecological systems?

Do not introduce the resource-guild answer as an a priori hypothesis.

### Methods
Use explicit chronology subsections:

1. original closed comparative screen;
2. original confirmatory estimator/audits;
3. post-freeze mechanism-localization family;
4. prospective external programmes;
5. Pteropus terrain diagnostic;
6. post-hoc ecological synthesis and its non-inferential status.

### Results
Order by evidential function, not chronology of branch creation.

1. original phenomenon;
2. centered-shape boundary;
3. mechanism localization;
4. external prospective heterogeneity;
5. Pteropus terrain audit;
6. descriptive ecological split.

### Discussion
Replace the current “comparative generality is encouraging but limited” section.

New Discussion order:
1. genuine vertical individuality exists after obvious confounds;
2. it is not a universal bat property;
3. where present, broad context allocation is insufficient;
4. ecological opportunity may determine whether stable individuality can be expressed;
5. resource anchoring × vertical opportunity is the leading generated prediction;
6. limitations/competing predictors;
7. prospective test design.

## Main-text wording rules

Use:
- “recurrent but heterogeneous”;
- “supported in the original comparative archive”;
- “prospective external FAIL/PASS under its frozen design”;
- “post-hoc ecological contrast”;
- “generated prediction”.

Avoid:
- “replicated but heterogeneous” as a title-level summary;
- “configurable spatial individuality” as explanatory principle;
- “plant-feeding species show…” without unique-taxon and confound caveats;
- “1/4 external replication rate”;
- “resource anchoring explains the pattern”.
