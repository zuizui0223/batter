# Spatial-solution geometry synthesis v1

## Generated synthesis

The new 3D-overlap analysis suggests a more specific ecological hypothesis than "ecological opportunity allows individuality":

> **The geometry of individual specialization should be predictable from the geometry of the ecological problem.**

Individual specialization can be expressed in different spatial dimensions. Persistent resource use does not necessarily imply one generic form of individuality. Instead, ecological constraints should determine whether stable individual differences are expressed primarily through:

1. **horizontal patch selection**;
2. **vertical configuration within shared patches**;
3. **both dimensions in a nested hierarchy**; or
4. weak/non-repeatable spatial geometry.

This synthesis was generated after the current 3D-overlap results and is not a confirmatory inference.

## Current empirical pattern: nested spatial specialization

In every structurally evaluable panel, repeated sessions of the same individual overlap more strongly in horizontal space than sessions of different individuals:

- *Hypsignathus*: self O_XY 0.619 vs other 0.322
- *P. hastatus* 2022: 0.549 vs 0.334
- *P. hastatus* 2023: 0.547 vs 0.202
- *P. hastatus* 2016: 0.511 vs 0.208

Thus a first layer of individuality is horizontal: individuals repeatedly reuse different portions of the landscape.

But this does not exhaust the structure. Within pairwise-shared 500-m cells, the same individual's centered vertical configuration is also more similar across sessions than the configuration of other individuals:

- *Hypsignathus*: self O_Z|XY 0.727 vs other 0.556
- *P. hastatus* 2022: 0.832 vs 0.766
- *P. hastatus* 2023: 0.834 vs 0.779
- *P. hastatus* 2016: 0.703 vs 0.558

All four satisfy the pre-specified identity-permutation criterion.

The current geometry is therefore consistent with **nested spatial specialization**:

horizontal patch reuse
→ residual shared space
→ repeatable individual-specific vertical configuration within that shared space.

This is not evidence that individuals intentionally avoid competitors. It is a geometric decomposition of realized individual niches.

## Two distinct forms of vertical individuality

The overlap decomposition also separates two phenomena that should not be conflated.

### A. Vertical solution fidelity

The same animal repeatedly uses a similar vertical configuration within horizontally shared space.

Measured by positive:
D = self O_Z|XY - other O_Z|XY.

Supported in all four structurally evaluable panels.

### B. Vertical niche segregation

Adding the vertical axis makes different individuals spatially more separated than repeated sessions of the same individual.

A descriptive indicator is:
Delta_R = other R_3D - self R_3D,
where R_3D is the fraction of horizontal overlap lost when height is added.

Current values:
- *Hypsignathus*: +0.103
- *P. hastatus* 2016: +0.130
- *P. hastatus* 2022: -0.017
- *P. hastatus* 2023: -0.004

Thus *Hypsignathus* and 2016 show the clearest present signature of actual added vertical segregation. The 2022 and 2023 panels instead show strong vertical solution fidelity without a large additional loss of raw spatial overlap.

This distinction prevents "repeatability" from being automatically relabelled as "partitioning".

## Ecology-to-geometry prediction

A stronger future hypothesis is that natural history predicts **which geometry of specialization should occur**.

### 1. Persistent, shared, vertically structured resource

Examples: repeatedly used feeding sites or social/resource patches that multiple individuals can access at different heights/routes.

Prediction:
- appreciable between-individual horizontal overlap;
- positive vertical solution fidelity D;
- potentially positive Delta_R if individuals also segregate vertically.

Expected geometry:
**vertical partitioning within shared space**.

### 2. Persistent but spatially divisible/exclusive resource patches

Individuals can repeatedly monopolize or specialize on different patches/feeding sites.

Prediction:
- strong self horizontal fidelity;
- low between-individual O_XY;
- vertical partitioning may be difficult to observe because little fine-scale space is shared.

Expected geometry:
**primarily horizontal partitioning**.

The 500-m structural failure of *Eidolon* is compatible with, but does not confirm, this geometry.

### 3. Surface-constrained feeding

The same resource problem may recur, but usable height is constrained near one surface.

Prediction:
- horizontal specialization can occur;
- conditional vertical solution fidelity should be weak;
- Delta_R should be near zero.

Expected geometry:
**planar/horizontal specialization with little vertical niche dimension**.

*Myotis vivesi* is the motivating future test, not current confirmation of this 3D-overlap prediction.

### 4. Mobile or ephemeral prey

The location and vertical layer of the task change through time.

Prediction:
- stable within-individual vertical geometry should weaken;
- any individual spatial fidelity may occur in routes, roost approaches or landscape use rather than a reusable prey-centred 3D solution.

Expected geometry:
**weak or context-dependent vertical strategy reuse**.

*Tadarida* and *Nyctalus* motivate this prediction but do not establish it.

## General ecological claim generated here

The strongest general idea is no longer:

"ecological opportunity produces individual specialization."

It is:

> **Ecological strategy may determine the spatial dimensionality and geometry through which individual specialization is expressed.**

A population can therefore contain:
- horizontal individual niches,
- vertical individual niches within shared horizontal space,
- nested horizontal-plus-vertical niches,
- or little stable spatial niche geometry.

This makes the hypothesis falsifiable at the level of spatial shape, not merely presence/absence of individuality.

## What would establish food/resource partitioning

To move from "nested spatial specialization" to resource partitioning, future data must connect 3D geometry to resource identity or competitor exposure.

Strong tests include:
- identified feeding trees/resources attached to fixes;
- individual-by-resource matrices;
- simultaneous tracking showing whether vertical separation increases during co-use of the same patch;
- diet/metabarcoding or direct prey identity;
- resource depletion or competitor-density effects;
- experimental manipulation of resource availability.

Only these can distinguish passive individual routines from competition-mediated partitioning.

## Current claim ceiling

Current 3D results support:
- strong horizontal individual fidelity in evaluable panels;
- repeatable vertical configuration within pairwise-shared 500-m cells;
- a nested horizontal-plus-vertical description of spatial individuality;
- stronger descriptive vertical segregation in *Hypsignathus* and *P. hastatus* 2016.

They do not establish:
- intentional avoidance;
- competition-driven niche partitioning;
- resource or diet partitioning;
- adaptive divergence;
- ecology-to-geometry prediction as a confirmed cross-system law.
