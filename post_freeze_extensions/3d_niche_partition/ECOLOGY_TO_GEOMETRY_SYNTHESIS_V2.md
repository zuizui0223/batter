# Ecology-to-geometry synthesis v2

## Why v2 is needed

The first generated hypothesis focused on whether the same ecological task recurs and permits multiple reusable vertical solutions.

The 3D-overlap decomposition now shows that this is incomplete.

A population can show strong realized separation in x-y-z space even when there is no stable individual-specific vertical configuration relative to the local terrain. The Pteropus terrain diagnostic demonstrates this directly.

Therefore the stronger ecological question is not simply:

> When does vertical individuality appear?

It is:

> **Which component of spatial geometry carries individual specialization, and can that component be predicted from the ecological strategy and physical embedding of the task?**

## Three-stage ecological decomposition

### Stage 1. Ecological task selects landscape elements

Individuals may repeatedly use particular:
- feeding patches;
- feeding trees;
- social sites;
- commuting corridors;
- ridges, valleys or coastlines;
- water surfaces or other physical interfaces.

This produces horizontal or fine-scale landscape fidelity.

Metric family:
- H = self O_XY - other O_XY.

A positive H indicates repeated individual-specific landscape reuse.

### Stage 2. The selected landscape is embedded in three-dimensional physical space

The same horizontal ecological choice can inherit a vertical coordinate from:
- terrain elevation;
- canopy height;
- bathymetry;
- cliff geometry;
- water-surface elevation;
- built structure.

Thus horizontal ecological specialization can become realized 3D niche segregation **without a distinct behavioural preference for height relative to the local substrate**.

Pteropus is the current demonstration:
- native MSL H = +0.221
- native MSL V = +0.282
- native MSL S = +0.280
- after DEM subtraction H remains +0.221 but V becomes -0.040 and S -0.042.

The strong realized 3D geometry therefore follows fine-scale topographic landscape use rather than a stable terrain-relative vertical overlap geometry.

### Stage 3. Residual vertical placement can add another niche dimension

After matching horizontal location and, where possible, removing the local physical surface, individuals may still differ in:
- height above ground;
- height above canopy or water;
- approach height;
- flight layer;
- route geometry;
- vertical response to atmospheric or social conditions.

This is the genuinely residual vertical-strategy component.

The original centered-shape and common-place analyses establish that this component can exist in several source systems, but the new terrain result shows that it cannot be assumed from raw x-y-z niche separation alone.

## Revised geometry variables

### H — landscape reuse

H = self O_XY - other O_XY.

Question:
Does an individual repeatedly use the same horizontal/landscape portions more than conspecifics do?

### V_abs — realized vertical geometry in geographic coordinates

V_abs = self O_Z|XY - other O_Z|XY using native centered altitude.

Question:
Within horizontally shared cells, does absolute z geometry repeat by individual?

This can contain both topographic embedding and residual vertical behavior.

### V_rel — substrate-relative vertical geometry

V_rel is the same overlap contrast after subtracting a locally relevant physical surface, such as DEM terrain.

Question:
After the selected landscape's inherited vertical coordinate is removed, does individual-specific vertical geometry remain?

### S — added realized 3D segregation

S = other R_3D - self R_3D.

Positive S means adding z separates different individuals more than repeated sessions of the same individual.

S is descriptive and may be terrain-mediated.

## Current empirical geometry

### Hypsignathus monstrosus

- H +0.297
- V_abs +0.171
- S +0.103
- primary 3D geometry supported.

Interpretation:
nested realized spatial specialization. Terrain-relative decomposition is not currently available, so do not assign the V_abs component uniquely to flight-height strategy.

### Phyllostomus hastatus 2022

- H +0.216
- V_abs +0.066
- S -0.017
- primary supported.

Interpretation:
strong patch reuse plus weaker but repeatable vertical solution fidelity; little evidence that adding z creates additional raw pairwise segregation.

### Phyllostomus hastatus 2023

- H +0.344
- V_abs +0.054
- S -0.004
- primary supported.

Interpretation:
same qualitative geometry as 2022: strong horizontal individuality and smaller repeatable vertical configuration, not strong added raw vertical segregation.

### Phyllostomus hastatus 2016

- H +0.302
- V_abs +0.145
- S +0.130
- primary supported.

Interpretation:
nested realized specialization with substantial added z segregation, but temporal overlap is weak in this historical panel and terrain-relative geometry is unresolved.

### Myotis vivesi

- H -0.003
- V_abs +0.036
- S +0.022
- primary p = 0.055, not supported.

Interpretation:
near-planar / weak stable geometry, consistent with a mobile marine prey system constrained near the ocean surface. The positive V direction prevents claiming complete absence of vertical information.

### Pteropus poliocephalus

Native MSL:
- H +0.221
- V_abs +0.282
- S +0.280
- strongly supported.

Terrain-relative proxy:
- H +0.221
- V_rel -0.040
- S_rel -0.042
- not supported.

Interpretation:
strong individualized realized 3D niches generated largely by repeated use of different fine-scale topographic settings. This source is the clearest warning that **3D niche partitioning is not synonymous with vertical behavioural partitioning**.

### Tadarida teniotis and Nyctalus noctula

Both are structurally non-evaluable under the fixed 500-m primary gate.

Their descriptive H/V values may be shown as open points, but neither can be used as a test of the geometry hypothesis.

### Eidolon helvum

No biological individual satisfies the fixed 500-m pair-support structure; no geometry point is inferred.

## Revised ecology-to-geometry predictions

### A. Persistent resources distributed among distinct landscape elements

Prediction:
- strong H;
- realized 3D segregation may arise automatically if landscape elements differ in terrain/canopy elevation;
- V_abs may be strong while V_rel is weak.

This is a **landscape-embedded 3D niche**.

Pteropus currently exemplifies this geometry.

### B. Shared persistent resource with multiple within-resource solutions

Prediction:
- horizontal overlap among individuals remains appreciable;
- V_rel or a suitable resource-relative vertical metric is repeatably individual-specific;
- potentially positive S after the physical substrate is removed.

This would provide the strongest evidence for true vertical partitioning within a shared resource.

The current archive suggests this possibility but does not yet establish it at an identified shared resource.

### C. Persistent but horizontally exclusive/divisible resource patches

Prediction:
- strong H;
- low between-individual O_XY;
- fine-scale vertical partitioning may be statistically unavailable because individuals rarely share the same local space.

This can create horizontal specialization without an evaluable within-patch vertical question.

### D. Surface-constrained feeding

Prediction:
- residual V_rel should be small because the task restricts usable distance from the surface;
- specialization, if present, should be primarily horizontal or temporal.

Myotis provides a currently consistent but borderline example.

### E. Mobile or ephemeral prey

Prediction:
- geometry should be context-dependent because the ecological problem itself moves;
- stable V_rel across sessions should weaken unless individuals repeatedly exploit recurring landscape or atmospheric structures.

## Stronger general hypothesis

The generated principle is now:

> **Ecological strategy should predict not merely the magnitude of individual specialization, but the spatial layer in which it is encoded: landscape selection, environment-embedded 3D position, or residual vertical behavior.**

This is less trivial than "more options allow more individuality".

It predicts a decomposition of individual niches.

Two populations can show equally strong 3D separation for entirely different ecological reasons:
- one because individuals repeatedly use different hills, trees or bathymetric patches;
- another because individuals share the same landscape element but repeatedly use different vertical layers within it.

Those mechanisms make different predictions under terrain/substrate correction.

## Connection to resource partitioning

The current results support **nested spatial specialization**, not yet competition-mediated resource partitioning.

To establish resource partitioning, the geometry must be tied to identified resources:
- same feeding tree / feeding patch used by multiple individuals;
- simultaneous or temporally comparable co-use;
- lower-than-null resource or 3D overlap;
- diet/resource identity where relevant.

To establish competition-mediated partitioning, additionally test whether:
- co-presence of competitors increases spatial/vertical separation;
- separation changes with competitor density;
- resource depletion changes individual geometry;
- experimental addition/removal changes overlap.

## New methodological implication

For terrestrial and aerial animals, a raw 3D utilization distribution can confound:
1. horizontal niche selection;
2. the vertical coordinate of the selected substrate;
3. residual vertical behaviour.

Therefore future 3D niche-overlap studies should report both:
- realized x-y-z overlap; and
- substrate-relative overlap where a biologically meaningful surface can be measured.

The Pteropus result shows these two quantities can lead to qualitatively different biological interpretations.

## Claim ceiling

Current evidence supports:
- hierarchical horizontal plus vertical structure in several bat panels;
- strong realized 3D individuality in Pteropus;
- terrain mediation of that Pteropus realized 3D geometry;
- weak/non-supported stable geometry in Myotis under the fixed test;
- the usefulness of decomposing 3D niche geometry into landscape and substrate-relative components.

Current evidence does not establish:
- a causal cross-species ecology-to-geometry law;
- competition as the driver;
- intentional avoidance;
- diet partitioning;
- terrain-relative vertical strategies in systems lacking a suitable substrate correction.
