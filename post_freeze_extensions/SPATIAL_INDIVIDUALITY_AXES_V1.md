# Spatial individuality axes v1

## Core idea

The completed programme indicates that individual spatial specialization should not be treated as one scalar property.

At minimum, the present evidence distinguishes three axes:

1. **horizontal location fidelity**
   - repeated use of the same site, route or home-range region;

2. **absolute vertical level**
   - repeatable tendency to occupy higher or lower absolute altitude;

3. **centered vertical-distribution shape**
   - repeatable organization of vertical use after absolute level is removed.

These axes can dissociate.

## Empirical boundary cases

### Horizontal fidelity without centered vertical individuality

The prospective *Hipposideros* source is reported by its source study to show pronounced horizontal foraging-site fidelity over repeated nights.

Yet its frozen source-level centered vertical-identity test fails:
- source calibrated excess = **-0.04468**
- p_upper = **0.8616**

Therefore:

> **strong horizontal routine is not sufficient for stable centered vertical individuality.**

This does not imply that horizontal and vertical behaviour are statistically independent in all systems. It establishes a concrete boundary case in which one can be strong while the other is not detected under the frozen estimator.

### Absolute vertical individuality without centered-shape individuality

The focal *Tadarida teniotis* panel shows strong repeatable absolute/common-cell vertical identity but loses identity after session-median centering.

Therefore:

> **repeatable absolute altitude level is not equivalent to repeatable vertical-distribution shape.**

### Centered vertical individuality beyond coarse horizontal place

Five original comparative panels retain centered-shape identity after coarse horizontal occupancy standardization.

Post-freeze localization further shows persistence after progressively richer place/state matching where structurally evaluable.

Therefore:

> **centered vertical individuality can exist beyond measured horizontal-location differences.**

## Conceptual model

For individual i in context c, vertical use can be thought of descriptively as having separable components:

```text
spatial individuality_i
    |
    +-- horizontal allocation H_i
    |
    +-- absolute vertical level L_i
    |
    +-- centered vertical organization S_i(c)
```

The present results do not claim statistical orthogonality in the strict covariance-matrix sense.

“Orthogonal axes” is therefore used as an ecological shorthand for **separable dimensions that can dissociate empirically**, not as a claim that all pairwise correlations are exactly zero.

## New comparative question

The programme changes the most useful general question.

Instead of asking only:

> Do animals have individual spatial niches?

ask:

> **Which dimensions of spatial organization are individualized in a given ecological system, and what predicts the covariance or dissociation among those dimensions?**

For bats, useful cross-system response variables would include:
- horizontal site-fidelity strength;
- absolute-altitude repeatability;
- centered vertical-shape identity;
- temporal persistence of each axis.

Potential predictors:
- foraging mode;
- clutter / habitat structure;
- central-place geometry;
- prey distribution;
- flight morphology;
- atmospheric dependence;
- social foraging;
- migratory versus resident movement;
- sampling resolution and tracking duration.

## Testable predictions generated by the current evidence

### Prediction 1 — horizontal fidelity is neither necessary nor sufficient for centered vertical shape

The *Hipposideros* boundary establishes non-sufficiency.

A future multi-species panel should test whether centered vertical individuality also occurs in systems with relatively weak horizontal site fidelity.

### Prediction 2 — absolute-level repeatability and shape repeatability need different ecological predictors

The *Tadarida* boundary predicts that variables controlling preferred flight altitude need not control within-session vertical-distribution shape.

### Prediction 3 — context-residual identity should be conditional on the existence of the axis

Where centered vertical individuality is present, measured context matching may fail to explain it away.

Where the axis is absent, increasingly rich conditioning should not be interpreted as a hidden mechanism search for a positive result.

### Prediction 4 — cross-system heterogeneity is biologically informative

Independent external failures should be used to model **where the axis emerges**, rather than treated only as failed replications.

The next large comparative programme should therefore estimate heterogeneity in centered vertical individuality and test ecological predictors of that heterogeneity.

## Claim boundary

Supported:
- dimensions of individual spatial specialization can dissociate;
- horizontal fidelity does not guarantee centered vertical individuality;
- absolute vertical identity does not guarantee centered-shape identity;
- centered-shape individuality can remain after measured horizontal context is standardized.

Not established:
- exact statistical independence among all axes;
- a universal three-axis decomposition for all animals;
- any one ecological cause of the covariance among axes;
- that the three axes exhaust spatial individuality.

## Implication for manuscript framing

The strongest broad ecological framing is not “bats have vertical personalities.”

It is:

> **Individual spatial specialization is multidimensional: repeated use of places, repeated absolute altitude, and repeated vertical-distribution shape are separable properties whose expression can differ among bat systems.**

That framing preserves the original positive result, incorporates the two independent prospective external failures, and turns the failures into an ecological boundary rather than an embarrassment to be explained away.
