# Next decisive mechanism experiment v1

## Goal

Separate **fine-route/resource allocation** from **within-context individual flight organization**.

The archival analyses show that broad movement-state composition is insufficient, but sparse cross-individual fine-place overlap—especially in *Eidolon*—prevents the decisive comparison.

## Core design principle

Create overlap deliberately rather than hoping archival tracks contain it.

Track multiple individuals repeatedly while ensuring that several animals use the **same fine-scale routes or resource patches during overlapping environmental periods**.

For each individual collect:
- calibrated terrain-relative altitude;
- high-frequency x-y movement sufficient to resolve fine route and turning behaviour;
- independent behavioural-state labels where possible;
- resource-patch / feeding-site identity;
- roost or central-place identity;
- body mass;
- wing dimensions sufficient to estimate wing loading / aspect-ratio proxies;
- simultaneous local atmospheric measurements;
- repeated stationary/tag calibration or tag-swap information.

## Primary causal hypotheses

### H1 — route/resource allocation

Individuality is generated because individuals repeatedly use different fine routes/resources.

**Prediction:** after conditioning on the same fine route/resource patch, behavioural state, and environmental window, the individual vertical-identity advantage should collapse toward its pipeline-specific null.

### H2 — within-context flight organization

Individuality persists even when individuals are observed in the same fine route/resource patch and broad behavioural/environmental context.

**Prediction:** same-individual history continues to predict centered vertical state better than other-individual history within matched route × state × environment strata.

### H3 — morphology-linked flight phenotype

Within-context vertical organization varies systematically with morphology.

**Prediction:** among non-self donors observed in matched contexts, profiles from individuals with more similar wing loading/body size should transfer better than profiles from morphologically dissimilar individuals.

### H4 — individual environmental reaction norms

Individuals differ in how vertical movement responds to wind, uplift or boundary-layer conditions.

**Prediction:** allowing individual-specific environmental slopes should improve held-out prediction beyond shared environmental slopes and reduce residual individual identity if reaction norms mediate the pattern.

## Identification sequence

1. Freeze structural eligibility without opening vertical outcomes.
2. Establish shared fine-route/resource × behavioural-state support.
3. Test self-vs-other centered vertical identity within matched contexts.
4. Only if identity persists, test morphology similarity and individual environmental reaction norms.
5. Calibrate every prediction statistic by replaying the complete pipeline under the biologically appropriate identity exchangeability null.

## Critical sampling target

The limiting quantity is not raw GPS fixes; it is **cross-individual overlap in the same fine context**.

A useful field design should therefore prioritize:
- multiple individuals using the same resource patches/routes;
- repeated nights per individual;
- temporal overlap among individuals;
- enough fixes within matched route × state strata for held-out scoring.

This directly addresses the failure mode that blocked the archival fine-place, same-night and body-mass transfer tests.

## Interpretation

A positive H2 test would support a genuine **individual flight-organization phenotype** relative to measured route, behaviour and environment.

A collapse under H1 matching would instead show that the present distribution-shape individuality is mainly generated by persistent individual route/resource allocation.

Either outcome is biologically informative.
