# Ecological heterogeneity hypotheses v1

## Status

**POST-HOC HYPOTHESIS GENERATION ONLY.**

This document is written after the original, Nyctalus, Hipposideros, Myotis and Pteropus vertical outcomes are known.

It may motivate a future prospective comparative programme, but it is not evidence that any proposed ecological predictor has already been tested.

---

## 1. Empirical pattern that requires explanation

Centered vertical individuality is now known to vary substantially among bat systems.

Examples:

### Strong / supported centered vertical individuality

- *Eidolon helvum* — original comparative PASS;
- *Hypsignathus monstrosus* — original comparative PASS;
- *Phyllostomus hastatus* — multiple original comparative PASS panels;
- *Pteropus poliocephalus* — independent prospective PASS under the frozen four-individual programme.

### Weak / unsupported centered vertical individuality

- *Tadarida teniotis* — centered-shape boundary FAIL despite strong absolute vertical identity;
- *Nyctalus noctula* — positive-direction prospective FAIL;
- *Hipposideros armiger / H. pratti* — source-level prospective FAIL;
- *Myotis vivesi* — prospective small-panel FAIL.

The ecological question is therefore:

> **What determines whether a bat system develops stable centered vertical organization at all?**

---

## 2. Hypothesis A — resource anchoring

### Idea

Centered vertical individuality may be strongest when foraging is organized around spatially persistent resources that can support repeatable individual routes and approach geometries.

Examples of potentially anchored resources:
- repeatedly visited fruiting / flowering trees;
- stable plant patches;
- fixed feeding territories or feeding areas;
- other persistent point or patch resources.

By contrast, highly mobile or ephemeral prey may impose a larger role for transient environmental conditions and shared prey distributions, weakening stable individual centered vertical profiles.

### Post-hoc pattern

The positive side contains several taxa with fixed or spatially anchored resources:

- *Eidolon helvum* is frugivorous/nectarivorous and tracked individuals use identifiable food trees and plantations.
- *Hypsignathus monstrosus* is primarily frugivorous; published natural-history accounts describe established dependable fruit resources in at least part of the population.
- *Phyllostomus hastatus* is omnivorous, but females have been described as occupying fixed feeding areas and the species also uses fruit/flower/nectar resources.
- *Pteropus poliocephalus* feeds on fruit, nectar, pollen and tree blossoms, and the prospective horizontal analysis shows extremely strong individual 5-km occupancy identity.

The weak/negative side contains several systems dominated by mobile prey or strongly constrained prey surfaces:

- *Tadarida teniotis* is an aerial insectivore.
- *Nyctalus noctula* is an aerial-hawking insectivore whose prey include ephemeral insect swarms.
- *Hipposideros armiger* and *H. pratti* are insectivorous.
- *Myotis vivesi* feeds on marine fish/crustaceans and forages close to the water surface.

### Prospective prediction

For independent future bat systems:

> **calibrated centered vertical-individuality effect size should increase with the degree to which foraging is anchored to persistent spatial resources.**

This prediction must be operationalized before new vertical outcomes are opened.

---

## 3. Hypothesis B — vertical opportunity / vertical resource complexity

### Idea

Even with stable individual behaviour, centered vertical individuality cannot emerge strongly if the ecological task offers little repeatable vertical freedom.

Examples:

- water-surface feeding can constrain flight to a narrow vertical envelope;
- open-air insect distributions may shift vertically with weather, wind and prey emergence;
- vertically structured forest/canopy resources may allow repeatable individual choices among approach heights, canopy layers and commuting geometries.

### Prospective prediction

> **centered vertical individuality should be stronger when the foraging environment offers persistent vertical heterogeneity and repeated individual choice among vertical routes or resource layers.**

This differs from simple horizontal site fidelity.

A system can revisit the same horizontal patch while remaining vertically exchangeable.

---

## 4. Hypothesis C — flight morphology

### Idea

Resource anchoring is confounded with morphology and phylogeny in the currently opened systems.

Several strong positive systems are large-bodied pteropodids, whereas several weak systems are smaller echolocating insectivores.

Candidate traits:
- wing loading;
- aspect ratio;
- wingspan;
- body mass;
- maneuverability / clutter adaptation.

The original within-panel body-mass analysis did not support simple body mass as the universal individual-level mechanism.

That result does **not** rule out a cross-species morphological predictor of whether vertical individuality is expressed.

### Prospective prediction

> **cross-system centered vertical-individuality effect size may vary with wing loading / aspect ratio even after resource guild is controlled.**

---

## 5. Hypothesis D — environmental forcing versus learned routine

### Idea

Open-air and aerial-insect systems may be more strongly forced by nightly atmospheric conditions and ephemeral prey layers, whereas resource-anchored systems may allow greater expression of learned individual routes.

This would connect two existing findings:

1. vertical individuality can persist across days where it occurs;
2. broad same-night context and measured Nyctalus contexts do not fully explain positive-system identity.

### Prospective prediction

Systems dominated by rapidly changing atmospheric/prey conditions should show:
- weaker centered vertical identity;
- stronger night-to-night environmental slopes;
- lower long-lag self-transfer.

Systems dominated by persistent resources should show the opposite pattern.

---

## 6. Why “frugivore versus insectivore” is too crude

Do **not** turn the current pattern into a simple trophic binary.

Reasons:
- *Phyllostomus hastatus* is omnivorous yet positive;
- Hipposideros can use repeated horizontal foraging sites while remaining vertically weak;
- morphology, sensory ecology, phylogeny and body size covary with diet;
- prey mobility and vertical habitat structure are more mechanistically relevant than taxonomic diet labels alone.

The preferred causal language is:

> **resource anchoring and vertical opportunity**, with morphology and environmental forcing as competing predictor families.

---

## 7. Frozen order for a future heterogeneity test

When enough independent unopened sources exist, the first comparative predictor test should be frozen in the following order:

### Primary predictor family

**resource anchoring / vertical opportunity**

Operationalization must be source-outcome blind.

Possible components:
- stationary plant / fixed patch resources versus mobile prey;
- persistent point-resource use documented independently of the tracking outcome;
- habitat vertical complexity;
- degree of foraging constrained to a surface or layer.

### Primary competing covariate

**wing morphology**

Prefer:
- wing loading;
- aspect ratio.

Body mass alone is secondary.

### Secondary candidate family

**environmental forcing**

Examples:
- wind / uplift variability;
- prey-layer or atmospheric dependence;
- nightly environmental variance.

Do not test multiple ecological codings and select the one with the best vertical association.

---

## 8. Minimum evidence required

No cross-source causal model should be treated as confirmatory until there are enough independent sources.

Current rule:

- fewer than 5 new independent source effects: descriptive only;
- >=5: summarize effect-size heterogeneity;
- >=8: a separately frozen predictor model may be opened.

The predictor coding must be frozen before the corresponding new source vertical outcomes are opened whenever possible.

---

## 9. Current interpretation

The current evidence supports the question, not the answer:

> **Stable vertical individuality may emerge where animals repeatedly solve the same vertically structured, spatially anchored foraging problem, whereas systems dominated by mobile prey, surface-constrained foraging or strong environmental forcing may remain vertically exchangeable.**

This is the leading ecological hypothesis generated by v11.

It is not yet a tested general law.
