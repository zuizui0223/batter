Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Persistent individual vertical strategies need not partition three-dimensional space in two tropical bat species,”** for publication in *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

Our study asks whether persistent differences in vertical space use require bats to occupy separate parts of three-dimensional space. This question matters because repeated altitude differences may result from horizontal site fidelity, terrain or device-specific altitude offsets rather than distinct vertical strategies.

Using six publicly archived bat-tracking panels, we compare held-out individual vertical-use distributions under identical horizontal-cell weights, calibrate prediction against session-level label permutations, and remove additive altitude levels by within-session median centering. All five comparative panels retain calibrated identity in vertical-distribution shape. In four structurally evaluable panels from *Hypsignathus monstrosus* and *Phyllostomus hastatus*, individuality remains detectable relative to local terrain within shared fine-scale spatial cells. None supports positive additional terrain-relative segregation, and three did not provide statistical support for additional vertical separation during synchronous co-use. The fourth, a 2023 *Phyllostomus* panel, is an explicit exception under a coarser encounter design.

Temporal holdouts and matching for local place and flight kinematics further support persistence of personal vertical organization. Crucially, we do **not** infer that competition is absent or that the mechanisms of learning, memory or adaptive benefit have been demonstrated. The motivating *Tadarida* panel and previously frozen external datasets also provide non-supporting boundary tests, which are retained rather than selectively discarded.

The ecological advance is therefore a distinction between **persistent individual organization** and **contemporaneous spatial exclusion**: maintained vertical-use strategies can remain detectable without evidence that individuals occupy mutually exclusive height layers. All underlying tracking sources are publicly archived; we conducted no new animal handling. Anonymized review material and a source-provenance and software-archiving package have been prepared for the authors' final release approvals.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
